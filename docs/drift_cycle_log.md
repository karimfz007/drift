### 1 — Carry weight

Every carried material has a per-unit mass and every owned tool a fixed one (`TUNE.materialMassKg` / `toolMassKg`, keyed by the same `MaterialKind` set the Ch.1 family/tag schema uses, so a future material cannot ship without deciding what it weighs). Total mass sorts into Light / Working / Heavy, which scale movement speed and energy cost.

**The energy half reuses the existing plumbing rather than duplicating it**, as instructed: the band multiplies D-052's ambient per-game-hour drain in `reconcile` and D-052's own `effortEnergyCostFor` charge in `gatherNode`. There is still exactly one place effortful energy is priced. **`light` multiplies by exactly 1 on both axes**, so an unencumbered castaway behaves bit-for-bit as before this chapter existed — that invariant has its own regression test (`an UNENCUMBERED castaway pays exactly the pre-Ch.6 tuned cost, to the digit`), because a carry-weight system that silently retunes the whole game for a player carrying nothing would be a defect, not a feature.

### 2 — The rest redesign, and Fatigue

C05's `sleep()` ran a reconcile span and then set `energy = energyMax` outright. **That line is gone.** A transient `resting` flag is raised for the sleep span; reconcile recovers energy and warmth at tuned rates over the elapsed game hours (scaled by `sleepRecoveryMultiplier`) and sheds fatigue as it goes. A full 8-hour sleep recovers 84 energy — an ordinarily-tired castaway wakes full, one who ran themselves to empty wakes genuinely short and must sleep again or push on tired. Recovery is bounded by the same ceilings everything else clamps to (25 consecutive sleeps stay at or under max, tested directly). Sleeping is never *worse* than being awake in the same spot: the warmth rate takes `Math.max` of the resting rate and the awake rate, so dozing beside a roaring fire still gets the fire's much better rate.

**A tuning defect this chapter's own tests caught.** The first pass set `energyRecoveryPerGameHourResting: 9`, giving 9 × 1.5 × 8 = 108 against a 100 ceiling — every full sleep capped out, making the new "curve" behaviourally identical to the instant refill it was written to replace, in exactly the case that matters most. Retuned to 7 (= 84) so the gap is real. Caught by a test asserting the behaviour, not by reading the number.

Fatigue accrues while ONLINE and in energy debt, sheds with rest, and reads out in three perceivable stages. **It is deliberately absent from the health-drain path entirely** — that is what keeps D-011's offline-death-impossible law structurally intact with a new body state added, the same scope-out energy's own C05 introduction made.

**THE MANDATORY PROPERTY TEST, and the real bug it caught.** The requirement: reconcile must never let absence turn Fatigue harmful. Implemented **structurally**, by branch order rather than a guard bolted on afterwards — across a qualifying absence fatigue can only fall or hold, never rise; a restful spot (roof, not freezing, not soaked) actively recovers, anywhere else holds. Swept over 2000 random states × random long absences, in the same file as the offline-death law, half of them carrying a real load so the band could not smuggle in an offline cost through the energy path. **The first draft failed it**: reconcile's 6-decimal `round()` could round a held value UP, raising fatigue by ~5e-9 across an absence.
