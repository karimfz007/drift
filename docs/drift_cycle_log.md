**GOAL:** Give the castaway a reason to stop wandering and build somewhere — a shelter that keeps the fire's warmth working for you while you rest, a bed that becomes home (the new respawn anchor), a place to keep what you've gathered, and the honest cost of neglecting any of it.

**PROMISE:** The island stops being a foraging loop with a fire at its center and starts being a place you are *building a life on* — what you build starts to need you back.

**HYPOTHESIS:** Cycles 01–04 proved the moment-to-moment loop feels good (feel, feedback, direct touch). This cycle tests the next rung of the Experience Constitution: does *investment* — something placed, something that can fall into disrepair, something worth returning to — deepen the pull, or does it just add chores? Construction is the first system whose payoff is measured in returns, not immediate feedback.

**PLAYTEST QUESTION:** Did building the shelter feel like progress — and did you notice, and care, when it started falling apart?

**SCOPE IN** (staged, in buildable order):
1. **Shelter (one tier — "lean-to").** A new placed structure, built like the fire (own button, gated on `shelterWoodCost`/`shelterStoneCost`/`shelterFiberCost`, clear-ground check, placed at the player). While the player is within `shelterRadius`, warmth drains slower and (see #4) wetness clears faster — the fire's job, extended. Analytic collision only (no Havok — see the Havok note below).
2. **Bed / respawn anchor.** Not a separate structure this cycle — the built shelter itself is where you sleep and where you wake. Once a shelter exists, death and any absence-triggered respawn place the player at the shelter instead of the original beach spawn. (Scope decision, stated plainly: a distinct bed structure is deferred — see SCOPE OUT.)
3. **Energy — the 5th vital.** Drains slowly, at all times, at `energyDrainPerGameHour` (a full-day rhythm, not a pressure clock like thirst/hunger). Below `energyLowThreshold`, the castaway is sluggish (`walkSpeedMps` reduced by `energySlowWalkMultiplier`, HUD says why) — a soft debuff, **never a death vector this cycle** (see SCOPE OUT). Restored by sleeping.
4. **Sleep.** Tap the shelter (once built) to sleep: this reuses the *exact* reconcile spine an offline absence already uses — a voluntary `sleepDurationGameHours`-long "absence" while stationary at the shelter, floors and all, ending with energy refilled. No new time-skip mechanism; the offline-death-impossible property this cycle's sleep span rides on is *already proven* (A1).
5. **Wet condition.** A `wet` meter (not a vital, not a death vector): rises fast while standing in the pond, decays slowly on dry land, decays fast within `shelterRadius`. While wet, warmth drains faster (`wetWarmthDrainMultiplier`, linearly interpolated by wetness) — this is *why* a roof over your head matters even with a fire already lit.
6. **Storage.** A second placed structure (own build option, wood/stone only — no fiber gate), holding raw materials (wood/stone/fiber) separately from personal inventory. One tap: if carrying any raw material, stores all of it; if not, and storage holds any, withdraws a fixed batch (`storageWithdrawBatch` per resource) — the same disjoint-state pattern that correctly resolved the pond's fill-vs-drink conflict (D-042 audit), applied up front this time instead of found by a bug report.
7. **Structure upkeep.** Shelter and storage each carry a `durability` (0–100) that decays slowly over game hours. At 0, the structure's *bonus* pauses (no warmth/dry bonus; storage still holds what's in it — nothing is ever destroyed or lost, per the charter's honest-systems law) until repaired. Repair: tap the structure while carrying wood — an instant, always-available action once any wood is held, restoring durability by `repairAmountPerWood` per wood spent.

**SCOPE OUT (explicit):**
- A second (or higher) shelter tier — one lean-to tier proves the system; more tiers are `NEXT`.
- A distinct bed structure separate from the shelter — folded into #2 above to keep this cycle's surface area buildable in one pass.
- Energy as a death vector — it is a soft debuff only. Wiring a fifth vital into the health-drain-stacking system safely (and re-proving the offline-death-impossible property against it) is real work that deserves its own scoped pass, not a rider on construction.
- Any change to personal-inventory carry capacity — storage is a second pool, not a cap on the first.
- Multiplayer or shared structures, weather events beyond the pond-driven wet meter, structure destruction (only disrepair, never deletion).
- Havok / any physics engine (see below).

**Three real bugs found and fixed during BUILD (before any director report — self-caught via the harness and manual verification, the last one only after the harness finally completed a clean full run and kept failing the same two checks until root-caused fresh rather than dismissed as more environment noise):**
- **Collision degenerate case.** `Island.resolveCollision`'s push-out formula divided by the distance between the target point and an obstacle's centre; when that distance was ~0 (building storage from the same spot/facing as the shelter, both using a 2.2 m offset), the push silently no-opped, landing storage exactly on top of the shelter. Fixed: a degenerate branch picks a deterministic push direction from the obstacle's own position instead of leaving the overlap unresolved. General fix — protects every caller (movement, camera-clip, all three build placements), not just this one collision.
- **Repair-threshold starvation.** `canRepairStructure` originally fired on any `durability < max`. Passive decay is continuous, so that is true almost always — repair, checked first in the sleep/storage-use disjoint choice, would win nearly every tap once a structure had existed for even a few seconds, the same one-condition-always-true bug class that starved Build-fire in C03. Fixed: repair only applies below `structureRepairThresholdFraction` (90%) of max. Regression-tested directly (`construction.test.ts`) and confirmed via the device harness (sleeping at a freshly-built, only-just-decayed shelter correctly still sleeps, not repairs).
- **Shelter-swallows-storage tap misroute.** `onTap`'s fire/pond/shelter/storage hit-test was a first-match if-chain, shelter checked before storage. Shelter and storage are both placed `~2.2 m` ahead of the builder, so in ordinary play they end up close together — and shelter's own forgiveness radius (`shelterCollisionRadius + 1.5` = 2.8 m) comfortably reaches past that 2.2 m gap. A tap landing exactly on the storage crate was silently claimed by the *shelter* branch instead, repairing the shelter (spending wood, moving nothing) rather than depositing into the crate — invisible to the 128 brain tests (the bug lives entirely in the body's hit-testing, not the brain's disjoint-state logic, which was correct throughout). Caught by the device harness's storage deposit/withdraw checks, which kept failing identically across three consecutive full runs — including one clean 75/75 run's only two failures — until traced by instrumenting live `storage.durability` mid-run (it read 91.8%, comfortably above the repair threshold, ruling out the threshold-starvation bug class and pointing at hit-testing instead) and reading `onTap`'s actual check order. Fixed: candidate objects within their own forgiveness radius are now collected and the *nearest centre* wins, not the first one checked in source order — a general fix, not special-cased to shelter/storage, so any two future objects placed close together resolve the same correct way.

### 1 — Energy inversion: root cause

`gatherNode()` (`state.ts`) never touched `state.energy` at all. The only drain that existed anywhere was `reconcile()`'s ambient per-game-hour rate, applied uniformly to elapsed real time regardless of what the player was doing — walking, idling, or mid-hold on a quarry tap. Because a hold's real-time duration is a couple of seconds at most, the ambient drain during an effortful action was negligible, while the action itself charged nothing further. The bug wasn't that idling drained energy (it should, and does — that's correct ambient behaviour); it was that **nothing else ever did**, so "effortful" and "idle" were indistinguishable to the energy system. Fixed by adding `effortEnergyCostFor(kind)`, charged once per successful gather, keyed to the same `hold`/`tap` distinction `NODE_SPECS` already draws — every hold-interaction kind (tree, deadfall, rock, quarry, coconut palm, crash box) now costs a tuned amount; every tap-interaction kind (driftwood, berries, reeds, shellfish, salvage) stays free, since an instant pickup isn't exertion the way a timed hold is. `energyDrainPerGameHour` retuned 5→2 so the ambient rate alone isn't harder than before now that action costs stack on top of it.

1. **The null-outcome combination journal.** Every recipe (`src/brain/recipes.ts`) is a list of slots — a role (`handle`, `blade`, `binding`, ...) requiring a `MaterialFamily` or `MaterialTag`, not a hardcoded resource name. Ch.2 (knowledge/experimenting) doesn't exist in this codebase yet; the event is a stub hook only, unwired beyond being recorded, exactly as instructed for content whose consumer chapter isn't built.
2. **Material family/tags schema (`src/brain/materials.ts`).** Every material gets a primary `MaterialFamily` (`organic` | `mineral`) plus a tags array (`fuel`, `woodwork`, `textile`, `masonry`, `blade`, `food`). Recipes match on primary OR any tag. **Deliberate scoping call, stated plainly:** today's actual crafting costs are unchanged — every current slot is disjoint, satisfied by exactly one material kind (a property test in `tests/materials.test.ts` locks this) — so this pass did not re-derive the already-tested exact-resource-check crafting math through the new slot system for zero behavioural gain. The schema is genuinely load-bearing for the journal today; it's forward-compatible plumbing for a future material pair (e.g. a later wire/bone combination) sharing a family or tag, not a rebalance of what's craftable now.
3. **Grades: crude / serviceable / refined / exceptional.** Rolled once at craft time via `rollGrade`, reusing the existing `hash32`/`seedFraction` determinism technique `deadfallYield`/`spawnSalvageNode` already use — never `Math.random()` — seeded by an incrementing `state.craftRollCount` (mirrors `salvageSpawnCount`'s counter-as-seed pattern). Plain stated odds (50/35/12/3), honest-systems law. Exactly ONE functional stat moves per item type, never more: the axe's fell speed (`axeChopMultiplierFor`), the torch's burn duration (`torchBurnGameHoursFor`), the shelter's warmth bonus (`shelterWarmthMultiplierFor`). **`serviceable` is deliberately defined to reproduce every pre-grade constant exactly** (chop ×1.0, burn ×1.0, warmth ×0.5 — the old flat `shelterWarmthDrainMultiplier`) — a save healed to that baseline grade is functionally invisible from before grades existed. ### 1 — Carry weight

Every carried material has a per-unit mass and every owned tool a fixed one (`TUNE.materialMassKg` / `toolMassKg`, keyed by the same `MaterialKind` set the Ch.1 family/tag schema uses, so a future material cannot ship without deciding what it weighs). **The energy half reuses the existing plumbing rather than duplicating it**, as instructed: the band multiplies D-052's ambient per-game-hour drain in `reconcile` and D-052's own `effortEnergyCostFor` charge in `gatherNode`. ### 2 — The rest redesign, and Fatigue

C05's `sleep()` ran a reconcile span and then set `energy = energyMax` outright. **That line is gone.** A transient `resting` flag is raised for the sleep span; reconcile recovers energy and warmth at tuned rates over the elapsed game hours (scaled by `sleepRecoveryMultiplier`) and sheds fatigue as it goes. A full 8-hour sleep recovers 84 energy — an ordinarily-tired castaway wakes full, one who ran themselves to empty wakes genuinely short and must sleep again or push on tired. Recovery is bounded by the same ceilings everything else clamps to (25 consecutive sleeps stay at or under max, tested directly). Sleeping is never *worse* than being awake in the same spot: the warmth rate takes `Math.max` of the resting rate and the awake rate, so dozing beside a roaring fire still gets the fire's much better rate.

**A tuning defect this chapter's own tests caught.** The first pass set `energyRecoveryPerGameHourResting: 9`, giving 9 × 1.5 × 8 = 108 against a 100 ceiling — every full sleep capped out, making the new "curve" behaviourally identical to the instant refill it was written to replace, in exactly the case that matters most. Retuned to 7 (= 84) so the gap is real. Caught by a test asserting the behaviour, not by reading the number.

Fatigue accrues while ONLINE and in energy debt, sheds with rest, and reads out in three perceivable stages. **It is deliberately absent from the health-drain path entirely** — that is what keeps D-011's offline-death-impossible law structurally intact with a new body state added, the same scope-out energy's own C05 introduction made.

**THE MANDATORY PROPERTY TEST, and the real bug it caught.** The requirement: reconcile must never let absence turn Fatigue harmful. Implemented **structurally**, by branch order rather than a guard bolted on afterwards — across a qualifying absence fatigue can only fall or hold, never rise; a restful spot (roof, not freezing, not soaked) actively recovers, anywhere else holds. Swept over 2000 random states × random long absences, in the same file as the offline-death law, half of them carrying a real load so the band could not smuggle in an offline cost through the energy path. **The first draft failed it**: reconcile's 6-decimal `round()` could round a held value UP, raising fatigue by up to ~5e-7 across an absence (max observed 4.995e-7; an earlier draft of this section said ~5e-9, corrected by C3). All verified.

**FINDING B1 — genuine, coverage: the ring-walk fallback has ZERO test coverage, because no seed in the property test's range is ever blocked.**

I replicated `spawnSalvageNode`'s numerics exactly — same `hash32`/`seedFraction`, `beachRadius` 96, `salvageShoreMarginM` 3, `WALKABLE_RADIUS` 108, same `Math.round` — and swept it. **Across seeds 0–499, the exact range `tests/reachability.test.ts:56-61` and `:70-79` use, the initial point is placeable every single time. The `!isPlaceablePoint(x, y)` branch at `state.ts:547` is taken 0 times out of 500.** The 500-seed property test therefore passes without ever executing the new code it exists to protect, and "a blocked spawn walks the ring rather than teleporting to the island centre" asserts `atCentre === 0` — which holds trivially, because no spawn is ever blocked in the first place. The harness's 200-seed sweep (`smoke.mjs:1306-1314`) shares the blind spot over a smaller range. **Neither the ring walk nor the centre fallback is exercised by anything in the repo.**

The reason is geometric and worth banking. The spawn annulus is 96 m to 105 m, and **exactly one** of the 14 rocks lies inside it with a stranding radius: `[-52, 84, 1.8]` at r = 98.8 m, needing 0.42 m of clearance. Because spawn coordinates are `Math.round`ed to integers, the only blocking point on that ring is `(-52, 84)` itself. Sweeping **200,000 seeds: 28 blocked — 0.0140%, about 1 in 7,100, first at seed 6384.** Under the *old* `isWalkablePoint`-only validator, **0 of the first 500 seeds** would have produced an unreachable find either.

None of that makes the fix wrong — the failure mode is real, it is a genuine class defect, and closing it at the class rather than the instance is exactly what the ruling asked for. But the incidence is far rarer than the as-built implies, and **the regression is a false lock**: it would stay green if the entire ring-walk block were deleted. Adding seed 6384 explicitly, or sweeping 0–10,000, closes it in one line and is the single highest-value follow-up in this package.

**FINDING B2 — genuine, harness: the check does NOT force a find against the largest rock; it collects an ordinary one.**

Two assertions inside it compound this:

### 3. `guardPanelLock` — scrutinised hard

**It cannot fire during a legitimate transition.** `fade` (`hud.ts:247-250`) adds `.leaving` at t=0, removes the element at t=320 ms, then calls `then()` → `endPanel()` → `window.setTimeout(restore, 0)`, so `panelOpen` clears at t≈321 ms. ### The shelter pickability fix — confirmed sound

- **The poles were unpickable.** `c04f140^:src/body/entities.ts` sets `pole.isPickable = false` inside the two-pole loop; `this.root.isPickable = true` (the roof slab) was the only pickable part. Confirmed.

## D-065 REMEDIATION (C2, in-session, after C3's PASS-WITH-NOTES)

### Gate 0 Part 2 — the movement hard-block, RESOLVED, and a wrong root cause I published first

**The block is real, reproducible, and is correct game behaviour meeting a flawed test.**

Measured on device, three runs, byte-identical: the player stalls at exactly **(0.0, 103.5)**, 0.5 m from the (0, 104) start, `panelOpen: false`, energy 58–62, 18 units carried. Isolated probes then established the mechanism directly:

| setup | result |
|---|---|
| walk due south into a shelter at (0, 98) | pins at **exactly (0, 99.70)** = 98 + 1.3 (shelter radius) + 0.4 (player radius) |
| stick during the pin | magnitude **0.87 — held and engaged**, `panelOpen: false`, no page errors |

So the player is **not** frozen: they have walked into a solid object and stopped, with the stick still pressed. The harness teleports to a fixed point and walks due south — straight into the shelter that same run built earlier. Correct collision, wrong scenario.

**Eliminated first, by evidence rather than argument:** the surf line (not a collider at all — only `TREES`/`ROCKS` register as obstacles); static obstacles (nearest 8.5 m); nodes (nearest 7.5 m); an open panel (`false` in every sample); exhaustion and load (7.63 m from the same spot on a clean save, 5.04 m at energy 5, 7.57 m at fatigue 95); the input layer (the stick persists until `pointerup`; two `releaseAll` callers, both panel-gated); `stepHold`.

**The wrong turn, recorded because it matters.** I committed collide-and-slide as *the root cause*, reasoning that a purely radial push-out must produce a head-on stalemate. The reasoning was sound and the conclusion was false, and the A/B the Vacuity Law now requires (D-066 b) is exactly what caught it:

| glancing approach past a shelter, 3 s of held stick | lateral | ended |
|---|---|---|
| radial push-out alone (pre-fix) | 0.50 m | (−0.50, **94.77**) |
| with collide-and-slide (post-fix) | 0.50 m | (−0.50, **93.35**) |

The radial push-out **already slides**, because a glancing contact carries a lateral component in the push itself. The fix buys ~1.4 m more travel in the same window — a real but modest improvement, not a cure. It is kept, and its comment now says so plainly.

Had D-066(b) not existed, "collide-and-slide fixes the movement hard-block" would have shipped as fact, and the next session would have believed the block was closed. **The law paid for itself within a day of being written.**

**What remains for the harness:** the fast-movement check must stop walking the player into their own base — it should pick a clear heading, or place the structures out of the path. That is a test fix, not a game fix, and it is not yet done.

### The real reason three attempts were needed — and it is not CPU

Prior write-ups attribute these crashes to **CPU contention**. Measured on this bench mid-run, that is wrong: **CPU load was 8%** while **free RAM was 1.1 GB of 7.9 GB**. Every crash was a `page.goto`/`waitForScene` stall, which is the signature of a browser starved of memory, not of cycles — and the run's own `ERR_NO_BUFFER_SPACE` is the same fingerprint from inside the page. Attempt 1 died at `startFresh` (`:412`, from `:679`) after `startFresh` had already **succeeded twice** in that same run, which rules out a deterministic scene-init fault. Attempt 2 died on a 90 s navigation timeout at `:391` from `:1906`. Attempt 3, unchanged, completed all 199.

**Practical consequence for whoever runs this next:** the harness is not flaky in its logic — it is flaky in its *resident set*. Close the other memory-heavy applications before a full run rather than re-running and hoping, and treat a mid-run `page.goto` timeout as a memory signal rather than a game regression. Three attempts, one variable, no source changed between them.
