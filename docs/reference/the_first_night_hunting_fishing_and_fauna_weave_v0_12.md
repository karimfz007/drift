## 1. Audit ruling

Earlier canon contains the outline of a strong food-and-fauna system:

- v0.7 establishes fish, small animals and large animals as ecological
  populations rather than respawning loot;
- v0.8 includes eggs, small game, reef and lagoon fishing, line fishing, nets,
  traps, spears, boat fishing, parasites, overhunting and fish decline;
- v0.9 defines the Adaptive Fisher, Shore Harvester, Wildlife Reader and
  intersecting fiber, food-safety, weather, seamanship and logistics skills;
- v0.10 connects fish observation, reef stewardship, diving, underwater
  navigation and selective harvest;
- v0.11 gives fauna habitat, needs, perception, breeding, migration, disease,
  physical traces, invasive arrivals and responses to human pressure.

That is not yet a complete hunting and fishing game.

The missing layer is the operational chain connecting:

> **population → sign → identification → method → equipment → approach →
> selection → taking → recovery → processing → transport → preservation →
> use → waste → ecological consequence → future knowledge**

v0.12 supplies that layer. Hunting, fishing and trapping are no longer food
list entries or weapon mini-games. They are one interdependent provisioning,
craft, ecology, body, weather and social system.

The governing identity is:

> **The hunt begins long before contact and ends long after the kill.**

## 2. Constitutional laws

### Law 1 — Animals are agents, not meat nodes

Every animal belongs to a population and has habitat, needs, senses, movement,
memory, social behavior, reproduction, mortality and an ecological role. An
animal does not wait at a spawn marker for the player.

### Law 2 — No universal Hunting or Fishing level

There is no hunting XP bar, fishing level, perk purchase or global damage
bonus. Capability emerges from body condition, technique, knowledge,
perception, equipment, environment, records and proof in relevant conditions.

### Law 3 — Different quarry demands different knowledge

Knowing reef fish behavior does not teach bird flight, pig sign, crustacean
molting, pelagic trolling or humane small-game trapping. Transfer exists only
where the underlying perception, movement, materials or planning genuinely
overlap.

### Law 4 — A weapon does not grant mastery

A bow, firearm, spear, trap, hook, net or speargun provides a physical method.
It does not provide species identification, safe range, shot judgment,
tracking, recovery, carcass hygiene, restraint or ecological knowledge.

### Law 5 — Every attempt produces a physical result

A shot, cast, hook-set, spear thrust, trap placement or net deployment can:

- miss cleanly;
- be detected and alter animal behavior;
- damage or lose equipment;
- strike an obstruction or non-target;
- wound without immediate recovery;
- take the intended animal;
- attract predators or scavengers;
- leave line, hook, net, projectile, blood, scent or disturbed habitat behind.

There is no harmless reset after a failed attempt.

### Law 6 — Wounded and hooked animals persist

An injured animal does not despawn when it leaves view. It may hide, weaken,
recover, become infected, attract scavengers, change movement, threaten a
cornered pursuer or die unrecovered. The player may track, withdraw, call for
help, mark the location or accept the loss. Recovery is part of the act.

### Law 7 — The carcass is a time-critical physical object

Mass, temperature, contamination, insects, damage, species, handling,
transport and time determine what can be safely used. “Collect meat” is not an
instant inventory operation.

### Law 8 — The method must fit place, purpose and population

Gear has selectivity, range, noise, labor, failure modes, bycatch, habitat
impact and retrieval burden. Efficient but indiscriminate methods can create a
short surplus and a long ecological or social disaster.

### Law 9 — Restraint is a capability

Passing a poor shot, releasing a breeder, closing a nursery, abandoning unsafe
water, checking a trap on time and returning home empty-handed are evidence of
mastery. Yield alone never defines success.

### Law 10 — Food safety remains causal

Species, disease, contamination, field handling, clean tools, separation,
temperature history, preservation and adequate cooking determine safety.
Smoke, salt, drying or freezing are not magical sterilization flags.

### Law 11 — Whole-animal use is valuable but never automatic

Meat, fat, hide, skin, scales, feathers, bone, shell, sinew, organs, bait and
fertility inputs require distinct knowledge, tools, sanitation and processing.
Some tissues are unsafe or inappropriate for use.

### Law 12 — Harvest changes the future

The game tracks breeding adults, recruitment, age/size structure, habitat,
movement, disease, predator/prey balance and learned avoidance. Overharvest is
not repaired by a longer respawn timer.

### Law 13 — Spawn and Community Islands have different hunting cultures

The private Spawn Island develops solitary subsistence, pest control, coastal
harvesting and physical legacy. The Community Island adds teachers, crews,
markets, law, shared grounds, managed fisheries, disputes, research and public
stewardship.

### Law 14 — Rare fauna is not inserted merely to create combat

Large predators, giant animals and trophy creatures do not appear as combat
tiers. Turtles, marine mammals, sharks, endemic cave fauna and breeding
seabirds are usually ecological presences, warnings, research subjects or
protected life—not routine quarry.

### Law 15 — Permanent character death remains causal

Animal attack, drowning, falls, weapon accidents, infection, venom, exposure,
boat loss and foodborne illness follow readable chains. A technical failure
cannot commit death. The player returns as a different survivor on the
persistent Spawn Island; the dead character does not respawn.

## 3. The complete hunt and fish operation

Every operation can enter, fork, pause, fail or terminate at any stage.

| Stage | Core questions | Crossed systems |
|---|---|---|
| Need | Is food, pest control, material, research or protection the real purpose? | nutrition, stores, ecology, law |
| Intelligence | What population exists, where, when and in what condition? | tracks, records, weather, tide, local teaching |
| Identification | Is the species, sex/age class, size and health understood well enough? | Perception, ecology, disease, restraint |
| Method selection | Which legal, ethical and available method is selective and recoverable? | tools, weapons, traps, tackle, habitat |
| Preparation | Is the body, route, equipment, retrieval plan and abort rule ready? | condition, maintenance, logistics, medicine |
| Approach | Can the survivor reach position without unsafe exposure or premature detection? | wind/current, stealth, cover, movement, boat |
| Opportunity | Is the target clear, the background safe and recovery probable? | range, angle, light, non-targets, terrain |
| Taking | Cast, release, strike, set, close, net, hook or capture | technique, timing, equipment physics |
| Immediate control | Is the animal landed, contained, released, dispatched or still mobile? | Composure, handling, line/load control |
| Recovery | Can sign, line, float, trail, current or team coordination recover it? | tracking, navigation, time, weather |
| Field processing | Can contamination, heat, sharp work and waste be controlled? | anatomy, sanitation, edge tools |
| Transport | Can the mass be moved before spoilage or scavenger loss? | Strength, Stamina, pack, sled, boat, cold |
| Preservation | What must be eaten, chilled, cooked, dried, smoked, salted or discarded? | heat, containers, records, fuel |
| Whole use | Which secondary materials are safe and worth the labor? | tanning, fiber, glue, tools, fertilizer, bait |
| Ecological review | What did the take reveal and change? | population ledger, habitat, Community rules |
| Legacy | What map, procedure, pattern, maintained gear or warning survives this person? | archive, caches, teaching, institutions |

A routine mastered operation can compress. Novel species, dangerous terrain,
unfamiliar equipment, uncertain identification, poor body condition or
high-consequence choices remain directly playable.

## 4. The living quarry and fauna roster

### 4.1 Roster philosophy

The roster is ecological, not a checklist of targets. Each family can contain
regional variants generated from the island seed, wreck history and Triangle
displacements. Exact species are selected during biome authoring.

Every authored species record requires:

1. habitat and range;
2. diet and ecological role;
3. activity rhythm;
4. senses and alarm behavior;
5. sign and calls;
6. movement, refuge and escape behavior;
7. social, nesting or schooling behavior;
8. breeding season and recruitment;
9. age/size/condition structure;
10. disease, toxin and contamination risks;
11. permitted or rational harvest methods;
12. non-target and protected lookalikes;
13. usable products and processing requirements;
14. response to human noise, light, roads, fire and pressure;
15. conservation or control status;
16. consequence of abundance, decline or removal.

### 4.2 Spawn Island baseline families

The private Spawn Island must support survival without becoming a supermarket.
Its exact initial ecology varies, but the baseline authoring pool is:

| Family | Examples/role | Typical interaction | Key danger or limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Invasive rodents | rats, mice, stowaways | track, exclude, live-capture or kill-trap, bait | disease, contaminated stores, non-target capture |
| Feral fowl | displaced or established chickens | eggs, observation, selective capture | nest depletion, disease, noise, overharvest |
| Small ground birds | rails, doves or analogous island birds | calls, feathers, nest study; limited harvest | endemic/protected confusion, breeding collapse |
| Seabirds | terns, shearwaters, gull-like scavengers | weather sign, egg pressure, fish-location clues | colonies are vulnerable; most are protected/no-take |
| Small introduced mammals | rabbits or analogous herbivores where habitat supports them | sign, stalking, trapping | population boom/crash, crop damage, disease |
| Rare feral stock | goat or pig only after authored introduction and sufficient habitat | control hunt, live capture, husbandry fork | severe habitat damage, charge/injury, disease |
| Shore crabs | intertidal and land-crab families | hand capture, baited pot, night observation | claws, tide, nursery depletion, contamination |
| Shellfish | bivalves, gastropods and analogous beds | hand gathering by zone and tide | toxins, water quality, slow bed recovery |
| Spiny lobster-like crustaceans | reef crevice scavengers | seasonal noose/capture, survey | diving risk, breeding season, undersize take |
| Lagoon fish | mullet-like, mojarras, baitfish and small predators | handline, cast net where selective, traps | nursery depletion, low oxygen, contamination |
| Reef fish | snapper-, grunt-, hogfish- and grouper-like roles | line, pole spear, trap or observation | size/age structure, ciguatera-like toxin, reef damage |
| Pelagic visitors | tuna-, wahoo-, mahi-like roles | boat line, trolling, drift observation | boat/weather dependence, mass and preservation |
| Invasive reef predator | lionfish-like ecological role | selective pole spear or trap | venomous spines, handling, depth |
| Sharks and rays | mobile predators and scavengers | avoidance, research, deterrence, rare release | attracted by struggling catch; normally no-take |
| Turtles and marine mammals | rare protected presence | observation, disentanglement, rescue | never ordinary food; interference can be lethal |
| Cave/endemic invertebrates | anchialine and submerged-cave life | survey and protection | tiny range; contamination can erase a lineage |

No exact land animal is guaranteed solely because it is useful. Large animals
require enough water, forage, range and breeding population to exist honestly.

### 4.3 Community Island expanded families

The larger Community Island may sustain:

- managed feral pig, goat, sheep-like or deer-like displaced populations only
  where island size and history make them viable;
- more substantial wetlands with waterfowl-like migrants;
- wider reef, shelf and pelagic fisheries;
- working poultry or small livestock descended from rare wreck arrivals;
- pest and invasive-control programs;
- protected rookeries, nurseries and cave reserves;
- shared hunting grounds, fishing grounds and seasonal closures;
- research-tagged animals and recovery populations;
- animals privately tended, communally managed or claimed under local law.

These are not “higher-tier animals.” They create different logistics,
institutions, relationships and moral decisions.

### 4.4 Status states

Every population has one current management state:

| State | Meaning | Rational player response |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown | insufficient evidence | observe, record, avoid broad take |
| Stable harvest | recruitment supports limited selective use | maintain records and restraint |
| Vulnerable | low abundance, poor recruitment or habitat stress | reduce/stop take, protect habitat |
| Protected | ecological, cultural, research or recovery value | no take; rescue/report if harmed |
| Invasive control | population damages food, habitat or health | selective sustained control with monitoring |
| Outbreak concern | disease or contamination suspected | isolate, sample, avoid consumption |
| Emergency subsistence | immediate survival pressure | minimal necessary take with future cost recorded |
| Domestic/claimed | human stewardship or ownership applies | permission, care and law rather than wild harvest |

An emergency can justify a desperate act in fiction without making its
ecological or social consequences disappear.

## 5. Animal perception, behavior and learning

### 5.1 Sensory world

Animals perceive different combinations of:

- motion, silhouette, contrast and gaze;
- scent cone, wind shift and contaminated approach;
- sound intensity, rhythm and substrate vibration;
- water pressure, turbulence, shadow and electrical/magnetic cues where
  appropriate;
- unnatural light, fire, engines, metal impacts and repeated routes;
- alarm calls and the flight of other species;
- bait condition, blood, waste and distressed prey.

The player never receives a universal stealth number. The same movement can be
hidden from one species and obvious to another.

### 5.2 Behavioral state

Each local animal can transition among:

> **feed/rest → orient → concern → avoid → flee/hide → defend → recover →
> resume or relocate**

States depend on need, cover, group, young, injury, escape route and previous
human pressure. Predators and scavengers may investigate a catch; prey may
freeze rather than flee; schooling fish may compress or scatter; a cornered
animal may attack.

### 5.3 Memory and pressure

Repeated disturbance changes:

- active hours;
- distance from paths and settlements;
- response to boats, lights, bait and calls;
- refuge selection;
- group cohesion;
- trap and lure avoidance;
- migration or abandonment.

The island therefore becomes harder through the player's own predictable
pressure, not through invisible enemy level scaling.

### 5.4 Sign is produced by life

Tracks are not decorative decals. Sign comes from simulated activity:

- footprints, trails, wallows, rubs, browsing and digging;
- droppings, pellets, feathers, hair, scales and molt;
- nests, roosts, burrows, beds and feeding remains;
- calls, alarm silence and predator commotion;
- surface strikes, wakes, bubbles, schooling and bird activity;
- damaged crops, gnawed stores and bait loss;
- carcasses, disease behavior and scavenger concentration.

Rain, tide, wind, substrate, age and later traffic degrade or confuse evidence.

## 6. Land hunting and capture methods

### 6.1 Method families

| Method | Best use | Crossed capabilities | Binding weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sign survey | learn presence, route and abundance | Perception, tracking, records, weather | yields knowledge, not immediate food |
| Still hunting | slow travel through likely habitat | quiet movement, wind, patience | fatigue and shifting wind |
| Spot and stalk | visible quarry in open/mixed terrain | optics, terrain, route, concealment | exposure, range and escape cover |
| Ambush/blind | repeated trail, feed or water route | habitat reading, concealment, waiting | wrong pattern or changed pressure |
| Call/decoy | species-specific social response | identification, timing, restraint | can attract non-targets or predators |
| Drive/flush | Community team moves quarry toward controlled zone | communication, terrain, safe sectors | high accident and social risk |
| Live capture | relocation, husbandry, study or control | trap design, handling, enclosure | stress, injury, escape and ongoing care |
| Restraining trap | selective small-animal capture | sign, placement, welfare, check routine | non-target injury, weather and neglect |
| Kill trap | pest or subsistence control | species fit, placement, maintenance | selectivity and humane-function burden |
| Snare/cable restraint | narrow trail use where appropriate | loop/anchor knowledge, sign, frequent checks | entanglement, non-targets, poor welfare |
| Pit/barrier/funnel | small terrestrial or amphibious capture | terrain, construction, escape prevention | flooding and indiscriminate capture |
| Opportunistic defense | immediate animal threat | Composure, movement, barrier/weapon | not a repeatable hunting method |

Traps are persistent devices. They require ownership, map location, weather
inspection, a check deadline and a deactivation state. An abandoned active trap
or lost net can continue harming animals and people.

### 6.2 Trapping quality

Trap performance depends on:

- target anatomy and behavior;
- selectivity and exclusion features;
- trigger force and mechanical condition;
- anchor strength and escape load;
- cover, weather and water exposure;
- bait and scent history;
- human route and bystander risk;
- inspection interval;
- live-release or dispatch readiness;
- record and recovery.

Best practice is evaluated through animal welfare, efficiency, selectivity,
safety and practicality—not capture probability alone.

### 6.3 Team-hunt safety

Community drives, large-animal control and night searches require:

- named leader and stop authority;
- known participants and positions;
- clear safe sectors and forbidden backgrounds;
- target and non-target identification;
- communication and lost-contact rule;
- wounded-animal recovery plan;
- medical and casualty extraction plan;
- meat transport and cold plan;
- witness and harvest record.

Strength may help control a load or defend against a charge. It does not
replace route planning, safe fields of fire or Composure.

## 7. Fishing, shellfish and marine harvest methods

### 7.1 Fishing is several disciplines

| Method | Environment/use | Main skill crossings | Main cost or danger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand gathering | rocks, flats, exposed shore | species ID, tide, footing, water quality | cuts, toxins, wave trap, bed depletion |
| Handline | shore, dock, raft or boat | knots, bait/lure, bite reading, line control | hand cuts, snags, poor leverage |
| Rod and reel | casting and controlled retrieval | Dexterity, timing, drag, maintenance | damaged guides/reel, line loss |
| Float line | suspended bait at known depth | depth, current, strike recognition | drift and entanglement |
| Bottom line | reef/shelf bottom species | depth, substrate, weight, barotrauma judgment | snag, habitat damage, release mortality |
| Jig/lure | active presentation | species behavior, rhythm, visibility | labor, lure loss, wrong presentation |
| Trolling | moving craft and pelagic fish | seamanship, speed, spread, watch | weather, fuel, line crossing, large load |
| Cast net | visible small schooling fish where appropriate | timing, spread, retrieval, selectivity | bycatch, nursery pressure, entanglement |
| Seine | shallow team harvest | habitat, crew coordination, sorting | destructive if dragged across nursery/reef |
| Gill/fixed net | only under exceptional managed rules | mesh/behavior, marking, frequent tending | bycatch and ghost fishing; normally prohibited |
| Fish trap/pot | reef or bottom species | entrance/mesh, bait, marker, retrieval | ghost fishing, theft, storm loss |
| Crustacean pot/noose | crab/lobster-like quarry | season, size, habitat, handling | undersize/breeder take, diving risk |
| Pole spear | selective breath-hold fish harvest | diving, species ID, range, recovery | hypoxia, wound/loss, venom and sharks |
| Speargun | only where Community law and ecology permit | advanced dive, maintenance, shot restraint | stored energy, line entanglement, misuse |
| Bow fishing | shallow visible invasive fish only where rational | refraction judgment, archery, retrieval line | non-target wound and line hazard |
| Fish weir/funnel | tidal channel or managed lagoon | tide, structure, release gate | blocks movement; high stewardship burden |
| Bait capture | small fish, crabs, worms or analogues | microhabitat, handling, reserve | removing prey can starve higher fishery |
| Dive observation | census, species/size selection, nursery mapping | v0.10 diving + reef ecology | not itself a harvest; exposure |
| Remote camera/sonar | deep or unsafe water | electronics, interpretation, mapping | false certainty and maintenance |

Poisoning, explosives, electrical stunning and indiscriminate habitat
destruction are prohibited design routes. They may exist only as crimes,
historical evidence or ecological disasters, never efficient player recipes.

### 7.2 Tackle is a system

A fishing setup is assembled from:

- line material, diameter, condition and visibility;
- leader and abrasion resistance;
- hook material, size, shape, barb and corrosion behavior;
- knot matched to line and load;
- sinker or weight;
- float;
- swivel or anti-twist provision;
- bait, lure or attractor;
- rod, pole, reel or hand protection;
- drag or load-release behavior;
- landing device;
- dehooking/cutting tool;
- storage and rinse routine.

Changing one part changes casting, sink rate, selectivity, presentation,
failure mode and repair. “Better rod” is not a linear damage upgrade.

### 7.3 The bite and fight

Fish interaction depends on:

- species feeding mode and activity;
- light, tide, current, temperature and water quality;
- bait/lure presentation;
- hook geometry and timing;
- line angle, tension and abrasion;
- fish mass, stamina and structure nearby;
- survivor grip, balance, fatigue and technique;
- boat drift and crew coordination.

The objective is controlled landing or release, not reducing an aquatic health
bar.

### 7.4 Bycatch and release

The player must be able to:

- identify the animal while minimizing air exposure;
- control hands, hooks and teeth/spines safely;
- cut line when removal would cause worse damage;
- use appropriate release tools;
- return depth-affected reef fish with a descending method where applicable;
- revive only when species and conditions support it;
- record mortality rather than treating release as guaranteed survival.

Circle/barbless options, non-persistent hook materials, selective mesh,
escape panels and frequently tended gear can reduce harm, but none makes a
method consequence-free.

### 7.5 Ghost gear

Lost pots, nets, lines and hooks persist. They can:

- continue capturing wildlife;
- entangle divers, turtles, boats and propellers;
- abrade reef;
- reveal ownership or negligence;
- wash ashore for recovery;
- become a cleanup, salvage or justice Open Loop.

Storm preparation includes retrieving or securing fishing gear.

## 8. Weapons, tools and equipment

### 8.1 Observation and approach

- field notebook, map, charcoal/pencil and specimen labels;
- measuring cord, scale, ruler and camera;
- trail markers used without corrupting the trail;
- improvised viewing tube, binoculars, monocular and night-safe lamp;
- wind indicator, weather instruments and tide records;
- hide/blind materials and portable seat;
- quiet footwear, protective clothing, gloves and eye protection;
- whistle/radio for team separation and emergency;
- pack, game sling, basket, sled, cart, boat and cold container.

### 8.2 Primitive and fabricated hunting weapons

| Family | Requirements | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Throwing stick | selected stock, balance, open use | simple small-game tool | short range, poor control in clutter |
| Sling | fiber/leather, matched projectile, extensive practice | light and repairable | steep accuracy and safe-background demand |
| Thrusting spear | shaft, point, binding, controlled force | defense and close capture | dangerous proximity |
| Throwing spear/javelin | straight shaft, balance, point | moderate reach and recoverable | mass, arc and poor dense-cover use |
| Spear-thrower | geometry, darts and practiced release | efficient projectile leverage | specialized ammunition and training |
| Self bow | suitable stave, tillering, string and matched arrows | quiet, reusable and craft-rich | humidity, limb/string condition and practice |
| Composite/recovered bow | adhesives/materials or sound salvage | compact power | maintenance, delamination and rare parts |
| Crossbow | stock, prod, string, trigger and safe spanning | held aim and mechanical study | stored-energy accident, slow reload, complexity |
| Blowpipe-like tool | culturally/biologically appropriate small quarry only | quiet and simple projectile | very limited humane use without prohibited toxins |

Poisoned hunting weapons are not a player technology route. Uncertain dose,
food contamination and ecological harm make them unsuitable for this game.

### 8.3 Projectiles and archery equipment

Arrows, bolts and darts require:

- straightness and matched mass;
- shaft material and moisture condition;
- point purpose and attachment;
- fletching or stabilization;
- nock fit;
- compatibility with bow/crossbow;
- inspection after impact;
- safe transport and accounting.

Points fork among practice, small-game, fishing/retrieval and appropriate
large-game functions. A more damaging point is not universally better; it can
reduce penetration, destroy food, fail on bone, snag vegetation or be
impossible to recover.

### 8.4 Salvaged firearms

Firearms may arrive in military, private-vessel, police, sporting or survival
wreckage, but they are never a mandatory progression tier.

Their use requires:

- verification that the arm is complete, unloaded and mechanically sound;
- correct ammunition identity and condition;
- corrosion, obstruction, headspace and part inspection by relevant knowledge;
- safe storage and access control;
- sight confirmation and conservative range;
- target, foreground and background certainty;
- recoil, noise and hearing protection;
- misfire/hangfire procedure;
- cleaning and finite spares;
- Community law, ownership and evidence.

Cartridges are finite, humidity-sensitive and provenance-dependent. Reloading
requires exact components, tooling and knowledge; it is not a casual recipe.
A firearm offers reach and energy while introducing noise, social alarm,
catastrophic handling risk, limited supply and forensic trace.

### 8.5 Fishing weapons

- sharpened pole and multi-prong fish spear;
- detachable-head spear with recovery line;
- pole spear;
- speargun only after appropriate materials, maintenance and law;
- lobster/crustacean noose;
- landing gaff for suitable retained fish;
- landing net;
- club/baton for controlled dispatch where appropriate;
- line cutter accessible with either hand.

Spearfishing crosses v0.10's Calm Submersion, Reserve Reader, Current Reader,
Quiet Propulsion, Entanglement Discipline and Reef Steward. Strength alone is
nearly irrelevant if the diver cannot identify a legal target, manage breath,
control position and recover safely.

### 8.6 Processing kit

- washable field sheet or clean hanging arrangement;
- dedicated sharp knife and backup edge;
- sharpening stone/steel and edge guard;
- saw or joint tool for suitable large carcasses;
- gloves where available;
- water and sanitation supplies;
- cordage, hooks, gambrel-like spreader or lifting rig;
- clean containers separated by tissue/use;
- thermometer where recovered or fabricated;
- salt, smokehouse racks, drying mesh, cool box or refrigeration;
- waste pit/container positioned away from water and camp;
- labels recording species, location, time and preservation history.

## 9. Wound, capture and recovery simulation

### 9.1 No damage-number hunting

The animal outcome is derived from:

- projectile/weapon energy and geometry;
- impact location and angle;
- penetration path;
- tissue and bone interaction;
- blood loss, respiratory compromise, mobility and shock;
- animal mass and current exertion;
- time and secondary injury;
- environmental escape opportunity.

The UI shows observed evidence—flight, gait, sound, sign and trail—not an
enemy health bar or guaranteed kill icon.

### 9.2 Opportunity judgment

The survivor assesses:

- confirmed species and allowed individual;
- clear view of vital anatomy appropriate to the method;
- distance and angle within proven capability;
- safe foreground and background;
- no hidden person, domestic animal or protected animal;
- terrain that permits recovery;
- sufficient daylight/weather/body reserve;
- equipment condition;
- transport and preservation capacity.

Composure is the ability to refuse marginal opportunity, not a critical-hit
stat.

### 9.3 Following up

Recovery can use:

- last-seen point and direction;
- tracks, broken cover, blood or disturbed water;
- sound, circling scavengers and alarm calls;
- line, float, marker or radio report;
- grid, expanding arc or shoreline/current search;
- a Community tracking team;
- camera, drone-like recovered system or acoustic aid where available.

Rushing can push an animal farther. Waiting can improve a trail but lose
daylight, weather or meat condition. The correct decision depends on the wound,
species, terrain and risk.

### 9.4 Capture handling

Live animals can injure themselves or the handler. Safe handling depends on:

- species-specific restraint;
- ventilation and thermal condition;
- minimizing handling time;
- hand/eye/airway protection;
- separation from food and domestic stock;
- quarantine;
- clear release, treatment, relocation or husbandry purpose.

“Taming” is not a progress bar. Long-term domestication is not achievable
within one survivor's short practice. Individual habituation and husbandry are
possible only with care, facilities and suitable animals.

## 10. Field processing, meat safety and whole use

### 10.1 Processing chain

> **confirm death/containment → inspect health → protect work area → bleed or
> drain where appropriate → remove contamination source → separate clean and
> dirty work → cool → divide by intended use → transport → preserve → record**

Species-specific anatomy and method alter the chain. The game need not become
graphic; meaningful hand placement, tool cleanliness, sequence, temperature
and time can be represented without visual excess.

### 10.2 Rejection cues

The survivor may reject all or part of an animal because of:

- abnormal behavior before death;
- unexplained death or uncertain cause;
- obvious disease, parasites or unusual organs;
- chemical, fuel, sewage, algal or heavy-metal exposure;
- advanced decomposition;
- ruptured digestive contents and uncontrolled contamination;
- extended warm time;
- species-specific toxin or advisory;
- unknown provenance from wreckfall;
- protected status or Community evidence requirement.

An animal found dead is evidence first, not free food.

### 10.3 Cross-contamination

Raw animal work can contaminate:

- hands and wounds;
- knives and sharpening tools;
- water containers;
- kitchen surfaces;
- cooked food;
- clothing and bedding;
- bait and feed stores;
- other carcasses.

Dedicated tools, gloves, washing, separation, clean containers and complete
heat history matter. The survivor can learn from symptoms and records, but
expertise cannot make unsafe meat safe.

### 10.4 Preservation routes

| Route | Needs | Advantage | Hidden failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediate cooking | fuel, clean surface, adequate heat | fastest safe use | uneven heat, contaminated utensil |
| Chilling | ice/cold water system/refrigeration, sealed drainage | preserves fresh quality | power history and warm center |
| Drying | thin preparation, airflow, insect protection, low enough humidity | low fuel and voyage food | humid case-hardening, insects, incomplete kill |
| Smoking | controlled heat/smoke, airflow, time | preservation plus flavor | smoke is not automatic sterilization |
| Salting/brining | sufficient salt, clean container, concentration control | robust storage route | weak brine, contamination, dehydration cost |
| Fermentation | known culture, salt, temperature and vessel | diversity and preservation | wrong organism or gas/seal failure |
| Pressure canning | sound vessel, heat/pressure knowledge, seals | long storage | catastrophic under-processing or vessel failure |
| Freezing | reliable sustained power and packaging | long storage | thaw history; some parasites remain a concern |

### 10.5 Whole-animal branches

| Material | Possible branches | Required crossings |
|---|---|---|
| Meat | meals, rationing, preserved stores, trade | anatomy, hygiene, heat, nutrition |
| Fat/oil | food, lamp, soap, dressing or technical use where suitable | rendering, contamination knowledge, storage |

Waste near camp or water attracts scavengers, contaminates water, spreads
disease and teaches animals to approach people.

## 12. Sixteen non-linear development braids

### Braid A — A print becomes an island map

Unknown print  
→ photograph/measure **or** follow cautiously  
→ compare substrate, weather and gait  
→ identify species **or** retain uncertainty  
→ feeding route / water route / nest / human disturbance  
→ hunt / avoid / protect crop / census / disease investigation

### Braid B — The missing ration

Damaged store  
→ inspect gnawing, droppings and access  
→ exclude entry **or** move food **or** set selective trap  
→ identify rodent/insect/bird  
→ sanitation / live relocation / kill control  
→ pest-free archive **or** disease Open Loop **or** invasive program

### Braid C — The first bow

Suitable wood observation  
→ staff/tool handle **or** test bending stock  
→ tiller practice piece  
→ cordage quality and string  
→ matched practice arrows  
→ range proof and safe background  
→ small-game use **or** fishing arrow **or** remain a toolmaker/teacher

A found modern bow can enter at equipment familiarity, but it does not bypass
range proof, opportunity judgment or recovery.

### Braid D — The rare large animal

Habitat damage / unfamiliar sign  
→ survey and camera evidence  
→ displaced goat/pig-like population confirmed  
→ protect, live-capture, husbandry, isolate **or** control  
→ solo avoidance **or** Community team hunt  
→ meat/hide/disease sample  
→ eradication / managed population / ecological recovery / social dispute

### Braid E — Egg hunger becomes stewardship

Nest located  
→ species and colony condition uncertain  
→ observe adult return and count nests  
→ take none **or** minimal emergency take  
→ protect colony from rats/fire/light  
→ future fish-location and storm cues  
→ Community protected rookery / research / seasonal subsistence rule

### Braid F — Shore food becomes marine knowledge

Low-tide gathering  
→ species and contamination recognition  
→ repeated bed map  
→ harvest rotation **or** closure  
→ bait discovery / shell craft / water-quality warning  
→ reef fishing / aquaculture experiment / sanitation response

### Braid G — The first handline

Recovered line/hook **or** fabricated line/point  
→ knot and load test  
→ bait observation **or** lure experiment  
→ shore/raft/boat choice  
→ bite interpretation and line control  
→ retain/release/lose  
→ food / tackle revision / species record / deeper fishing route

### Braid H — Reef observation becomes selective spearfishing

Snorkel observation  
→ nursery and protected species map  
→ breath and current discipline  
→ pole-spear practice on inert target  
→ choose invasive or abundant legal fish  
→ clean take **or** wound/recovery operation **or** abort  
→ food / Reef Steward / population restoration / diving medicine

### Braid I — A net becomes an institution

Fiber and knots  
→ small test net  
→ mesh selectivity experiment  
→ cast net **or** landing net **or** trap panel  
→ catch and bycatch record  
→ repair / redesign / abandon harmful method  
→ Community gear standard / net maker / ghost-gear service

### Braid J — Pelagic evidence

Birds/current/debris/temperature clue  
→ repeated offshore observation  
→ boat and return envelope  
→ troll/jig/line setup  
→ large-fish handling and cold plan  
→ catch **or** abort **or** rescue diversion  
→ food/trade / migration map / weather service / voyage preparation

### Braid K — A wounded animal becomes a tracker

Observed impact uncertain  
→ mark last-seen point  
→ immediate follow **or** wait based on evidence  
→ trail interpretation  
→ recover, require team, lose trail or find non-target  
→ dispatch/treat/reject  
→ revise range, point, opportunity and teaching doctrine

Surviving a reckless shot does not grant mastery. Honest reconstruction does.

### Braid L — Carcass becomes infrastructure

Recovered animal  
→ health inspection  
→ clean field processing  
→ meat now / preserve / reject  
→ hide / bone / sinew / feather / shell branches  
→ clothing, bindings, tools, lures, fertilizer or research  
→ workshop specialization and successor cache

### Braid M — Illness rewrites food practice

Meal history  
→ symptoms appear  
→ isolate likely sources  
→ compare species, handling, temperature and diners  
→ treatment/support + discard/quarantine  
→ revise tools and process  
→ Food Safety Keeper / outbreak record / Community inspection

### Braid N — Wreckfall brings more than gear

Fishing vessel or aircraft arrives empty  
→ secure fuel/fire/structure  
→ recover tackle, cold system and records  
→ detect rats, insects, eggs, larvae or live animal  
→ quarantine/contain **or** careless spread  
→ new fishery capability **and/or** invasive outbreak  
→ control program / husbandry / disease / ecological transformation

### Braid O — Private knowledge becomes Community law

Spawn Island catch records  
→ crossing preserves archive  
→ Community fisher compares local trend  
→ disagreement and joint survey  
→ evidence supports size/season/area/gear rule  
→ compliance, evasion or conflict  
→ healthier fishery / black market / warden institution / revised rule

### Braid P — Death leaves a field lesson

Survivor fails to return  
→ body, weapon, active trap or boat remains  
→ next survivor finds map/gear **or** only physical evidence  
→ deactivate hazards and reconstruct chain  
→ recover records **or** repeat ignorance  
→ memorial warning / improved cache / Community report / continued risk

No braid is a mandatory corridor. Each crosses earlier documents and can be
entered from salvage, food shortage, ecology, craft, medicine, diving,
Community teaching, a death scene or an animal-created problem.

## 13. Two-island provisioning cycle

### 13.1 Private Spawn Island

The Spawn Island supports:

- solitary sign reading and population mapping;
- rodents, crop pests and invasive control;
- eggs/small game only under real population limits;
- shore gathering, handlines, traps and reef observation;
- small-scale bow, spear, tackle, trap and net craft;
- carcass hygiene, drying, smoking and whole-use experiments;
- persistent blinds, trap maps, cold stores, smokehouses and records;
- wreckfall gear and rare non-human introductions;
- direct return-home provisioning or food for the Community crossing.

It does not guarantee large game, ammunition, a fishing boat or a safe fishery.
The player can permanently damage its food web.

### 13.2 Community Island

The Community Island adds:

- specialist hunters, fishers, trappers, divers, butchers and ecologists;
- teaching that accelerates understanding but not physical adaptation;
- team hunts, drive/flush methods and large-load recovery;
- boats, harbors, ice, market chains and communal preservation;
- ownership, permits, customary grounds and contested access;
- public catch records, spawning closures and protected areas;
- fishery councils, wildlife wardens and public-health inspection;
- black markets, poaching, sabotage and forensic evidence;
- rescue, search, humane handling and invasive-control teams;
- ceremonies, meals and identity built around food without reducing culture to
  buffs.

### 13.3 Movement between islands

Transporting catch across water requires:

- time-to-spoil estimate;
- clean and protected container;
- ice/cooling or preserved form;
- boat load and stability allowance;
- weather and delay margin;
- quarantine where disease or invasive transfer is possible;
- proof of ownership and legal harvest on Community Island.

A fish is not tradable merely because it entered inventory.

## 14. Difficulty and challenge without grind

Hunting and fishing become challenging through interacting truths:

- rain erases tracks but creates mud sign elsewhere;
- wind hides sound while carrying scent;
- drought concentrates animals at water but makes populations vulnerable;
- storms move fish, damage reef and expose or bury shell beds;
- heat shortens carcass handling time;
- cold water reduces dive and boat work capacity;
- moon and tide alter shore access and current;
- wildfire displaces animals and removes cover;
- wreckfall creates new structure, contamination and invasive organisms;
- repeated hunting makes animals nocturnal or relocates them;
- crop expansion removes habitat but attracts pests;
- a successful fishery draws labor, markets, theft and regulation;
- injury can close archery, knife, boat or diving routes while leaving tracking,
  trap design, record analysis or teaching open;
- Community conflict can close a ground while trade or survey opens another.

The game does not make late animals absorb more hits. Difficulty emerges from
knowledge, uncertainty, logistics, ecology and consequence.

### 14.1 Anti-grind law

Repeated low-risk action ceases to produce meaningful learning once the
relevant movement is stable. Growth requires changed context:

- different substrate or weather;
- unfamiliar species or sign;
- damaged or substituted equipment;
- team coordination;
- tighter selectivity;
- illness/injury accommodation;
- teaching and diagnosis;
- population management;
- proof in a real but rational operation.

Thousands of identical casts do not produce a master fisher.

## 15. Failure and fatal chains

### 15.1 Hunting failures

- misidentification of protected, domestic or diseased animal;
- unsafe background or unseen companion;
- bow/crossbow limb, string or trigger failure;
- firearm corrosion, obstruction, wrong ammunition, misfire or unsafe storage;
- spear or knife injury during close control;
- charge, kick, bite, antler/tusk-like trauma or crush;
- fall while stalking, tracking or carrying;
- becoming lost after following sign past turnaround;
- heat, dehydration or exhaustion during pursuit;
- wounded-animal recovery after dark;
- trap injury, non-target capture or forgotten active trap;
- carcass mass causing strain, rollover or boat instability;
- infection through cut hands or contaminated tools.

### 15.2 Fishing and shore failures

- swept rock, rising tide, surf knockdown or reef cut;
- hook in hand/eye, line cut or tackle recoil;
- line/net entanglement;
- dragged from unstable footing by a large fish;
- capsized or overloaded small craft;
- diving blackout or failed surf exit;
- venomous spine, bite, allergic reaction or toxic species;
- shark/scavenger attracted by struggling or bleeding catch;
- barotrauma and waste from deep catch;
- lost pot/net continuing to kill;
- shellfish toxin, ciguatera-like risk or contaminated water;
- cold-chain loss, undercooking or cross-contamination.

### 15.3 Causal example

> Pelagic signs observed at noon → survivor launches late without a cooling
> plan → larger fish than expected strikes → loose line crosses ankle → sudden
> tension causes fall → unattended tiller turns boat → survivor cannot reach
> cutter → capsize under asymmetric load → failed reboarding in rising wind →
> drowning.

The cause is not “fishing level too low.” Warnings and interventions existed at
gear layout, launch time, forecast, line management, boat control, load and
reboarding readiness.

## 16. Community economy, ownership and justice

Every harvested item carries provenance:

- species/family;
- approximate size/condition;
- place and time;
- method and gear owner;
- harvester/crew;
- protected or managed-ground status;
- field and preservation history;
- inspection or unresolved concern.

This allows:

- honest trade premiums for safe, selective catch;
- rejection of uncertain meat;
- theft and poaching evidence;
- outbreak tracing;
- quota/closure debate;
- compensation for damaged gear;
- shared ownership of team-hunt yield;
- public provisioning contracts;
- institution memory after an individual dies.

Poaching is not a red-name debuff. It is a physical and social chain involving
tracks, missing tagged animals, cartridges, projectiles, net marks, boat
movements, witnesses, records and possession.

## 17. Interface and player comprehension

### 17.1 What the player sees

The normal view shows:

- physical sign in the world;
- posture, movement, sound and animal reaction;
- line tension and equipment state through animation/audio;
- readable item condition;
- a small contextual verb set;
- plain-language uncertainty when the survivor can interpret it.

It does not show:

- animal health bars;
- exact detection cones;
- guaranteed catch percentages;
- magical species labels before recognition;
- glowing blood-trail arrows;
- a universal Hunting/Fishing skill tree.

### 17.2 Field journal

The journal gradually records:

- confirmed and uncertain species;
- sketches/photos/sign;
- observed times, habitats and behavior;
- locations with uncertainty bounds;
- catch/harvest and released non-targets;
- equipment setups and failures;
- processing and illness histories;
- population trends and closures;
- warnings written for later survivors.

Knowledge survives death only if it has a physical carrier: notebook, marked
map, labeled specimen, maintained station, camera archive, Community teacher or
institutional ledger.

### 17.3 Accessibility

Accessibility can strengthen:

- track contrast without inventing tracks;
- directional audio visualization;
- line-tension vibration/visual cues;
- color-independent floats and status markers;
- hold/toggle alternatives;
- aim stability accommodations that do not identify targets or choose shots;
- reduced graphic processing presentation.

It must preserve identification, judgment and consequence.

## 20. Acceptance tests

The system fails its constitution if:

- animals spawn as isolated food pickups;
- a universal hunting/fishing number governs success;
- buying/finding a weapon grants accuracy or species knowledge;
- a wounded animal despawns;
- an unchecked trap stops existing offscreen;
- lost net has no consequence;
- all fish respond to the same bait and timing;
- all land animals share the same senses and flee behavior;
- every larger animal takes more hits merely because it is “higher tier”;
- carcass processing is one button with no time, hygiene or transport;
- smoking/drying/freezing automatically removes every hazard;
- overharvest only lengthens a respawn timer;
- protected and invasive species have no gameplay distinction;
- Strength substitutes for tracking, accuracy or ecology;
- Reasoning substitutes for practiced hands;
- the Spawn Island guarantees large game;
- the Community Island has markets but no provenance, law or public health;
- firearms become abundant linear upgrades;
- death can be committed by a disconnect or simulation downgrade.

The system passes when a player can tell a story such as:

> “I found fewer reef fish, but I did not assume bad luck. My earlier runoff
> had silted the nursery; the remaining large fish were breeders. I closed that
> ground, trapped invasive lionfish-like predators farther out, repaired an old
> handline, traded preserved catch for seed, and left the new survivor a map
> explaining why the easy lagoon must remain untouched.”

## 21. Research translation

This is a game constitution, not real-world hunting, trapping, diving, food
safety or firearms instruction. It translates high-level principles into
causal play.

### 21.1 Responsible fisheries

The FAO Code of Conduct for Responsible Fisheries calls for selective,
environmentally safe gear and practices, reduced waste and non-target catch,
habitat and biodiversity protection, and recorded information for management.
The game therefore gives gear selectivity, bycatch, ghost fishing, habitat
effects and catch records mechanical weight.

- FAO, *Code of Conduct for Responsible Fisheries*:  
  https://openknowledge.fao.org/bitstreams/23312a48-cb99-4abf-b7e9-4f2f38604695/download

NOAA guidance supports circle/barbless or crimped-barb options, non-stainless
hooks in relevant contexts, and descending devices for depth-affected reef
fish. The game treats release survival as an operation, not a binary button.

- NOAA Fisheries, *Catch and Release Fishing Best Practices*:  
  https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/national/resources-fishing/catch-and-release-fishing-best-practices
- NOAA Fisheries, *Recreational Anglers Get Help Combatting Post-Release
  Mortality*:  
  https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/feature-story/recreational-anglers-get-help-combatting-post-release-mortality-partnership-fishery

### 21.2 Trapping quality

Modern trapping best-management work evaluates welfare, efficiency,
selectivity, safety and practicality. Those become required trap authoring
axes, together with check routines and persistent abandoned-gear consequences.

- Association of Fish & Wildlife Agencies, *Furbearer Management and Best
  Management Practices*:  
  https://www.fishwildlife.org/afwa-inspires/furbearer-management

### 21.3 Wild-food health

CDC material shows why game handling cannot end at the kill: abnormal or dead
animals, raw/cooked separation, hand and tool hygiene, adequate cooking and
species/disease context matter. Some wild-game parasites are not reliably
controlled by drying, smoking, curing or freezing alone.

- CDC, *Hunters and Bird Flu*:  
  https://www.cdc.gov/bird-flu/risk-factors/hunters-and-bird-flu.html
- CDC, *About Chronic Wasting Disease*:

### 21.4 Bermuda ecological grounding

The game islands are fictional and anomalous, not a one-to-one simulation of
Bermuda. Bermuda nevertheless provides useful Atlantic-island evidence:
minimum sizes, bag limits, hook limits, gear restrictions, protected areas,
spawning closures, catch reporting, lobster seasons and protected species all
demonstrate that method, place, season, size and evidence matter together.

## 22. Final canon statement

Hunting and fishing are now a complete connected discipline:

> **Read life → choose with restraint → prepare honestly → take selectively →
> recover what was harmed → process safely → use fully → record truth →
> protect the next population.**

The private Spawn Island teaches that one human can become observant,
resourceful and dangerous without ever becoming omniscient. The Community
Island proves that lasting abundance requires shared evidence, institutions
and restraint.

The player may become a tracker, trapper, bowyer, fisher, spearfisher,
butcher, hide worker, invasive controller, wildlife warden, provision planner
or fauna systems ecologist—but never by selecting a class.

They become one through the crossing of body, senses, knowledge, tools,
weather, ecology, consequence and proof.
