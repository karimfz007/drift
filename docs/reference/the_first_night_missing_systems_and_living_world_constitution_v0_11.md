**Missing-Systems Audit, Continuity, Operations and Living-World Constitution
v0.11 — 26 July 2026**

**Project:** THE FIRST NIGHT  
**Internal codename:** DRIFT / RUSTED  
**Engine and target:** Godot 4.x; first-person 3D; web and Android  
**Status:** Authoritative supplement to v0.7, v0.8, v0.9 and v0.10  
**Implementation authority:** This document does not authorize expansion before
the v0.1.2 physical interaction-recovery build passes Director testing.

---

## 1. Executive verdict

The existing canon is already unusually complete in four areas:

1. human survival, material identity, crafting and permanent character death;
2. interacting hazards, food ecology and non-linear capability development;
3. the private Spawn Island, Community Island and repeated castaway cycle;
4. swimming, diving, submerged geography and wreck salvage.

The audit found that the next risk is not lack of content. It is lack of an
operating layer between that content.

The game already explains what hunger, a storm, a wreck, a crop, a forge, a
diver, a raft and an ending can do. It does not yet fully explain:

- why the player chooses one problem now instead of twenty others;
- how time, distance, transport and storage convert a plan into actual work;
- how a mature island remains challenging without arbitrary disasters;
- how knowledge survives a death without becoming magical account XP;
- how a new human reaches a Spawn Island whose wrecks never contain people;
- how that stranger accesses or fails to access a dead survivor's locked legacy;
- how authored inhabitants and players make Community Island function while
  nobody is looking at them;
- how social life becomes worth preserving instead of being another resource
  machine;
- how the Bermuda anomaly is investigated rather than merely collected;
- how thousands of persistent objects and people remain credible on Android and
  web;
- how permanent death is protected from disconnects, save corruption, server
  faults and version migrations;
- what successful escape means for the player, the survivor and the islands.

The missing signature is:

> **Matter must move. Systems must age. Knowledge must be externalized. People
> must remember. Time must close some doors and open others.**

v0.11 therefore adds a fifth governing weave:

> **The Continuity and Operations Weave**

It connects the four existing designs across lives, locations, sessions and
communities. It is not a new meter. It is the grammar by which the whole game
keeps working.

| Entropy | Can infrastructure fail for legible, preventable reasons? |
| Knowledge | Can observations, plans and errors survive a character death physically? |
| Identity | Is each new castaway a credible different human? |
| Health | Do illness, disability and recovery extend beyond a wound timer? |
| Ecology | Do animals behave as populations rather than spawnable ingredients? |
| Terrain | Are vertical and subterranean spaces first-class work domains? |
| Senses | Are danger and expertise expressed through the world, not only text? |
| Society | Do inhabitants have needs, agency, memory and obligations? |
| Conflict | Can one-life violence be prevented, survived, evidenced and resolved? |
| Culture | Is there a reason to preserve life after basic survival is solved? |
| Mystery | Can claims about the Triangle be tested and disproved? |
| Generation | Can islands vary without becoming unfair or incoherent? |
| Wreckfall | Does every arrival have provenance, consequences and a place in scarcity? |
| Persistence | Is offline and cross-island state constitutionally defined? |

| Closure | Does an ending close a human story without erasing the persistent world? |

Where v0.7–v0.10 already establish a rule, v0.11 preserves it. Where later
canon contradicts earlier canon, later canon remains authoritative:

- one survivor still has one irreversible life;
- the player continues through later castaways;
- the private Spawn Island persists across those deaths;
- it remains solitary and never receives living occupants through ordinary
  wreckfall;
- Community Island remains the exclusive social world;
- no universal level, XP pool or spendable skill points exists;
- no skill, tool or anomaly grants immunity from physics;
- diving is valuable but not mandatory for first-night survival;
- every common lethal chain needs readable warning and counterplay;
- v0.1.2 physical acceptance remains Gate 0.

## 4. New constitutional laws

### Law 19 — Every pressure competes with an opportunity

A storm does not only threaten. It fills cisterns, exposes wreckage, shifts fish,
opens or closes a passage and consumes preparation time. A calm day is not
absence of gameplay; it is a window for travel, drying, roofing, diving,
inspection or recovery.

The player should rarely face a single correct task. They should face a
legible choice among needs whose costs and deadlines differ.

### Law 20 — Distance is a material property

An engine on a reef is not workshop inventory. A tree across a ravine is not
timber. Water below a cliff is not hydration. Until matter is reached, secured,
transported, stored and made accessible, it remains a location-bound
possibility.

### Law 21 — Infrastructure converts acute danger into chronic obligation

A cistern defeats daily water collection and creates inspection, cleaning,
sealing and overflow work. A boat defeats a channel and creates mooring,
weather, maintenance and stores work. Progress is real relief plus new
responsibility—not a disguised treadmill.

### Law 22 — Knowledge exists in a carrier

Knowledge can exist in a living person's memory, practiced body, spoken lesson,
written page, diagram, labeled sample, calibrated instrument, tested jig,
working structure, scarred failure or witnessed event.

If it has no carrier, it does not persist.

### Law 23 — A new survivor is an arrival, not a resurrection

Death never transforms the dead body into a fresh body. A new adult human is
displaced separately onto the player's Spawn Island. That arrival is not an
occupant of an ordinary empty wreckfall and does not violate the no-people-in-
Spawn-wrecks rule.

### Law 24 — Privacy is architectural; access is physical

Other living people cannot enter a player's private Spawn Island. This protects
the island from raiding and spawn killing. It does not unlock doors, safes,
machines or archives for a new survivor.

The dead must have prepared succession access—or the newcomer must bypass,
repair, decode or abandon what was secured.

### Law 25 — Society is remembered action, not a faction bar

Trust attaches to people, deeds, evidence, promises, institutions and witnesses.
Coalitions form around issues and can change. No universal reputation number
makes every stranger like or hate a new survivor.

### Law 26 — The world speaks before the interface explains

Fire crackles, wet timber changes sound, a pump vibrates differently, a sick
crop smells and spots, a slope sheds grit, a storm changes swell, a cave loses
air movement and a tired hand trembles. Text clarifies what the character can
interpret; it should not be the only place reality exists.

### Law 27 — The anomaly may contradict models, never authorial fairness

Triangle effects can break a known prediction only after leaving measurable
contradiction. They cannot silently replace cause with magic, select the player
for punishment or erase preparation without a discoverable rule.

### Law 28 — Offline time may change property, never secretly kill agency

Supplied routine processes may advance. Crops may grow, batteries may discharge,
food may age and a known storm may pass. An absent protected character cannot
silently die, and a private island cannot be destroyed by an event the player
had no opportunity to perceive or answer.

### Law 29 — Technical uncertainty suspends death

Disconnect, server crash, corrupted transaction, duplicate authority,
unverified migration or impossible state cannot commit terminal death. The
system resolves to the last verified causal boundary and records the technical
incident separately.

This is fault recovery, not a player-accessible rollback.

### Law 30 — Simulation detail follows consequence

The foreground simulates exact bodies, hands, loads and hazards. Nearby systems
simulate actionable state. Distant or offline systems advance through bounded,
auditable ledgers. Reduced detail may postpone or aggregate resolution; it may
not invent a different physical truth.

## 5. The campaign heartbeat: why this, why now?

The current canon has excellent possible actions but needs a player-facing
method to expose priority without conventional quests.

The game uses **Open Loops**. An Open Loop is a perceived unresolved
relationship:

- “The cistern water smells wrong after the flood.”
- “The tide will uncover the propeller around dusk.”
- “The west roof tie is moving in gusts.”
- “Two days of dried provisions remain.”
- “A radio echo repeats every 47 minutes.”
- “Mara promised an inspection if the hull reaches Dock Two by tomorrow.”
- “The dead survivor's red cabinet is locked; a key diagram exists in the
  damaged archive.”

An Open Loop contains:

1. **evidence** — why the issue is believed to exist;
2. **stakes** — what may be gained, lost or learned;
3. **window** — now, soon, conditional or persistent;
4. **uncertainty** — what is not known;
5. **credible next actions** — usually two to five, including postpone or
   abandon;
6. **commitment** — what becomes difficult to reverse;
7. **memory** — how the result will remain in the world.

Open Loops are not orders. The world can create more than the player can solve.
Choosing what to leave undone is part of survival.

### 5.1 Three planning horizons

| Horizon | Player question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** | What threatens or enables the next few minutes? | bleeding, air, surf, fire, falling load, exposed salvage |
| **Soon** | What window closes this day or cycle? | tide, drying weather, crop harvest, storm preparation, meeting |
| **Long** | What project changes future possibilities? | archive, boatyard, clinic, route survey, escape program |

The interface may show one pinned Loop per horizon. It must not fill the screen
with twenty exclamation marks.

### 5.2 The playable action rhythm

> **Perceive → interpret → choose → prepare → commit → adapt → recover →
> record**

The loop supports short and long play:

- a three-minute session can inspect a roof, move food and update a storm plan;
- a twenty-minute session can survey a wreck or complete a field expedition;
- a long session can attempt a crossing, disaster response or departure trial.

No session-length advantage grants immunity. Long undertakings contain real
abort points and resumable safe boundaries.

### 5.3 No quest-board failure

Reject:

- NPCs who wait forever beside icons;
- arbitrary daily chores;
- rewards disconnected from the physical need;
- automatic waypoint knowledge;
- “complete three dives” objectives;
- hidden mandatory story gates;
- task lists that continue after the underlying problem disappeared.

A contract, plea, warning or research question may become an Open Loop, but it
must originate from a person, observation, institution or physical state.

## 6. One world clock, many rhythms

Time is currently present but not constitutionally reconciled across a private
instance, shared Community Island, solo sessions, offline persistence, seasons,
tides and survivor aging.

### 6.1 Canonical time ruling

The project uses one authoritative **World Date** for all connected spaces.

- Community Island always advances on server time.
- A connected Spawn Island advances on the same World Date.
- An offline private island is resolved on return through bounded background
  simulation.
- The Crossing Sea uses the destination world's current date and conditions.
- A new survivor arrives at the current World Date, not at a reset Day 1.

“Day 1” therefore means that survivor's first day, not the island's first day.
The island may already have years of history.

### 6.2 Bounded offline resolution

Offline simulation separates:

1. **deterministic consumption** — fuel, charge, stored feed, known water use;
2. **bounded biology** — growth, spoilage, pest pressure and disease progress;
3. **scheduled work** — only proven routines with supplied inputs and safe
   limits;
4. **weather exposure** — wear and non-terminal damage within declared
   protection rules;
5. **deferred incidents** — dangerous events pause at a readable intervention
   state instead of resolving unseen into arbitrary ruin.

Example:

> The player returns after a cyclone. The storm existed and the exposed pier is
> damaged. A staffed forge did not burn the island down while nobody could act.
> A poorly secured boat may be missing only if the player knowingly left it in
> an exposed state before logging off and received the departure warning.

### 6.3 Natural calendars

The island's rhythms include:

- daylight and darkness;
- heat and humidity cycle;
- lunar phase;
- local tide and current phase;
- wet, dry and storm tendencies;
- flowering, fruiting, spawning and migration windows;
- crop and seed cycles;
- insect/vector cycles;
- timber seasoning, fermentation and curing;
- corrosion, rot and maintenance intervals;
- recurring radio, wreckfall and anomaly patterns.

The player begins with observation, not a perfect calendar. Marks, shadows,
moon, tides, tagged plants, weather logs and instruments gradually turn cycles
into predictions.

### 6.4 Time disagreement remains an anomaly

Clock disagreement may occur only as a measured Triangle effect. The engine
retains authoritative transaction order even if characters disagree about
elapsed time. Spoilage, age and causality never become exploitable through
client clock changes.

## 7. The Spatial Logistics Weave

Crafting, building and salvage must include the work of moving matter. This
becomes a major non-linear progression domain.

### 7.1 A material has a logistics state

Every substantial item or batch can be:

> **In place → reachable → detachable → supportable → mobile → in transit →
> stored → accessible → staged → consumed/installed**

An item may be technically owned but operationally unavailable.

### 7.2 Movement methods

| Method | Strength | Limitation | Cross-skills |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hand carry | Immediate, precise | grip, mass, bulk, fatigue | Strength, Dexterity, route judgment |
| Drag/roll | Low technology | friction, damage, terrain | Strength, materials, path preparation |
| Pole/litter | Shares load | coordination, narrow routes | Composure, teamwork, casualty handling |
| Sled/travois | Good on prepared surfaces | turning and gradient | joinery, rope, route building |
| Wheelbarrow/cart | Efficient repeated hauling | wheel, axle, paths, maintenance | carpentry, mechanics, balance |
| Ropeway/zip line | Crosses slopes and gaps | anchors, braking, inspection | climbing, rigging, load paths |
| Hoist/crane/gin pole | Controls heavy lifts | foundation, overturning, lines | mechanics, structures, signaling |
| Float/tow | Moves exceptional mass | current, stability, landing | buoyancy, seamanship, rigging |
| Pack animal | Possible only if ecology permits | care, feed, disease, training | animal behavior, medicine, logistics |
| Vehicle/engine | High throughput | fuel, parts, roads, noise | mechanics, power, maintenance |

No method is a universal tier upgrade. A cart loses to a ropeway on a cliff; a
canoe loses to a hand carrier inside dense forest.

### 7.3 Routes become infrastructure

A path records use:

- vegetation clears or returns;
- mud, erosion and compaction change footing;
- bridges, steps, markers and drainage improve safety;
- repeated noise and scent alter animal behavior;
- ambush and observation possibilities change;
- travel-time evidence improves estimates;
- abandoned routes become uncertain again.

Routes can connect camps, water, fields, wrecks, caves, docks and emergency high
ground. A route network is one of the private island's greatest persistent
legacies.

### 7.4 Storage is not a bottomless box

Storage controls:

- shelter from water, heat, salt, pests and fire;
- separation of food, fuel, chemicals, seed and medicine;
- access order and retrieval time;
- support load and stack stability;
- labels, provenance and expiry;
- ownership, locks and tamper evidence;
- inventory accuracy;
- emergency accessibility.

The player manages zones and rules, not individual spoon placement. A good
storehouse compresses chores while creating inspection and record obligations.

### 7.5 Expedition logistics

Every expedition balances:

> **Goal + route + time window + body + load out + return capacity + reserve +
> cache/abort plan**

The return load matters. Reaching an engine with a full pack does not recover
the engine. A diving mission may need the shore team, float, winch and
preservation bath prepared before the first descent.

## 8. Entropy, maintenance and decommissioning

The existing documents recognize wear, but a unified lifecycle is still
required to prevent both invincible bases and repair-chore fatigue.

### 8.1 The infrastructure lifecycle

> **Design → build → commission → operate → inspect → maintain → repair →
> modify → mothball/decommission → recover/dispose**

Every system has:

- operating envelope;
- known wear surfaces;
- hidden failure possibilities;
- inspection cues;
- maintenance actions;
- replaceable and irreplaceable parts;
- safe shutdown state;
- failure consequences;
- records and responsible owner.

### 8.2 Condition is specific

Reject a universal “63% durability.”

A dock may have:

- sound piles;
- one loose cross-brace;
- corroded fasteners at splash height;
- marine growth on the ladder;
- a chafed mooring line;
- an overloaded deck area;
- an outdated inspection after a storm.

The player can use it while deciding what risk remains.

### 8.3 Maintenance debt

Deferred work creates a ledger of changing uncertainty:

- inspection becomes stale;
- known small defects propagate;
- spares are consumed elsewhere;
- specialists die or leave;
- emergency repair becomes harder;
- records diverge from current configuration.

Debt does not deal invisible damage because a timer expired. It changes cues,
confidence and failure probability through actual mechanisms.

### 8.4 Reliability is designed

Players can build:

- redundancy;
- graceful failure;
- bypasses;
- isolation valves;
- fuses and breakers;
- overflow paths;
- inspection access;
- standard parts;
- jigs and gauges;
- spares;
- test points;
- clear shutdown procedures.

Reasoning + maintenance history + fabrication therefore creates safer power,
not merely a faster repair animation.

### 8.5 Decommissioning matters

Abandoned machines leak, batteries corrode, pressure vessels remain hazardous
and structures shed material. A mature survivor may gain more by safely
draining, isolating, dismantling and documenting a system than by keeping every
salvaged machine alive.

Waste can be:

- reused;
- repaired;
- remanufactured;
- recycled;
- composted;
- contained;
- buried in a deliberately chosen site;
- discharged and allowed to create consequences.

There is no magical deletion bin.

## 9. Physical knowledge, mapping and succession

The persistent-island death cycle succeeds only if knowledge preservation is
itself playable.

### 9.1 Five knowledge ledgers

| Ledger | Owner | Can survive death? | Example |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Player recognition | person at the screen | yes, outside fiction | remembers a route from a prior life |
| Survivor memory | current human | no unless externalized | recalls hearing a pump knock |
| Embodied technique | current body | no | can tie a loaded wet knot reliably |
| Physical record | world object | yes if preserved | tide table, diagram, labeled sample |
| Social memory | witnesses/institutions | yes, but not automatically transferable | Mara remembers the dead mechanic's promise |

The interface may never confuse these sources.

### 9.2 The field record loop

> **Observe → locate → describe → sample/measure → interpret → mark confidence
> → preserve → compare → revise**

Records can include:

- hand-drawn maps;
- route markers and survey points;
- tide, current and weather logs;
- crop, seed and harvest records;
- patient and treatment notes;
- material samples and test coupons;
- machine configurations and maintenance history;
- wreck plans and hazard markings;
- photographs, audio and recovered digital files;
- witness statements;
- anomaly observations and competing explanations;
- death reports and warnings.

### 9.3 Maps are authored evidence

A map begins blank except for what is directly perceived. It records:

- landmarks;
- route traces;
- elevation and slope estimates;
- water and shelter;
- hazards and uncertain areas;
- tide-dependent access;
- depth soundings and underwater features;
- ownership and social boundaries;
- date, author and revision.

Maps can be incomplete, outdated, damaged, copied inaccurately or deliberately
falsified. Instruments improve precision but do not auto-reveal unvisited land.

The player may place a temporary personal marker for usability, but the current
survivor cannot navigate toward knowledge that exists only in the player's
memory without normal uncertainty.

### 9.4 Successor access protocol

A survivor who wants the next castaway to benefit can create a **Continuity
Cache**:

- protected water and basic clothing;
- simple tools;
- first-aid and fire means;
- a current island overview;
- keys, combinations or physical bypass instructions;
- hazard and maintenance status;
- archive index;
- emergency shutdown instructions;
- a short statement of unfinished work.

The Cache is not indestructible. It can be flooded, burned, looted on Community
Island, corrupted or made obsolete. Redundant caches and signs improve
survival.

If no access protocol exists, a new survivor may find:

- a locked store with food inside;
- a powered system whose controls are unknown;
- a boat but no key or fuel map;
- an archive in a language they cannot read;
- a dangerous machine still energized;
- a predecessor's body carrying the only obvious key.

This is consequence, not punishment. Alternative entry, dismantling,
translation, patient inspection and fresh construction remain possible.

### 9.5 Plans retain provenance

Every serious plan can record:

- author;
- source evidence;
- material assumptions;
- revisions;
- tests passed and failed;
- operating limits;
- known defects;
- witnesses or inspector;
- date relative to environmental change.

An old tested boat plan does not prove that today's rotten timber, altered load
and replacement fasteners will pass.

## 10. The castaway arrival and identity constitution

v0.9 correctly rules that a dead survivor never returns, but the mechanism of a
new arrival was undefined.

### 10.1 Arrival ruling

After terminal death, the player's opportunity continues only when a new adult
castaway is displaced onto the private Spawn Island.

This event is called a **Castaway Translation**.

- It is separate from ordinary wreckfall.
- It carries one living person and only minimal objects plausibly on that body.
- It normally deposits the person in surf, on shore, in a cave mouth or in
  another survivable but urgent arrival state.
- It never places the new person inside the dead survivor's locked shelter.
- It does not create a second living person on Spawn Island.
- It leaves a measurable anomaly trace that later research may compare.
- It cannot be farmed for cargo, traits or wreck generation.

The fiction remains honest:

> Empty vessels and aircraft can be displaced as wreckfall. Living castaways
> are translated separately. Spawn Island never becomes a populated crash site.

### 10.2 Why only one living person?

The private island is a **single-human stability pocket** within the Triangle.
When its current occupant dies or successfully leaves, the pocket can capture
another living displacement. Attempts by another living person to enter from
Community Island lose alignment and return them to ordinary sea space.

This rule:

- explains the private instance;
- prevents raids and spawn killing;
- preserves physical crossings for the owner survivor;
- makes the island solitary in fiction, not only in server architecture;
- provides a measurable anomaly boundary rather than an invisible wall.

Cargo passes only when physically accompanied and retained by the aligned
survivor. No remote warehouse link exists.

### 10.3 Identity matrix

Each castaway has a coherent life profile:

- name and adult age band;
- body size, composition and handedness;
- pre-island occupation and lived tasks;
- languages and literacy;
- relevant principle knowledge;
- practiced techniques;
- current health, old injuries and medical needs;
- sensory profile;
- comfort/fear history without hard-coded phobia caricatures;
- values, unfinished personal attachment and home context;
- initial clothing and carried pocket objects;
- uncertain or fragmented arrival memory.

There is no “Intelligence 14.” A marine engineer may reason well about pumps and
know little about edible plants. A gardener may diagnose soil by sight but have
unpracticed hands with a radio.

### 10.4 Fair diversity

Backgrounds change routes, not worth.

Every castaway must have:

- at least one credible strength;
- at least one unfamiliar domain;
- a viable first-night route;
- the ability to learn;
- no concealed condition that can cause unavoidable early death;
- readable body and knowledge evidence.

Potential differences include:

- strong but poor endurance;
- enduring but small;
- excellent reasoning with tremor or low grip;
- good swimmer unfamiliar with local reef behavior;
- mechanic with no medical confidence;
- nurse with poor navigation;
- multilingual negotiator with weak fabrication;
- older survivor with judgment and chronic limitations;
- young adult with fast adaptation and little procedural knowledge.

### 10.5 Anti-reroll protection

Intentional death does not improve arrivals.

- Translation quality is not a pity timer.
- Rare backgrounds are not loot tiers.
- repeated destructive death narrows safe arrival conditions and consumes world
  time;
- the island retains damage and depletion;
- no preview-and-reject screen exists.

The player learns who arrived by orienting, inspecting the body and recalling
fragments—not by shopping a character-build menu.

### 10.6 Attachment and mortality

Each life develops a **Human Thread**:

- what this person feared losing;
- what they learned;
- whom they trusted;
- what they built or damaged;
- which promises remain;
- what home meant to them;
- how they died or left;
- what physical evidence they left behind.

This is not a morality score. It is the narrative spine that makes permanent
character death matter.

## 11. Health ecology, impairment and rehabilitation

The current canon contains injury, disease and fatal chains, but it needs a
complete post-crisis life. Surviving trauma should create adaptation, not only
a longer debuff.

### 11.1 Health is a changing capacity network

Track clinically meaningful state through:

- airway, breathing and circulation;
- hydration, nutrition and temperature;
- tissue injury and bleeding;
- infection and contamination;
- pain and inflammation;
- mobility, balance, grip and fine control;
- vision, hearing and sensation;
- sleep and fatigue;
- medication and treatment history;
- chronic condition;
- cognition, composure and decision quality;
- recovery load and reinjury risk.

The player sees symptoms, functional effects and learned interpretation—not a
diagnostic encyclopedia at first glance.

### 11.2 Long-horizon conditions

The full game may include:

- wound infection and sepsis;
- water- and food-borne illness;
- fungal skin and foot disease;
- parasites and vector-borne disease;
- venom and allergic response;
- smoke, fuel, solvent and heavy-metal exposure;
- decompression and pressure injury;
- dental pain and infection;
- malnutrition and deficiency;
- repetitive strain;
- heat and cold injury;
- hearing or vision damage;
- fractures, joint instability and nerve damage;
- chronic respiratory or metabolic needs brought by a castaway;
- grief, isolation, traumatic stress and sleep disruption.

No disease exists merely to drain a bar. Exposure, incubation, symptoms,
uncertainty, transmission where appropriate, treatment, recovery and prevention
form one chain.

### 11.3 Rehabilitation is a capability braid

> **Stabilize → protect → restore range → restore control → rebuild load →
> adapt method → return to proof**

Rehabilitation crosses medicine, body awareness, tool design, nutrition,
rest, Composure and patient recordkeeping.

A damaged shoulder may close solo reboarding while leaving radio work possible.
A missing finger may change grip and knot technique. Hearing loss may increase
dependence on visual alarms. A cane, brace, adapted handle, hoist, jig or partner
can reopen work honestly.

### 11.4 Assistive design, not stat cancellation

Assistive equipment can:

- redistribute force;
- stabilize a joint;
- enlarge a grip;
- convert a two-hand operation;
- provide visual or vibration warning;
- reduce lifting through leverage;
- support mobility;
- make a workstation seated or one-handed.

It does not erase pain, fit, maintenance, energy or training. The capability
hypergraph makes disability a changed route, not an automatic end to the life.

### 11.5 Mental life without “sanity damage”

Isolation, grief, uncertainty and repeated exposure affect:

- sleep;
- attention;
- risk appraisal;
- motivation;
- irritability and communication;
- intrusive recall;
- confidence in ambiguous perception.

Protective factors include routine, purposeful work, daylight, rest, writing,
radio contact, companionship on Community Island, memorial, competence and
credible hope.

Reject hallucination as a generic low-sanity punishment. A Triangle perception
conflict must remain distinguishable through evidence, redundant observation
and later comparison.

## 12. The living fauna web

The food ecology describes populations but not yet a complete animal world.

### 12.1 Fauna are agents in ecology

Animals have:

- habitat;
- feeding and nesting needs;
- daily and seasonal activity;
- perception and avoidance;
- breeding and recruitment;
- migration or territory;
- disease and contamination relationships;
- response to noise, light, fire, roads and harvest;
- ecological role;
- physical traces.

No animal exists only at a spawn point waiting to become meat.

### 12.2 Island fauna families

Appropriate families may include:

- seabirds and land birds;
- reef, lagoon and pelagic fish;
- crabs, lobsters, shellfish and other invertebrates;
- insects and pollinators;
- rats or other invasive stowaways;
- reptiles and amphibious species suited to the setting;
- marine mammals or turtles as rare ecological presences;
- scavengers attracted by waste or carcasses.

Large land predators should not be inserted merely to create combat. The most
dangerous fauna may be venomous organisms, aggressive nesting animals, sharks
in specific contexts, diseased rodents, stinging marine life or a frightened
animal in confined terrain.

### 12.3 Wreckfall can alter ecology

An empty wreck may bring:

- rats, insects, eggs or larvae;
- seeds and fungi;
- fouling organisms;
- contaminated ballast or standing water;
- trapped domestic animals only when a deliberate exceptional story justifies
  it and does not violate the no-human rule.

Introductions begin as local, detectable events. They can be contained,
exploited, studied or allowed to spread.

### 12.4 Tracking and stewardship

Tracking crosses Perception, ecology, weather, time and movement:

- prints and trails;
- feeding signs;
- nests and burrows;
- calls and silence;
- droppings and remains;
- water disturbance;
- seasonal absence;
- camera and acoustic records.

Successful stewardship may preserve pollination, fish recruitment, pest
control, clean water and reliable harvest. Overharvest changes future behavior
and population rather than merely lengthening a respawn timer.

## 13. The fourth terrain: vertical and subterranean island

v0.10 correctly establishes land, surface water and underwater work. The audit
found a major omitted environment connecting all three: the island's height and
interior.

### 13.1 Vertical spaces

- tree canopy;
- cliffs and ravines;
- wrecks lodged above ground;
- masts, towers and radio structures;
- roofs, cranes and scaffolds;
- sea caves and blowholes;
- sinkhole rims;
- steep high-ground evacuation routes.

Vertical work introduces:

- route finding;
- three-point movement;
- fall consequence;
- anchor selection;
- rope access;
- load hauling;
- rescue;
- exposure to wind, lightning and falling objects;
- controlled descent and ascent;
- communication when people cannot see each other.

Strength alone is not climbing. Mobility + grip + Composure + route perception
+ anchor/rope knowledge + body condition creates credible access.

### 13.2 Subterranean spaces

On a Bermuda-influenced carbonate island, caves and sinkholes are not fantasy
mines. They can form karst and anchialine systems connecting rainwater,
groundwater and seawater.

Possible environments:

- dry solution cave;
- unstable collapse chamber;
- fresh/brackish lens access;
- tidal cave;
- anchialine pool;
- flooded sump connected to v0.10 diving;
- buried wreck or cargo displacement;
- underground estuary;
- anomaly cavity with contradictory instruments.

### 13.3 Cave operations

> **Locate → test air and stability → establish entry control → map → light →
> protect route → survey water/geology/biology → recover or withdraw → preserve
> record**

Hazards include:

- darkness and failed light;
- bad air;
- flooding and tide;
- collapse and rockfall;
- narrow restriction;
- disorientation;
- contaminated water;
- fragile ecosystems;
- vertical fall;
- inability to turn or carry a casualty;
- silt and zero visibility in flooded sections.

Solo Spawn Island operations must allow non-entry alternatives: line probes,
air tests, cameras, mirrors, poles, water sampling, surface mapping and later
Community-supported teams.

### 13.4 Why the fourth terrain matters

It connects:

- rain and aquifer behavior;
- drought and water security;
- sinkholes and landslides;
- rope, climbing and rescue;
- geology and finite material;
- cave-adapted ecology;
- diving and underwater navigation;
- shelter and temperature;
- hidden salvage;
- Triangle measurement.

The cave is not a chest room. It is a relationship between the island's surface
and sea.

## 14. Sensory ecology and expert perception

The existing documents promise readable cues. v0.11 specifies how the world
communicates them.

### 14.1 Sensory channels

| Channel | Survival information |
|---|---|
| Sight | color, movement, deformation, smoke, cloud, water, wound, corrosion |
| Sound | surf, wind, crack, knock, hiss, electrical arc, animal call, voice |
| Touch/proprioception | heat, vibration, slip, resistance, balance, grip, pulse |
| Smell | smoke, fuel, rot, contamination, spoiled food, mold, overheated wiring |
| Taste | only cautious known uses; salinity and obvious spoilage, never a free poison test |
| Instrument | pressure, voltage, bearing, depth, time, chemical indication |
| Social observation | posture, speech, hesitation, conflicting testimony |

Mobile haptics, captions and visual equivalents support signals without turning
all danger into icons.

### 14.2 Expertise changes interpretation

The raw cue may be available to anyone:

> A rhythmic knock comes from the pump.

Different evidence produces different interpretation:

- unfamiliar: “The pump sounds wrong.”
- practiced operator: “Flow is pulsing.”
- mechanic: “The inlet may be drawing air.”
- system expert with records: “This began after the low cistern fell below the
  intake; cavitation is likely. Shut down and inspect before the impeller is
  damaged.”

Expertise reveals earlier, more specific and more actionable meaning. It does
not create supernatural outlines.

### 14.3 Darkness must be designed, not black-screen difficulty

Moon, adaptation, fire, lamps, reflective markers and known routes matter.
Darkness reduces identification and distance, changes animal behavior and makes
light visible to other people. Accessibility can raise minimum legibility while
preserving the difference between illuminated and unobserved space.

### 14.4 Audio is a gameplay system

Sound propagates through:

- distance;
- wind and surf masking;
- walls, caves and water;
- machinery;
- hearing condition;
- attention and fatigue.

Noise can warn, attract, conceal, communicate and damage hearing. Sirens,
bells, horns, drums, radio and shouted relays become low-technology public
infrastructure.

## 15. Community Island as a living society

v0.9 establishes Community Island's purpose but does not yet define how its
people live. This is the largest Act II omission.

### 15.1 Population sources

Community Island contains:

- active player survivors;
- absent player survivors under offline protection;
- authored adult inhabitants;
- new human translations from many periods, cultures and places;
- people who arrived by credible rescue, crossing or anomaly route;
- people who die, depart, disappear or become unable to work.

The initial release should not simulate pregnancy or childhood. Community
population continuity comes from arrivals, deaths, departures and migration.
This keeps the adult survival focus and avoids treating family life as a
production mechanic.

### 15.2 Every authored person has an operating model

An inhabitant is defined by:

- identity and origin;
- body and health;
- languages;
- knowledge and techniques;
- current needs;
- responsibilities;
- possessions and access;
- relationships and trust;
- values and boundaries;
- active projects;
- witnessed events;
- promises, debts and grievances;
- risk tolerance;
- place and daily intent;
- ability to perceive, communicate and act.

They do not exist to stand beside a shop interface.

### 15.3 Daily agency

Authored inhabitants can:

- eat, sleep, recover and seek treatment;
- work on real supplied tasks;
- inspect and maintain assigned systems;
- teach and learn;
- move goods;
- negotiate and trade;
- respond to alarms;
- care for another person;
- investigate a concern;
- abandon unsafe work;
- hide, lie, steal, sabotage, warn or confess;
- form, revise or leave a coalition;
- pursue an escape or settlement goal without the player.

Offscreen simulation uses intentions and ledgers, not full animation, but must
respect the same resources and constraints.

### 15.4 Need, competence and consent

No inhabitant becomes an obedient worker because a settlement UI assigned them.
A task requires:

> **Awareness + ability + access + time + condition + consent/obligation**

Coercion is possible as a social harm, not as an efficient management mode. It
creates resistance, concealment, flight, evidence and political consequence.

### 15.5 Institutions emerge from repeated coordination

Institutions are persistent agreements plus people, places, tools and records.

| Institution | Physical basis | Human basis | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Clinic | clean space, water, supplies, records | trained care and consent | infection, shortage, secrecy, burnout |
| Harbor service | dock, signals, boats, weather data | watch, rescue crew, authority | missed alarm, corrupt access, failed craft |
| Fire service | water, tools, alarms, routes | drills, command, responders | empty cistern, unsafe heroics, no accountability |
| Seed trust | dry storage, samples, plots | stewards, records, distribution rules | contamination, monopoly, lost diversity |
| Workshop guild | machines, standards, test rigs | teaching, inspection, responsibility | gatekeeping, bad norms, undocumented modification |
| Weather station | instruments, archive, radio | observers, forecast method | stale calibration, rumor overriding evidence |
| Council/court | meeting place, records, custody | legitimacy, procedure, witnesses | faction capture, retaliation, no enforcement |
| Departure authority | test sites, manifests, stores | inspectors, crew consent, go/no-go power | sabotage, favoritism, schedule pressure |

An institution can outlive its founder only if roles and records are
distributed.

### 15.6 Coalitions, not permanent colored factions

People can align over:

- water access;
- land and shoreline use;
- salvage claims;
- food distribution;
- outsider quarantine;
- research ethics;
- public works;
- defense;
- rescue obligations;
- who may depart and with what;
- whether Triangle experiments are tolerable.

The same two people may cooperate on fire protection and oppose each other on
anomaly research. This creates politics from issues rather than immutable
teams.

### 15.7 Memory and identity after player death

Witnesses remember the dead survivor. The next castaway is a stranger.

The new person may present:

- a recovered letter;
- a physical key;
- a contract naming a bearer;
- evidence of shared island origin;
- knowledge only the dead recorded;
- a claim to recover abandoned property.

None automatically transfers trust or guilt. Some people may welcome anyone
from the same Spawn Island; others may suspect manipulation. Social continuity
must be negotiated through evidence.

### 15.8 Community Island must function without ideal population

If few human players are online:

- authored inhabitants sustain basic systems within actual limits;
- critical institutions can shrink, fail or request help;
- public work advances slowly rather than freezing;
- no simulated worker creates missing resources;
- new arrivals prevent permanent demographic extinction under normal world
  settings;
- the island remains socially legible rather than appearing empty.

## 16. Trade, economy and public goods

Trade is currently named but not mechanically grounded.

### 16.1 Value is contextual

An object's value depends on:

- present scarcity;
- condition and provenance;
- location and transport cost;
- compatibility;
- perishability;
- legal ownership;
- known demand;
- confidence in claims;
- substitutes;
- urgency;
- future maintenance.

There is no universal vendor price.

### 16.2 Exchange forms

- direct barter;
- labor-for-access;
- repair or teaching service;
- ration entitlement;
- salvage share;
- loan with collateral;
- deposit and return;
- written credit;
- institution-issued token only after credibility exists;
- gift, rescue obligation or mutual aid.

Currency may emerge on Community Island, but it cannot create food, erase debt
or open a private Spawn Island.

### 16.3 Contracts are world objects

A contract records:

- parties;
- identity method;
- promised action or object;
- condition and deadline;
- witnesses;
- collateral or custody;
- amendment;
- completion evidence;
- breach and remedy.

Illiteracy, language difference, coercion and damaged records create real
problems. Spoken agreements can still be valid through witnesses and repeated
custom.

### 16.4 Public goods create political play

Harbors, roads, firebreaks, clinics, water systems, alarms, weather stations and
rescue boats help people who did not build them. Funding and maintaining them
creates disputes over labor, access, priority and free riding.

The player can contribute without becoming ruler. Leadership is a practiced
coordination capability, not mind control.

## 17. Conflict, security and justice under one life

The game permits PvP and sabotage. Without a full contract, this would be one of
the most dangerous missing systems.

### 17.1 Conflict ladder

> **Tension → signal → boundary → negotiation → deterrence → evasion/restraint
> → force → casualty → aftermath**

Most conflict should resolve before lethal force. Weapons do not exist only to
increase damage; they affect distance, confidence, escalation and evidence.

### 17.2 Violence model

Violence obeys the same body simulation as accidents:

- reach and line of action;
- surprise and awareness;
- footing and terrain;
- stamina and grip;
- weapon condition;
- protection and coverage;
- tissue and bleeding;
- pain and shock;
- retreat and surrender;
- first aid and rescue;
- witnesses and evidence.

No health sponge, execution bonus, critical-hit lottery or high-skill immunity
is permitted.

### 17.3 Security is a capability network

Security can come from:

- visibility and lighting;
- locks and access design;
- custody and inventory records;
- seals and tamper evidence;
- safe storage separation;
- watch routines;
- alarms;
- community presence;
- route control;
- redundancy;
- authentication;
- conflict mediation;
- defensible retreat;
- protective equipment;
- trained response.

The best security solution is often preventing a desperate shortage or making
tampering detectable, not building a stronger wall.

### 17.4 Non-lethal resolution

The game supports:

- withdrawal;
- surrender;
- disarmament;
- separation;
- restraint when credible and supervised;
- negotiated restitution;
- exclusion from a site;
- custody under community rules;
- medical care regardless of side;
- appeal, mediation or public hearing.

Surrender is non-graphic and never an invitation to procedural abuse. Captivity
cannot become an indefinite offline trap. An absent player remains protected by
clear custody-resolution rules.

### 17.5 Evidence and justice

Physical and social evidence may include:

- access logs;
- missing stock;
- altered fasteners;
- footprints and route sightings;
- tool marks;
- contaminated samples;
- radio recordings;
- witness statements;
- ownership marks;
- motive and opportunity;
- conflicting maintenance history.

Evidence has uncertainty. A court, council, leader or ad hoc group may interpret
it well or badly. Justice is a social process whose legitimacy affects future
cooperation.

### 17.6 Spawn Island remains non-raidable

No other player or authored inhabitant can enter the private Spawn Island,
attack its castaway, loot its base or wait at its crossing boundary. Risk begins
when the aligned survivor physically exports themselves or cargo into shared
space.

## 18. Culture, attachment and the reason to keep living

Once water, food and shelter are stable, survival games often collapse into
production optimization. THE FIRST NIGHT needs human meaning.

### 18.1 Human life is more than readiness

Characters can create and value:

- names for places;
- personal spaces;
- communal meals;
- stories and jokes;
- music and rhythm;
- games and contests;
- art and decoration;
- memorials;
- rituals of arrival, departure and mourning;
- correspondence;
- friendship, mentorship and chosen family;
- privacy;
- promises about home;
- traditions tied to storms, harvests or rescue.

These do not provide “+10 morale.” They change relationships, routine,
attention, grief, willingness to cooperate and what a person considers worth
risking.

### 18.2 Solitary meaning

Spawn Island remains solitary, but not emotionally blank. A castaway may:

- speak into a recorder;
- mark days or tides;
- maintain a memorial;
- cook a meaningful meal;
- preserve an object from home;
- name projects and places;
- hear distant Community radio;
- prepare something for the unknown next arrival.

Such acts externalize identity and make later discovery powerful.

### 18.3 Community culture is plural

People arrive from different eras, languages and histories. No single culture
is the default. Translation, adaptation, borrowing, disagreement and hybrid
practice emerge through actual people.

The game does not convert religion, nationality or trauma into class bonuses.
It treats them as parts of identity expressed through choices and
relationships.

### 18.4 Memorial and death

A body is not generic loot. Communities may identify, recover, bury, cremate,
preserve evidence, build a memorial or leave the dead inaccessible because
recovery is too dangerous.

The Human Thread records what happened without glorifying self-destruction.
Self-harm remains non-procedural, unrewarded and non-instructional.

## 19. The Triangle investigation engine

The current canon has anomaly events and endings but not a complete method for
knowing what is true.

### 19.1 Investigation loop

> **Notice contradiction → preserve raw evidence → authenticate source →
> propose alternatives → predict a difference → test safely → compare →
> revise → reproduce → operationalize**

Progress is not “collect ten anomaly fragments.” It is improved ability to make
predictions that survive contact with the island.

### 19.2 Evidence classes

- direct observation;
- instrument reading;
- sample;
- wreck provenance;
- timestamp or clock disagreement;
- radio/signal record;
- route and drift track;
- material behavior;
- witness testimony;
- historical document;
- repeatable probe outcome;
- negative result;
- failure trace.

Each record retains source, chain of custody, calibration, environment,
observer and uncertainty.

### 19.3 Hypotheses remain plural

Example explanations for a repeating radio signal:

- ordinary reflection or equipment fault;
- delayed transmission;
- deliberate deception;
- displaced signal from another time;
- local anomaly repeating recorded energy;
- misidentified pattern.

Each predicts different behavior under movement, frequency change, time,
shielding, independent receiver or controlled transmission.

The interface shows:

- supported;
- contradicted;
- untested;
- dependent on questionable evidence;
- locally predictive.

It never announces an omniscient “correct theory” before the world proves it.

### 19.4 Safe experimental architecture

Advanced investigation uses:

- remote floats;
- timed cameras and recorders;
- redundant clocks;
- independent instruments;
- shielded or isolated samples;
- control sites;
- uncrewed boats and ROVs;
- abort thresholds;
- observers outside the affected zone;
- data duplication.

This makes Reasoning cross with fabrication, radio, navigation, weather,
diving, medicine, logistics and social trust.

### 19.5 The anomaly's constitutional boundary

The Triangle may affect:

- position;
- signal path;
- clock agreement;
- bounded weather behavior;
- material state;
- memory/evidence correspondence;
- arrival of wrecks and castaways;
- access to a return corridor.

It may not:

- grant arbitrary crafting powers;
- resurrect a dead person;
- create infinite energy without consequence;
- delete protected players;
- rewrite a failed random roll as lore;
- excuse a missing warning;
- replace a physical escape program with a cut-scene key.

## 20. Island generation and geography grammar

Repeated castaway lives need replayable geography, but pure procedural
randomness would undermine causal design.

### 20.1 Authored grammar, variable realization

Each private Spawn Island is generated from authored relationships:

- a survivable arrival envelope;
- coast, reef and at least one landing problem;
- freshwater possibilities with different evidence and work;
- high ground;
- shelter opportunities;
- renewable food populations;
- finite geology;
- timber/fiber ecology;
- at least two meaningful route barriers;
- wreckfall approach zones;
- shallow and deeper underwater sites;
- vertical or subterranean opportunities;
- multiple long-term base locations with tradeoffs;
- no single mandatory unique item.

The shape and relationship vary; the survival logic does not.

### 20.2 No soft-lock constitution

Every island must support:

- immediate air and movement;
- at least two first-night survival postures;
- renewable calories and water through learnable routes;
- natural low-tech fire/shelter possibilities;
- a pathway to cordage, cutting and containers through substitutes;
- at least one credible Community crossing program;
- at least one direct return-home family over a long enough history;
- recovery from loss of any one wreck;
- escape from local depletion through stewardship, substitution or transport.

Advanced endings may depend on rare arrivals. Continued survival may not.

### 20.3 Wreckfall does not reroll the map

The island seed persists. Wreckfalls alter it:

- cut vegetation;
- create fire or contamination;
- expose or bury terrain;
- add structures and debris;
- change shore and reef access;
- introduce organisms;
- create new salvage and research sites.

The player lives with history instead of restarting until a favorable world
appears.

### 20.4 Community Island is authored at higher social density

Its geography supports:

- settlements with different risk and access;
- public and contested land;
- farms, docks, workshops, clinics and ruins;
- transport chokepoints and alternative routes;
- disaster assembly areas;
- freshwater and waste relationships;
- expansion pressure;
- visible historical layers;
- wider ocean access.

It must remain navigable without floating markers. Landmarks, signs, maps,
language and local directions do the work.

## 21. The Wreckfall Director

Repeated empty planes and boats are central to Spawn Island identity. Their
arrival must be authored by rules, not a loot timer.

### 21.1 A wreckfall record

Every event has:

- source era and vessel/aircraft class;
- last plausible operational state;
- anomaly displacement signature;
- approach or materialization path;
- impact environment;
- breakup and debris field;
- fire, fuel, battery, pressure and contamination state;
- organisms or cargo introduced;
- recoverable capabilities;
- knowledge and provenance;
- deterioration timeline;
- interactions with current weather, tide and structures;
- links to anomaly hypotheses and endings.

### 21.2 Event cadence

Wreckfall probability may respond to:

- observed anomaly cycles;
- storm and current conditions;
- high-energy experiments;
- unresolved debris fields;
- world era and narrative pacing.

It does not respond to:

- the player being poor;
- recent death;
- missing a desired recipe;
- opening a monetized container;
- a fixed daily-login reward.

### 21.3 Warning and impact fairness

Possible precursors:

- radio burst;
- compass/clock disagreement;
- distant sound;
- unusual cloud or light;
- animals reacting;
- debris before main body;
- instrument trace;
- surf or pressure change.

A mature player may:

- move people and stores;
- isolate fuel areas;
- prepare fire response;
- mark probable impact;
- launch observation equipment;
- choose not to approach.

The event can damage an exposed area, but it cannot select an occupied safe
shelter for unavoidable deletion.

### 21.4 Wreckfall creates work, not a prize

After impact:

> **Account → contain → extinguish/isolate → observe → map → prioritize →
> stabilize → recover → preserve → integrate → monitor aftermath**

A magnificent engine may be unusable for months but reveal metallurgy,
fasteners, fuel systems or a radio component today.

### 21.5 Community human arrivals

Community Island may receive living arrivals because it is the social stability
zone. They can appear through:

- group translation;
- survivor-bearing wreck or lifeboat;
- arrival from another island;
- rescued wider-ocean castaway;
- historical or future displacement.

Every arrival creates medical, quarantine, language, housing, legal and
political questions. People are never reward dispensers.

## 22. Persistent-world and offline constitution

### 22.1 Five authoritative world partitions

1. **Account island ledger** — terrain, ecology, structures, archives, stores
   and local history.
2. **Current survivor ledger** — body, knowledge, inventory, memory, identity
   and location.
3. **Community ledger** — population, property, institutions, projects,
   relationships and public events.
4. **Wider ocean ledger** — craft, routes, weather, wrecks, rescue and endings.
5. **Causal integrity ledger** — verified transactions, death commits,
   migrations and technical incidents.

No client-side inventory shortcut may cross these partitions without a verified
physical transfer.

### 22.2 Offline character protection

When a player leaves safely:

- the survivor enters a declared protected rest, shelter, berth, clinic or
  custody state;
- their body does not perform risky work;
- ordinary needs are resolved only through supplied safe rules;
- other people cannot injure, move, rob or imprison them through direct avatar
  interaction.

Property left outside protected possession follows world rules. A boat at a
public dock is not invulnerable because its owner logged out.

Abrupt disconnect during danger:

- freezes new player intent;
- attempts a brief safe authority hold;
- resolves already-committed physical events;
- never invents continued walking, swimming or fighting;
- commits death only when the verified pre-disconnect state made the outcome
  unavoidable independent of missing input;
- otherwise places the case in technical review/recovery.

### 22.3 Cross-island transfer transaction

A crossing commits:

1. survivor and craft identity;
2. exact cargo;
3. departure condition;
4. origin removal;
5. route exposure;
6. destination arrival or causal failure;
7. destination insertion.

Duplication, lost cargo and two-location survivors are invalid states.

### 22.4 Death commit protocol

Terminal death requires:

- authoritative body state;
- causal event chain;
- confirmed world/location authority;
- no unresolved disconnect or duplicated session;
- transaction flush for inventory and body;
- immutable survivor-death record;
- release of the Spawn Island's single-living-person slot.

The next Castaway Translation cannot occur before the prior death is committed.

### 22.5 Save protection and migrations

Because death and depletion are irreversible, persistence must use:

- append-before-aggregate event records for material actions;
- transaction identifiers;
- checksums;
- periodic verified snapshots;
- versioned schema migrations;
- dry-run migration validation;
- duplicate and impossible-state detectors;
- server backups unavailable as player save-scumming tools;
- incident audit and restoration to the last verified causal boundary.

Restoration after technical corruption recreates what had already truthfully
happened. It does not undo a legitimate death.

## 23. Truth at distance: the shippable simulation

The design cannot render or tick every crop, fish, fastener, inhabitant and
chemical process continuously on a phone. The answer is not to abandon truth;
it is to allocate detail by consequence.

### 23.1 Four simulation resolutions

| Resolution | Used for | Model |
|---|---|---|
| R0 — Exact | hands, body, current interaction, lethal hazards | continuous or high-frequency authoritative state |
| R1 — Local | nearby structures, people, animals, fire, water | event and interval state with visible embodiment |
| R2 — Operational | offscreen projects, institutions, crops, stores | bounded ledger using supplies, capacity and risk |
| R3 — Historical | distant ecology, long decay, population tendency | aggregated state with conserved totals and scheduled events |

Promotion from R3/R2 to R1/R0 reconstructs a state consistent with the ledger.
It does not reroll hidden qualities for advantage.

### 23.2 Conservation requirements

Across resolution changes:

- matter is not created;
- unique items remain unique;
- people do not teleport;
- wounds do not vanish;
- fuel and food consumption remain accounted;
- completed work required real inputs and time;
- hazards retain origin and consequence;
- ownership and evidence persist;
- random latent state is seeded before observation.

### 23.3 NPC offscreen resolution

An offscreen inhabitant resolves:

> **Intent + route + time + condition + access + supplies + competence +
> interruption risk**

The result can be:

- completed;
- partially completed;
- safely aborted;
- delayed;
- failed with recoverable consequence;
- escalated to an incident requiring foreground play.

No offscreen simulation resolves an unwitnessed authored person into arbitrary
death unless the previously known situation and world event made death a
credible causal outcome and normal rescue opportunity existed in the world.

### 23.4 Mobile/web budgets are design constraints

The project needs explicit budgets for:

- active physics bodies;
- AI agents at R0/R1;
- navigation updates;
- dynamic lights and shadows;
- water and vegetation fidelity;
- audio voices;
- network state;
- save size and migration time;
- initial download and scene load;
- sustained CPU/GPU and battery use;
- thermal headroom;
- memory;
- touch targets and modal transitions.

Godot web export can operate single-threaded for broader compatibility, so no
core gameplay truth may depend on unrestricted background threads. Android can
thermally throttle sustained load; adaptive detail must reduce presentation and
simulation frequency safely before input latency becomes dangerous.

### 23.5 Performance degradation order

When a device is constrained, reduce:

1. distant visual density;
2. shadow distance and update rate;
3. reflection and water detail;
4. non-critical particles;
5. distant animation;
6. R2/R3 processing frequency;
7. cosmetic audio layering.

Never reduce:

- input reliability;
- nearby lethal-hazard authority;
- collision truth;
- interaction ownership;
- death validation;
- urgent warning presentation.

## 24. Comprehension, onboarding and accessibility

The physical Director playtest proved that a system does not exist if a player
cannot reliably operate it.

### 24.1 Three kinds of ignorance

The game distinguishes:

1. **Control ignorance** — the human player does not know how to use the game.
2. **World ignorance** — the player has not recognized a causal relationship.
3. **Survivor ignorance** — the current character lacks evidence or competence.

Control ignorance may never be lethal. World ignorance may be dangerous only
after cues and learning opportunities. Survivor ignorance creates uncertainty
and alternative cautious actions.

### 24.2 Diegetic learning ladder

> **See consequence safely → receive plain cue → attempt bounded action →
> inspect result → repeat with variation → face real commitment**

Examples:

- shallow moving water teaches drift before an open crossing;
- a loose non-critical joint teaches inspection before a roof failure;
- a dry training knot teaches tension before a live hoist;
- a tide marker teaches change before a cave sump closes;
- a failed seed tray teaches moisture and labeling before the entire seed
  archive is risked.

### 24.3 Interface surfaces added by v0.11

The existing seven surfaces gain four focused views:

8. **Open Loops** — one Now, Soon and Long focus, with evidence and options.
9. **Map and survey** — authored geography, uncertainty, routes and revision.
10. **Operations board** — supplied routines, maintenance, custody and safe
    offline state.
11. **Continuity archive** — succession access, current system status,
    provenance and unfinished work.

These views share the same data. They are not separate management games.

### 24.4 Mobile interaction law

Every common action must support:

- one obvious contextual intent;
- inspect before dangerous commitment;
- large and movable touch controls;
- left/right-handed layouts;
- hold/toggle alternatives;
- cancellation before the commit boundary;
- clear interaction ownership;
- camera protection while menus are open;
- visible mode state;
- recovery after focus loss.

Precision assembly may use a work surface, snap logic, step selection and
preview rather than demanding mouse-like pixel accuracy on glass.

### 24.5 Accessibility is multi-channel truth

Important cues use at least two channels where practical:

- visual + audio;
- audio + caption/direction;
- color + shape/pattern;
- vibration + screen cue;
- motion + text description.

Assistive timing can slow private-world commitment windows or increase warning
clarity. It cannot disguise a rules change in shared Community play.

## 26. Complete inter-system braids

### 26.1 Death to competence braid

> Survivor notices recurring pump fault → writes symptoms but not cause → dies
> away from island → new castaway translates → finds Continuity Cache → can
> operate water but not repair pump → follows dated log → compares sound and
> cistern level → builds low-risk test → discovers air leak → repairs with poor
> first workmanship → records result → later maintainer verifies → next life
> inherits a better procedure and test port, not magical mechanics skill.

### 26.2 Wreckfall to epidemic braid

> Fishing vessel displaces offshore → precursor radio burst → impact breaks fuel
> tank → debris reaches lagoon → rats/standing water reach shore → rushed salvage
> ignores containment → rainfall creates vector habitat → fever cases appear on
> Community cargo route → clinic compares exposure records → harbor quarantine
> and source reduction begin → trade dispute tests public trust → recovered pump
> eventually improves sanitation.

### 26.3 Cave to water-security braid

> Drought lowers spring → survivor maps sinkhole airflow → samples brackish pool
> → observes tide lag → recognizes underground sea connection → avoids poisoning
> main cistern → installs monitored non-potable pump → cave survey finds fresh
> lens seep → careful extraction risks salt intrusion → weather logs and
> hydrology create a sustainable limit → aquifer becomes emergency reserve, not
> infinite well.

### 26.4 Community death to return braid

> Skilled navigator dies during rescue → charts remain in harbor office →
> witnesses remember unresolved promise → next castaway crosses from private
> island with no inherited reputation → presents a route notebook carrying the
> same calibration marks → former apprentice agrees to compare evidence →
> navigator's plan is revised rather than blindly trusted → new crew proves
> route with unmanned float → departure program continues under different
> leadership.

### 26.5 Mature base to disaster braid

> Years of safety produce inspection complacency → archive shows generator
> modifications but switchboard drawing is stale → cyclone warning forces choice
> between pump and refrigeration → operator hears contactor chatter → shuts down
> unsafe branch → food warms but electrical fire is prevented → communal kitchen
> preserves remaining stock → investigation updates drawing and adds isolation
> point → progress comes from surviving with a changed system, not replacing a
> durability bar.

### 26.6 Culture to survival braid

> Community meal establishes a recurring gathering → people exchange small
> observations → fisher mentions changed bird flight → weather observer connects
> it to distant swell → harbor service advances storm watch → boats are hauled
> early → storm destroys empty dock edge rather than crews → annual meal becomes
> a remembrance and practical start of storm preparation.

## 27. Ending and post-ending continuity

### 27.1 Successful escape belongs to one survivor

When a survivor reaches a valid return-home ending:

- that person leaves, arrives, is rescued or completes the anomaly transition;
- their body and mastery do not return to Spawn Island;
- carried property leaves the island economy;
- promises, people left behind and island systems continue;
- the ending archive records truth, uncertainty and consequence;
- the player has completed that lineage's goal.

### 27.2 Player choices after an ending

The player may:

1. close the island history as a completed chronicle;
2. continue observing the causal epilogue without controlling the escaped
   survivor;
3. permit a later Castaway Translation and begin a new human story on the same
   island;
4. begin a new private island seed for a genuinely fresh campaign;
5. continue Community Island participation through a new castaway who must
   physically return there.

No escaped survivor becomes an immortal account avatar.

### 27.3 Ending quality is multidimensional

The archive records:

- survival and body condition;
- who left and who remained;
- consent;
- truth of destination and time;
- craft/system condition;
- provisions and route proof;
- promises kept or broken;
- evidence delivered;
- Community consequences;
- private-island condition;
- successor readiness;
- lives and deaths that enabled the outcome.

There is no single best numerical score. An early lone rescue and a community
bluewater evacuation are different human endings.

## 29. Acceptance constitution

The v0.11 design fails if any of the following is true.

### Purpose

- the player faces more than five urgent-looking objectives without hierarchy;
- an objective exists only because a menu generated it;
- a deadline is invisible;
- the interface reveals an answer without evidence.

### Death and continuity

- the dead survivor reappears;
- a new survivor arrives as an occupant of routine Spawn wreckfall;
- mastery transfers automatically;
- a private archive unlocks because of account ownership rather than physical
  access;
- intentional death is an efficient character reroll;
- a technical fault commits irreversible death.

### Time and offline play

- different systems use irreconcilable clocks;
- logging out pauses public danger selectively for advantage;
- an absent protected character dies silently;
- the private island is arbitrarily destroyed without prior actionable state;
- offline work creates missing inputs.

### Logistics and maintenance

- heavy salvage enters inventory at discovery;
- storage ignores environment, access and load;
- all condition is a universal percentage;
- a repair consumes generic scrap and erases the mechanism;
- maintenance becomes mandatory repetitive clicking after the method is proven;
- decommissioned hazards disappear.

### Health and capability

- illness is only a bar;
- disability is equivalent to permanent uselessness;
- a brace or prosthetic cancels physics;
- isolation becomes a supernatural sanity meter;
- expert interpretation appears without a sensory cue.

### Ecology and terrain

- animals respawn as isolated food nodes;
- a cave is only a loot room;
- climbing is a Strength check;
- aquifer extraction is infinite;
- wreckfall organisms have no ecological consequence.

### Society

- NPCs exist only as vendors or quest markers;
- labor assignment overrides consent without consequence;
- reputation is one global number;
- institutions run without people, supplies, records or sites;
- a new castaway inherits the dead person's friends or crimes automatically;
- Community Island becomes compulsory for all endings.

### Conflict

- lethal combat is a damage-per-second contest;
- high skill creates bullet or blade immunity;
- surrender is graphic, rewarded abuse;
- evidence is infallible;
- private Spawn Islands can be raided;
- offline players can be trapped indefinitely.

### Mystery

- anomaly objects are generic research currency;
- one collectible unlocks an ending;
- the anomaly excuses missing causality;
- a hypothesis cannot be tested or contradicted;
- a Triangle event randomly selects an occupied safe player for death.

### Platform and persistence

- distant simulation creates matter or people;
- an object duplicates across islands;
- reduced device performance delays input or nearby lethal truth;
- save migration can silently alter death, ownership or depletion;
- automated graph audits cannot find mandatory single-item bottlenecks.

## 30. Research translation

This constitution translates real principles without turning the game into
professional training.

### 30.1 Human factors and maintenance

FAA maintenance guidance identifies communication, complacency, knowledge,
distraction, teamwork, fatigue, resources, pressure, assertiveness, awareness
and norms as common contributors to error. THE FIRST NIGHT therefore models
maintenance as people + environment + action + records, not a repair-skill
percentage.

Source:

- Federal Aviation Administration, *Human Factors in Aviation Maintenance*:
  https://www.faa.gov/about/initiatives/maintenance_hf

### 30.2 Tides, currents and natural calendars

Tidal range and current change with lunar relationships, and local measurements
are necessary for navigation and safety. The game therefore makes tide tables
learned local predictions, not a universal sine-wave HUD.

Sources:

- NOAA, *Tides & Currents Education*:
  https://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/education.html
- NOAA, *Tides and Currents*:
  https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/navigation/tidesandcurrents/

### 30.3 Caves and the island interior

Bermuda contains anchialine cave and karst subterranean-estuary systems whose
salinity, habitat and water relationships connect land and sea. This supports a
subterranean domain based on hydrology, fragile ecology and access risk rather
than fantasy ore corridors.

Sources:

- van Hengstum et al., *Development of anchialine cave habitats and karst
  subterranean estuaries since the last ice age*:
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-48058-8
- Cresswell et al., *Habitat Partitioning in the Marine Sector of Karst
  Subterranean Estuaries and Bermuda's Walsingham Cave System*:
  https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/environmental-science/articles/10.3389/fenvs.2020.594554/full

### 30.4 Public health and environmental causality

Floodwater can contaminate drinking, irrigation and recreational water, while
animals, waste and standing water can create disease exposure. The game links
water, sanitation, vectors, wildlife, records and community response instead
of generating random sickness.

Sources:

- CDC, *Waterborne Infectious Diseases Associated with Exposure to Tropical
  Cyclonic Storms*:
  https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/29/8/22-1906_article
- CDC, *About Leptospirosis*:
  https://www.cdc.gov/leptospirosis/about/index.html
- WHO, *Environmental health in emergencies and disasters*:
  https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/42561/9241545410_eng.pdf

### 30.5 Community incident organization

Real community emergency response joins fire safety, light search and rescue,
medical operations, communication, organization and damage assessment. This
supports institutions and practiced roles rather than a leader stat.

Sources:

- Ready.gov, *Community Emergency Response Team*:
  https://www.ready.gov/cert
- Ready.gov, *CERT Basic Training Participant Manual*:
  https://www.ready.gov/sites/default/files/2019.CERT_.Basic_.PM_FINAL_508c.pdf

### 30.6 Isolation and social connection

Research associates social isolation and loneliness with changes in cognition,
stress and health. THE FIRST NIGHT uses sleep, attention, motivation,
communication and meaningful connection—never a sensational sanity bar.

Sources:

- Cacioppo and Hawkley, *Perceived Social Isolation and Cognition*:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2752489/
- Hawkley and Cacioppo, *Loneliness Matters*:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3874845/

### 30.7 Platform reality

Godot's web environment may use a single-threaded export for broad
compatibility; persistent multiplayer requires server-authoritative validation.
Android devices can thermally throttle sustained workloads. These constraints
support the R0–R3 truth-at-distance model and the rule that performance may
reduce presentation before input or lethal-state authority.

Sources:

- Godot, *Exporting for the Web*:
  https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/tutorials/export/exporting_for_web.html
- Godot, *High-level multiplayer — Secure multiplayer design*:
  https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/networking/high_level_multiplayer.html
- Android Developers, *Thermal API*:
  https://developer.android.com/games/optimize/adpf/thermal

## 31. Final canon statement

The complete game identity is now:

> **The island combines pressures. The human combines capabilities. Water hides
> capability. Matter must be moved. Systems age. Knowledge needs a carrier.
> Society remembers deeds. The Triangle can be tested. Every survivor has one
> life; every player inherits a world, not a body.**

The revised campaign is:

> **Translate alone → survive the naked minute → interpret the inherited island
> → choose among competing needs and windows → move matter and externalize
> knowledge → build systems that require stewardship → explore land, height,
> caves, sea and depth → cross into human society or pursue a solitary return →
> cooperate, trade, investigate, conflict or depart → leave alive or die →
> allow the world to remember truthfully.**

The largest remaining work is no longer discovering more features. It is
proving, through small vertical slices, that these relationships remain clear,
fair, performant and enjoyable in a player's hands.
