# THE FIRST NIGHT — Beneath the Island

**Underwater World, Diving, Wreck Salvage and Amphibious Capability Weave v0.10 — 26 July 2026**

**Project:** THE FIRST NIGHT  
**Internal codename:** DRIFT / RUSTED  
**Engine and target:** Godot 4.x; first-person 3D; web and Android  
**Status:** Authoritative supplement to v0.7, v0.8 and v0.9

---

## 1. Executive correction

Diving was present in the existing canon, but it was not developed in proportion
to its importance.

Earlier specifications already established:

- swimming, breath discipline and current reading;
- reef crossings and recovery swims;
- submerged-wreck survey;
- underwater hull inspection and salvage-line placement;
- breath-hold blackout, entanglement, silt, pressure injury and drowning;
- forty-two swim-enabled routes and several underwater dependencies within the
  return-home endings.

Those elements are retained. This specification makes the missing constitutional
change:

> **The underwater world is a third major terrain, work domain and progression
> network—not a blue corridor between islands.**

The playable world now has three interdependent faces:

1. **Land** sustains, shelters, cultivates and transforms.
2. **Surface water** connects, transports, fishes, signals and threatens.
3. **The submerged world** conceals wreckage, evidence, rare capability,
   ecological knowledge and lethal uncertainty.

Diving must be valuable from the first day without becoming mandatory for first-
night survival. It must remain useful after boats, workshops and the Community
Island are reached. It must offer several technical solutions to the same
underwater problem. It must never become a linear depth ladder in which a larger
air tank automatically unlocks better loot.

The signature loop is:

> **Notice → infer → survey → plan → enter → navigate → work → recover → preserve
> → transform → revisit deeper or differently.**

The player does not “loot a wreck.” The player conducts an underwater operation.

## 2. Binding canon

### 2.1 One survivor, one life

Every survivor still has one irreversible life.

- Unconsciousness is not death and may still permit rescue where another person
  is present.
- Once terminal death is committed, that survivor never returns.
- A new castaway begins on the player's persistent private Spawn Island.
- The dead survivor's physical work persists according to world rules.
- Body capacity, practiced technique, private memory and relationships do not
  transfer to the next survivor.
- Written dive maps, marked lines, recovered tools, shore infrastructure,
  moorings, winches, decompression records, remotely operated equipment and
  preserved salvage can remain.

Underwater death does not produce a magical recovery marker. The body and
equipment remain subject to depth, current, entanglement, visibility, tide,
scavenging, wreck movement and later discovery. A successor can recover them
only through evidence and capability.

### 2.2 Two islands, two diving cultures

#### Private Spawn Island

- Contains no arriving living people.
- Aircraft, boats, cargo and debris may crash, sink, strand or be displaced into
  its waters without occupants.
- New wrecks can alter currents, reef passages, contamination, hazards and
  available materials.
- All early diving is solitary.
- Solitary conditions do not make unsafe practices safe; they make avoidance,
  remote work, line systems, recovery engineering and conservative aborts more
  important.
- The island can accumulate a private marine workshop, slipway, observation
  tower, dive station, hoist, survey archive and unmanned recovery capability
  across many lives.

#### Community Island

- Contains living people, teaching, trade, institutions, rivalry, theft and
  cooperation.
- Supports buddy teams, tenders, boat crews, medics, trained supervisors,
  communal compressors, maintained cylinders, recompression capability, sonar
  surveys and large salvage projects.
- Creates professions and politics around dive access, maps, recovered cargo,
  ecological protection and ownership.
- Does not invalidate a skilled solo player. It changes which methods are
  rational, how quickly difficult work can be done, and how much residual risk
  can be reduced.

### 2.3 No magical equipment

- A mask does not grant underwater vision if it leaks, fogs or is scratched.
- Fins improve propulsion only when they fit, remain attached and suit the
  water.
- A cylinder is not usable merely because it looks intact.
- An aircraft oxygen cylinder is not automatically safe diving equipment.
- A compressor does not automatically produce breathable air.
- A regulator, valve, hose, gauge or seal must match the pressure, gas, material
  and service condition.
- Improvised pressure vessels and improvised underwater breathing systems are
  not casual crafting recipes.
- Reading a manual creates knowledge evidence, not trained reflexes or a safe
  apparatus.

The game may permit restoration and adaptation of credible equipment, but only
through inspection, compatible components, clean gas handling, proof testing,
maintenance and appropriate learned capability.

## 3. Why the player goes underwater

Diving must answer several different motivations, not only greed.

| Motivation | Underwater objective | Downstream consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate survival | Retrieve a floating container, line, knife, water can, raft pack or medical case | Alters the first day without making diving compulsory |
| Shelter and tools | Recover canvas, fasteners, tubing, valves, cable, glass, sheet or sealed containers | Accelerates construction and repair |
| Food and ecology | Observe fish movement, shell beds, reef condition, seagrass and spawning zones | Improves sustainable gathering and weather/ecology inference |
| Navigation | Mark reef passages, depth changes, current lanes, anchors and submerged landmarks | Enables safer launch, landing and inter-island routes |
| Boatbuilding | Inspect hulls, keels, propellers, through-hulls, moorings and launch channels | Prevents hidden failure and opens restoration |
| Power and machinery | Recover motors, pumps, alternators, hydraulic parts, batteries or intact mechanisms | Opens workshop and infrastructure branches |
| Communication | Recover protected radios, recorders, beacons, cable, aerial components or instrument cases | Enables rescue evidence and Triangle research |
| Medicine | Recover sealed kits or identify contamination while avoiding unsafe cargo | Creates rare treatment opportunities and new hazards |
| Story | Read wreck orientation, impact damage, era, cargo, markings and impossible displacement | Reveals how the island accumulates history |
| Community | Survey public wrecks, rescue divers, map resources, enforce or violate claims | Creates trust, profession, crime and institutional memory |
| Escape | Obtain the exact component, route proof, hull survey or anomaly record needed by an ending | Turns diving into part of the return-home architecture |

The best underwater rewards are **capability-changing objects**:

- an intact hand pump rather than ten units of metal;
- a pressure-rated valve rather than universal scrap;
- a sealed chart case rather than a navigation skill point;
- an aircraft recorder containing timing evidence rather than a lore collectible;
- a compatible propeller shaft rather than an engine upgrade token;
- a working depth sounder transducer rather than a generic electronic part;
- a corrosion-resistant cable that may serve a hoist, antenna or steering system;
- a recoverable inflatable raft cylinder that creates several strategic choices.

## 4. The submerged geography

Depth is only one variable. Access also depends on distance, water movement,
temperature, visibility, overhead obstruction, bottom type, contamination,
surface conditions, exit routes and the work being attempted.

### Zone U0 — Wash and wreck edge

The boundary where waves, tide and wreckage repeatedly move.

**Opportunities:** loose containers, line, fabric, small tools, flotation,
identification plates and clues to the debris trail.

**Hazards:** knockdown, backwash, rolling wreckage, sharp edges, foot traps,
surge, head injury and being drawn seaward.

**Early forks:** wait for tide, use a hook, secure from land, enter with
flotation, build a retrieval line, or abandon the object.

### Zone U1 — Lagoon and protected shallows

Clear or turbid water with visible bottom and frequent air access.

**Opportunities:** first controlled submersion, mask practice, shell/fish
observation, small wreck pieces, dropped tools, route markers and basic hull
inspection.

**Hazards:** coral cuts, venomous organisms, stingrays, contaminated pockets,
unexpected channels, sun/heat and false confidence.

**Design role:** training emerges from useful work; there is no disconnected
diving tutorial.

### Zone U2 — Reef face, pass and surf channel

Three-dimensional terrain shaped by waves, current, coral, rock and tide.

**Opportunities:** passage survey, anchors, fishing evidence, wreck trails,
valuable components lodged in structure, observation of outgoing and returning
current.

**Hazards:** breakers, surge, abrasion, entrapment, current separation, poor
exit timing, rising turbidity and lost orientation.

**Design role:** Perception, Composure and marine knowledge matter as much as
Stamina.

### Zone U3 — Coastal wreck field

Aircraft and vessel remains reachable by advanced breath-hold, restored scuba,
surface-supported systems or remote methods depending on the exact site.

**Opportunities:** intact compartments, instruments, machinery, sealed stores,
rigging, structural members, black boxes, charts, fuel systems and anomaly
evidence.

**Hazards:** sharp metal, collapse, entanglement, silt-out, trapped loads,
contaminants, unstable cargo, overhead spaces and long task fixation.

**Design role:** this is a worksite, not a treasure room.

### Zone U4 — Outer shelf and deep wreck

Beyond rational casual breath-hold recovery. Bottom time, gas, ascent,
temperature, surface support, navigation and recovery engineering dominate.

**Opportunities:** less-stripped wrecks, intact engines, heavy components,
historic vessels, deep anomaly instruments and rare hulls.

**Hazards:** pressure exposure, gas limitation, decompression obligation,
narcosis or other gas effects where applicable, equipment failure, darkness,
cold, current, missed ascent location and inability to lift the target.

**Design role:** unlocks through a network of medicine, equipment,
infrastructure, seamanship, planning and team/remote capability—not a single
diving level.

### Zone U5 — Blue-water and anomaly depth

No ordinary diver casually operates here.

**Methods:** sonar, cameras, weighted probes, grapnels where appropriate,
remotely operated vehicles, winches, lift engineering, submersible apparatus,
temporary anomaly effects and Community-scale projects.

**Opportunities:** major wreck bodies, impossible materials, corridor evidence,
lost beacons and endgame discoveries.

**Hazards:** crushing pressure, cable loss, vehicle entrapment, power failure,
navigation divergence, false readings, extreme recovery loads and anomaly
exposure.

**Design role:** Reasoning and infrastructure let the player reach where body
stats cannot.

## 5. The underwater operation model

Every meaningful site uses a persistent operation state:

> **Suspected → located → observed → mapped → hazard-assessed → marked → accessed
> → stabilized → target-identified → detached → secured → lifted/towed → landed
> → cleaned/preserved → tested → integrated**

Stages can be skipped only when the physical situation genuinely permits it.
Skipping creates uncertainty rather than a hidden percentage penalty.

### 5.1 Suspected and located

Evidence can include:

- debris type and drift direction;
- oil, bubbles or discoloration;
- a mast, wing, cable or shadow;
- sound, magnetometer or receiver response;
- fish behavior and altered reef growth;
- old maps or a dead survivor's field notes;
- a storm exposing or moving material;
- a wreckfall event observed from shore;
- inconsistent Triangle measurements.

### 5.2 Observed and mapped

The player records:

- entry and exit points;
- depth class and bottom profile;
- current direction and change;
- visibility;
- overhead spaces;
- loose cable, nets, structure and sharp hazards;
- target location and estimated mass;
- lift/tow route;
- surface support position;
- environmental or cargo contamination;
- abort conditions.

Mapping can be mental and temporary, marked physically, sketched on a slate,
written at camp, photographed by restored equipment or stored in a digital
survey archive. Only persistent records survive character death reliably.

### 5.3 Accessed and stabilized

Access may require:

- waiting for tide or weather;
- clearing an exterior line;
- bracing or avoiding unstable structure;
- opening rather than destroying a hatch;
- pumping or draining a partially submerged space;
- moving the entire wreck;
- cutting from outside with the correct method;
- using a camera or tool on a pole;
- sending a remote device;
- choosing a different target.

Penetration is never the assumed answer. Many valuable targets can be reached
by removing an exterior panel, shifting the wreck, draining a compartment,
working through an opening, towing the wreck shallower or using a remote tool.

### 5.4 Identified and detached

The player must understand both the target and what holds it.

An excellent mechanic may recognize a pump but lack the underwater dexterity
to remove it. A strong diver may tear it free and destroy the casing. A careful
diver may preserve the pump yet fail to notice that the mounting carries a
shifted load. A rigger may decide to lift the surrounding frame intact.

### 5.5 Secured and recovered

Underwater mass is deceptive. A target that can be shifted underwater may
become unmanageable at the surface or on shore. Recovery considers:

- apparent and actual mass;
- trapped water, mud or air;
- center of mass and attachment points;
- lift force and control;
- snag path;
- surface waves;
- boat stability;
- shore landing;
- personnel placement;
- containment if hazardous.

Recovery methods include hand carry for small objects, tethered bags,
baskets, drag lines, staged caches, floats, lift devices, boat cranes, shore
capstans, pontoons, pumps, controlled tow, remote grab and dismantling in place.

### 5.6 Preserved and integrated

Recovery is not completion.

- Salt, sediment and biological growth can continue damaging a component.
- Wet documents and organic material need appropriate stabilization.
- Batteries, fuel, chemicals and pressure vessels require isolation.
- A mechanism may need fresh-water cleaning, drying, lubrication, inspection
  and testing.
- Historical or anomaly evidence can be destroyed by careless cleaning.
- Community ownership and archaeological value can conflict with immediate use.

## 6. Diving modes are alternative branches

There is no fixed order, although later methods usually require more
infrastructure.

| Mode | Best use | Strength | Binding limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shore reach/pole/hook | Wash zone and visible shallows | No submersion | Limited reach and control |
| Wade and surface swim | Loose nearshore objects | Minimal equipment | Surf, footing and carrying |
| Breath-hold dive | Short open-water observation and small recovery | Quiet, simple, mobile | Hypoxia, cold, task fixation and short work window |
| Snorkel survey | Repeated surface observation and route marking | Long visual search with airway near surface | Waves, sun, current, limited work depth |
| Line-tended shallow work | Marking, tool passing, small recovery | Surface reference and retrieval aid | Line can snag; no substitute for breathing reserve |
| Restored open-circuit equipment | Mobile survey and moderate work | Longer underwater time | Gas, maintenance, pressure exposure and training |
| Surface-supplied system | Sustained local work with support | Communications and surface gas potential | Umbilical, machinery, tender and station dependency |
| Drain/pump/cofferdam | Partially submerged compartment | Converts dive work into surface work | Construction, power, seal and stability |
| Tow/roll/reposition | Reach target without penetration | Changes geometry | Rigging loads, tide, boat/shore power |
| Camera/probe/sonar | Locate and inspect | Keeps body out of danger | Interpretation and limited manipulation |
| ROV/remote manipulator | Deep or contaminated site | Persistent remote access | Power, control, cable, repair and operator skill |
| Engineered heavy salvage | Hull, engine or major structure | Recovers unique capability | Community-scale engineering and risk |

The game rewards choosing the least dangerous method that can accomplish the
objective. Entering the water personally is not automatically the most skilled
choice.

## 7. The amphibious capability hypergraph

A diving capability exists only when the required capacities, techniques,
knowledge, tools, condition and proof meet the current task.

Conceptually:

`available capability = body ∩ technique ∩ knowledge ∩ perception ∩ equipment ∩ condition ∩ site evidence`

The intersection symbol is intentional. Exceptional Strength cannot replace an
exit route. Exceptional Stamina cannot replace breathable gas. Excellent
Reasoning cannot replace practiced equalization or equipment inspection.

### 7.1 Foundational human variables

- Strength
- Stamina
- Dexterity
- Mobility
- Perception
- Reasoning
- Composure
- present warmth and energy
- hydration and nutrition
- injury and illness
- fear, fatigue and confidence accuracy

### 7.2 Underwater technique families

- water comfort and airway control;
- efficient surface swimming;
- breath discipline without exploitative hyperventilation;
- descent, ascent and equalization;
- buoyancy and trim;
- fin or bare-foot propulsion;
- low-visibility orientation;
- underwater hand-tool use;
- line handling and release;
- search patterns;
- object securing;
- re-entry and surf exit;
- casualty approach and tow;
- equipment assembly, check and maintenance;
- gas and exposure planning;
- remote observation and manipulation.

### 7.3 Knowledge families

- tides, waves, current and surf;
- reef and underwater terrain;
- weather and launch conditions;
- human physiology and diving medicine;
- materials, corrosion and contamination;
- mechanics and electrical systems;
- wreck structure and load paths;
- rigging, buoyancy and lifting;
- boats, anchoring and seamanship;
- ecology and cultural-resource stewardship;
- navigation, mapping and instruments;
- anomaly observation and experimental method.

## 8. Twelve non-linear development braids

Each braid has several entrances, forks and recombinations. None is a mandatory
class.

### Braid A — The visible object

Floating debris  
→ wading retrieval **or** hook-and-line retrieval  
→ secure carry **or** tow  
→ examine provenance  
→ preserve component **or** dismantle material  
→ toolmaking / shelter / signal / boat / archive

This is the day-one entrance to salvage reasoning without requiring a dive.

### Braid B — The first submerged container

Shallow sighting  
→ wait for lower tide **or** calm water **or** improve reach  
→ breath-hold retrieve **or** line basket **or** move it shoreward  
→ discover it is sealed, flooded or trapped  
→ open now **or** transport intact **or** record and return  
→ supplies / contamination / knowledge / disappointment

### Braid C — Swimming becomes diving

Water comfort  
→ efficient stroke / floating / surf exit  
→ Calm Submersion  
→ Efficient Descent + Reserve Reader  
→ observation **or** retrieval **or** line work  
→ rescue / hull survey / ecology / navigation / salvage

Stamina improves the envelope but never supplies judgment by itself.

### Braid D — Diving without diving

Shore observation  
→ pole camera / viewing box / weighted line  
→ depth map / target mark  
→ grapnel or basket **or** pump/drain **or** tow/reposition  
→ remote camera  
→ ROV / sonar / recovery engineering

Reasoning, fabrication and electronics can outperform athleticism.

### Braid E — The wreck exterior

Debris trail  
→ locate hull  
→ map current and exits  
→ exterior survey  
→ propeller/keel/through-hull inspection **or** accessible compartment  
→ vessel restoration / component recovery / route safety / story evidence

### Braid F — The intact machine

Recognize mechanism  
→ identify mounts, lines and stored energy  
→ isolate **or** leave intact  
→ stabilize structure  
→ selective dismantling **or** lift surrounding frame  
→ land and conserve  
→ restore original function **or** adapt into new infrastructure

Mechanics without diving cannot reach it; diving without mechanics may destroy
it.

### Braid G — The deep signal

Receiver anomaly / log / survivor archive  
→ bearing comparison  
→ boat transect / depth sounding  
→ camera or sonar confirmation  
→ ROV survey **or** supported dive where rational  
→ retrieve recorder / beacon / sensor  
→ rescue proof / false signal diagnosis / Triangle program

### Braid H — Food and reef

Shore gathering  
→ identify safe species and closures  
→ snorkel observation  
→ map nursery/spawning sites  
→ fish/trap selectively **or** protect habitat  
→ diagnose decline, contamination or storm damage  
→ sustainable food / reef restoration / Community regulation

### Braid I — Diver becomes medic

Body warning recognized  
→ conservative abort  
→ record conditions and symptoms  
→ physiology study / treatment knowledge  
→ equipment or procedure correction  
→ assist another diver  
→ Diving Medic / mission planner / institutional safety

Surviving recklessness does not grant mastery. Correct recognition and response
does.

### Braid J — Salvage becomes boatbuilding

Underwater hull survey  
→ material/corrosion grading  
→ retrieve fitting or pattern  
→ naval construction comparison  
→ repair test section  
→ restore small craft **or** apply principle to handmade hull  
→ sea trial / dive boat / escape craft

### Braid K — Solitary Spawn Island legacy

Risky site discovered  
→ successor finds notes, marker or abandoned tool  
→ builds safer shore station  
→ adds winch, mooring, camera, weather record or recovery rig  
→ later survivor completes recovery  
→ recovered capability changes the island

The island remembers the operation, not the dead survivor's technique.

### Braid L — Community marine institution

Individual map or recovered object  
→ trade, conceal or publish  
→ diver/tender/medic collaboration  
→ shared station and maintenance ledger  
→ salvage claim, protected wreck or black market  
→ rescue service / public infrastructure / faction control / endgame project

## 9. Cross-capacity signatures

The same nominal dive changes according to the human doing it.

| Human weave | Advantage | New temptation or weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Strength without Stamina | Moves a stuck object briefly | Exhausts rapidly and may overcommit |
| Strength + Stamina | Tows, carries and works against resistance longer | Still cannot diagnose mount or route |
| Strength + Dexterity | Applies controlled force to tools and fasteners | Can damage unseen load paths without Reasoning |
| Strength + Reasoning + rigging | Redirects force through lift and mechanical advantage | Requires sound anchors and inspected materials |
| Stamina without Perception | Remains active longer | Can travel farther into the wrong current |
| Stamina + Perception + swimming | Chooses efficient route and preserves reserve | Weather or injury can still close the route |
| Dexterity without Composure | Performs fine work in calm conditions | Loses quality during leak, entanglement or darkness |
| Dexterity + Composure + mechanics | Conducts controlled underwater repair | Cannot certify gas or structure without knowledge |
| Mobility + spatial Perception | Moves cleanly around exterior structure | Overhead entry still requires different preparation |
| Perception without knowledge | Notices corrosion, bubbles or strange growth | May misidentify cause and response |
| Perception + Reasoning + records | Compares site changes and detects instability | Needs tools to act on diagnosis |
| Reasoning without water practice | Designs a strong operation on paper | May be unable to execute or sense bodily limits |
| Composure without physiology | Feels calm under pressure | Calmly exceeds a real limit |
| Composure + physiology + honest practice | Aborts before crisis and preserves options | Gives up some immediate prizes |

The desired emotional result is that a survivor becomes recognizable through
how they solve underwater problems—not through a displayed “Diving 73” number.

## 10. Body, breath, pressure and condition

### 10.1 Breath-hold model

The game tracks a hidden physiological reserve influenced by:

- pre-dive condition;
- exertion before and during the dive;
- stress and inefficient motion;
- water temperature;
- injury, illness, hydration and energy;
- practiced movement economy;
- time and work intensity;
- ascent demand.

The player receives imperfect bodily evidence:

- urge to breathe;
- involuntary strain;
- narrowing attention;
- clumsy hands;
- route fixation;
- visual and auditory change;
- poor decision speed;
- loss of purposeful control.

Warnings are not a precise countdown. An expert learns to leave a real reserve
for ascent, surface conditions and unexpected delay.

The game must not reward dangerous hyperventilation as an optimal preparation
minigame. It can suppress early warning without creating the safety players may
assume. Mastery means honest reserve estimation and early aborts.

### 10.2 Compressed-gas model

Once compressed breathing equipment exists, the simulation adds:

- gas amount and credible reserve;
- breathing workload;
- depth and time exposure;
- ascent history;
- pressure equalization;
- gas contamination and compatibility;
- regulator/valve/hose/gauge condition;
- buoyancy and weighting;
- thermal exposure;
- post-dive symptoms and activity restrictions.

There is no universal “oxygen” bar. Breath-hold hypoxia, cylinder gas quantity,
carbon-dioxide retention, contaminated gas and oxygen exposure are different
problems.

### 10.3 Injury and impairment

Underwater-relevant states include:

- ear or sinus pain and barotrauma;
- vertigo and orientation loss;
- coral, shell and metal cuts;
- puncture and envenomation;
- cold impairment;
- heat and sun exposure at the surface;
- aspiration and nonfatal drowning injury;
- crush or pinning;
- decompression illness;
- gas toxicity or contamination;
- exhaustion and delayed recovery;
- panic, fixation and confidence error.

An injury can close only some branches. A damaged hand may prevent fine
salvage while still permitting mapping or remote operation. Ear injury may
close diving but increase the value of pumps, poles, boats and ROVs.

## 11. Water, weather and wrecks form one pressure weave

Dive sites evolve rather than reset.

| Combination | Opportunity | Threat and world change |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy rain + runoff | New debris trail | Zero visibility, contamination and silt burial |
| Drought + low water | Previously submerged access | Heat, unstable exposure and ecological stress |
| Cyclone + wreck field | New cargo and opened compartments | Shifted structure, sharp debris and lost markers |
| Storm surge + coastal wreck | Material moved shoreward | Salinity, violent loads and buried hazards |
| Tsunami + reef | New channel or exposed hull | Complete route invalidation and massive debris movement |
| Earthquake + wreck | Door or compartment opens | Collapse, trapped load and seabed instability |
| Wildfire + rain | Ash and runoff reveal current | Contaminated lagoon and reef damage |
| Fuel wreck + current | Recoverable machinery | Moving toxic/flammable plume and fish loss |
| Algal bloom + warm water | Ecological evidence | Unsafe water, low visibility and illness |
| Wreckfall + calm sea | Time-limited intact salvage | Fire, fuel, sinking, structural motion and urgency |
| Compass divergence + deep site | Triangle evidence | Navigation disagreement and false return |
| Current reversal + old map | New route insight | Trusted exit becomes a trap |

Forecasting never removes uncertainty. It converts surprise into a decision:
dive now, change method, protect infrastructure, mark the site, or wait.

## 12. Hidden salvage architecture

### 12.1 Reward placement rules

1. A site must be inferable before it is visible.
2. Valuable objects follow plausible wreck structure, cargo and impact.
3. The best target may be shallow but technically difficult.
4. Depth does not directly determine value.
5. Every rare target has at least two meaningful uses.
6. Every critical basic-survival function retains a non-diving path.
7. Extraction can damage condition and future use.
8. Some sites are better preserved, mapped or protected than stripped.
9. Recovered mass remains in the world's material economy.
10. New wreckfalls can replenish technological opportunity, not natural
    ecology or human mastery.

### 12.2 Salvage families

| Family | Examples | Cross-system uses |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency | raft pack, flotation, beacon, sealed water, medical case | first-day survival, rescue, crossings |
| Textile and line | webbing, rope, cable, net, sailcloth | shelter, rigging, fishing, hoists, boats |
| Containers | drums, cases, tanks, lockers, galley vessels | water, food, quarantine, flotation |
| Structure | timber, frames, aluminum panels, hatches, spars | buildings, boat repair, machines |
| Flow control | pumps, valves, hoses, filters, through-hulls | water systems, irrigation, diving, engines |
| Mechanical | gears, shafts, bearings, winches, propellers | workshops, boats, lifts, power |
| Electrical | cable, motors, batteries, lights, switches, transducers | radio, grid, tools, sonar, ROV |
| Navigation | compass, chart, sextant, log, sounder, GPS components | mapping, crossing, endings |
| Communication | radio, recorder, antenna parts, locator beacon | rescue, Community contact, anomaly evidence |
| Medical | sealed kits, oxygen hardware, splints, sterile supplies | treatment; pressure hardware remains specialist |
| Fuel/chemical | diesel, oil, gas, refrigerant, cleaning agents | power and process, but high contamination/fire cost |
| Knowledge | manuals, labels, plates, logs, recorder data | recognition and principles, not instant mastery |
| Historical | tools, weapons, personal effects, cargo, markings | era history, trade, ethics, design patterns |
| Anomalous | phase-stable glass, altered sensor, cold cell, impossible log | experiments and endings with instability |

### 12.3 Quality is spatial and causal

The same component differs by:

- depth and time submerged;
- salt exposure and corrosion;
- burial in protective or damaging sediment;
- impact deformation;
- biological growth;
- contamination;
- extraction damage;
- cleaning and preservation;
- original manufacturing era;
- compatibility with the intended system.

This makes repeat site knowledge valuable without turning wrecks into fixed
loot tables.

## 13. Failure and death chains

Permanent death demands understandable causality.

### 13.1 Breath-hold fixation

Valuable object seen  
→ diver spends reserve freeing it  
→ ascent begins late  
→ impairment suppresses response  
→ loss of consciousness near surface  
→ drowning.

**Counterplay:** smaller objective, surface interval, retrieval line, abandon
target, alternate method, support.

### 13.2 Silt and lost exit

Poor trim or disturbed debris  
→ visibility collapses  
→ diver loses orientation  
→ searches deeper into overhead area  
→ gas/breath and composure degrade  
→ exit missed  
→ drowning.

**Counterplay:** exterior-first survey, no entry, continuous route reference,
silt-aware movement, remote camera, drain/reposition wreck.

### 13.3 Entanglement cascade

Line/net/cable not mapped  
→ fin, valve, limb or tool catches  
→ force tightens entanglement  
→ cutting attempt fails or tool inaccessible  
→ reserve exhausted  
→ drowning.

**Counterplay:** route clearing, configuration inspection, accessible release
tools, support, abandon overhead site.

### 13.4 False lift

Heavy target rigged without load-path knowledge  
→ lift begins  
→ target shifts or trapped air changes force  
→ structure collapses or line snaps  
→ diver pinned/struck or boat destabilized  
→ fatal crush or drowning.

**Counterplay:** load survey, staged test, exclusion zone, remote lift, correct
anchors, recovery engineer.

### 13.5 Contaminated compartment

Sealed cargo appears valuable  
→ leak or residue ignored  
→ diver and equipment exposed  
→ symptoms emerge underwater or later  
→ judgment/respiration/organ function fails  
→ death or persistent site contamination.

**Counterplay:** recognize markings and ecology, isolate, sample remotely,
quarantine, reject target.

### 13.6 Pressure-system confidence

Restored apparatus passes superficial check  
→ contaminated gas, incompatible seal or hidden corrosion remains  
→ breathing or buoyancy failure at depth  
→ emergency ascent or incapacitation  
→ pressure injury, drowning or decompression illness.

**Counterplay:** provenance, component compatibility, clean handling, proof
testing, redundant reserve, maintained records and trained inspection.

### 13.7 Weather closes the surface

Dive begins in marginal window  
→ wind/current/sea state rises  
→ boat or exit moves  
→ diver surfaces separated  
→ exhaustion, exposure and loss of visibility  
→ drowning.

**Counterplay:** forecast margin, fixed alternatives, surface tender, beacon,
conservative no-go decision.

### 13.8 Success causes the death

Target is recovered  
→ exhausted diver attempts heavy surf landing  
→ object traps hands or changes buoyancy  
→ wave knocks diver down  
→ head injury or submersion  
→ death within sight of safety.

**Counterplay:** stage object, tow it, shore team, landing plan, preserve return
reserve.

The game records the full chain, not merely “drowned.”

## 14. Enjoyment and presentation

The system must feel tense, beautiful and legible—not administrative.

### 14.1 The reveal rhythm

1. The player notices a clue from safety.
2. The water partially reveals the site.
3. A first survey answers one question and creates two more.
4. The player chooses a target and method.
5. The operation produces a physical change.
6. Recovery reveals function, history or a new problem.
7. The site remains for later approaches.

### 14.2 Information is layered

Novices see:

- “current feels stronger”;
- “ears hurt”;
- “hands are becoming clumsy”;
- “the cable is moving”;
- “water turned cloudy”;
- “this case is heavier than expected.”

Experienced survivors perceive:

- tide-relative current change;
- equalization failure rather than generic pain;
- early thermal impairment;
- a loaded cable rather than decorative motion;
- silt caused by fin wash;
- water trapped inside the case;
- corrosion pattern suggesting the wrong lifting point.

Expertise reveals better evidence; it does not paint a perfect safe path.

### 14.3 No glowing loot

Targets are found through shape, color, reflection, bubbles, markings, sound,
instrument response, debris association and learned wreck anatomy. Optional
accessibility assists may improve contrast or provide non-diegetic cues without
turning the site into a checklist of icons.

### 14.4 Tension comes from commitment

The player chooses:

- one more fastener or surface now;
- carry the target or mark it;
- enter the compartment or change the geometry;
- trust the old line or replace it;
- spend a rare seal on dive equipment, a water system or the escape craft;
- keep a wreck secret or publish its location;
- recover the engine or preserve the historical/anomaly evidence around it.

## 15. Community economy and conflict

Underwater capability creates social value without fixed classes.

Possible roles include:

- shore observer;
- swimmer and diver;
- tender;
- dive-boat operator;
- mapper;
- equipment maintainer;
- diving medic;
- salvage rigger;
- recovery engineer;
- conservator;
- marine ecologist;
- wreck historian;
- sonar/ROV operator;
- claim broker;
- safety inspector;
- smuggler or wreck thief.

Conflicts include:

- who discovered versus who recovered a wreck;
- whether a culturally important wreck may be dismantled;
- ecological closure versus immediate hunger;
- private coordinates versus public safety;
- who owns a component needed by several escape projects;
- falsified inspection or gas records;
- cut lines, shifted markers and sabotaged equipment;
- rescue duty toward a rival;
- dangerous dives demanded by leaders;
- black-market anomaly artifacts.

Sabotage must leave evidence: tool marks, changed knots, broken seals, altered
records, substituted components, missing reserves, moved markers or witness
history.

## 16. Connections to escape and endings

Underwater work can support every ending family without becoming a compulsory
key for all runs.

| Ending family | Underwater contribution | Non-diving alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Early rescue | recover beacon, raft pack or recorder | land signal, surviving radio |
| Community crossing | survey reef pass, anchor and landing | long observation, sounding pole/boat |
| Drift route | recover flotation, sea anchor or current evidence | handmade components and shore drift study |
| Handmade vessel | inspect launch channel; recover fittings | fully natural construction and surface survey |
| Restored boat | hull, keel, propeller and through-hull survey | haul vessel ashore before inspection |
| Powered vessel | recover engine/shaft/pump/electrical parts | land wreck or alternative propulsion |
| Aircraft | recover float, recorder, control or navigation parts | land-based aircraft wreck |
| Triangle corridor | retrieve sensor, cold cell or boundary record | radio, land station and remote probe chain |
| Swim-supported ending | cache, route, flotation and exit survey | surface craft support |

Some endings become qualitatively different:

- A restored yacht can fail because a hidden through-hull was never inspected.
- A reef map may turn a sturdy raft into a credible Community Island crossing.
- A recorder may authenticate that wreckfalls arrive empty on the Spawn Island.
- A deep anomaly sensor may reveal why people appear only on the Community
  Island.
- A recovered pump may serve irrigation for years before being reassigned to a
  salvage operation or escape craft.

## 17. Persistence across lives

The persistent Spawn Island makes underwater work unusually meaningful.

### Persists

- discovered wreck geometry unless physically changed;
- installed shore markers, moorings and observation posts;
- maps, slates, logs, photographs and instrument records stored safely;
- dive station, workshop, hoist, winch, boat and remote equipment;
- recovered and unrecovered physical objects;
- opened, collapsed, shifted, contaminated or protected sites;
- ecological damage and recovery;
- dead survivor and lost equipment according to world simulation.

### Does not persist magically

- remembered route not written down;
- breath discipline;
- equalization technique;
- calm under darkness;
- underwater dexterity;
- personal knowledge of a particular fastener or snag;
- trust earned with Community divers;
- the dead survivor's fitness or medical tolerance.

A later survivor may learn faster from a well-designed training pool, labeled
equipment, accurate site map and honest accident report. They still must
practice.

## 18. Anti-grind and anti-exploit rules

- Repeated harmless submersion does not endlessly increase capability.
- Remaining underwater until crisis teaches poor calibration and may impair or
  kill; it is not an efficient training strategy.
- Hyperventilation is never an optimal buff.
- Carrying excessive weight underwater does not safely train Strength.
- Intentionally damaging equipment does not cheaply train maintenance.
- Discovering a high-value target does not respawn it.
- Character death never triggers a desirable wreckfall.
- New survivors cannot harvest predecessor bodies for unique progression
  rewards.
- Equipment quality cannot erase missing knowledge or bad condition.
- High “diving mastery” never grants immunity to physics.
- The player is rewarded for varied tasks, accurate prediction, early aborts,
  clean execution, successful transfer and teaching—not raw repetition.

## 20. Acceptance constitution

The underwater system is successful only if:

1. Diving changes survival, construction, boats, knowledge, ecology, story and
   endings.
2. A player can gain meaningful underwater capability without following one
   linear sequence.
3. Strength, Stamina, Dexterity, Perception, Reasoning and Composure create
   genuinely different solutions.
4. The strongest diver can still make a fatal planning or engineering mistake.
5. A physically weak survivor can become a powerful marine operator through
   instruments, mapping, remote systems, planning and collaboration.
6. Every high-value recovery requires more than touching an object.
7. Basic survival and at least one credible escape route remain possible without
   diving.
8. The Spawn Island supports a deep solitary marine legacy without pretending
   that solo diving has buddy support.
9. The Community Island makes people materially important to advanced diving.
10. Weather, current, ecology and wreck condition change sites over time.
11. Death is causal, final for that survivor, and persistent in the world.
12. The player can look across the water and understand that the island continues
    beneath it.

## 21. Research basis and realism boundaries

The design uses professional diving guidance as a source of hazard structure,
planning variables and operational roles—not as a claim that improvised game
equipment would be safe in reality.

- The NOAA Diving Standards & Safety Manual establishes planning, equipment,
  personnel, environmental and operational safeguards and treats diving as a
  managed operation rather than an individual depth statistic:
  <https://omao.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/2023-05/NDSSM%20MAY%202023%20FINAL.pdf>
- The U.S. Navy Diving Manual separates breath-hold, self-contained,
  surface-supplied and other modes and documents the distinct physiological and
  operational hazards of diving:
  <https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUPSALV/Diving/US%20DIVING%20MANUAL_REV7.pdf>
- The U.S. Navy Salvage Manual and Salvor's Handbook frame recovery as
  engineering involving survey, stability, rigging, lifting, towing, machinery
  and risk control:
  <https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUPSALV/Salvage%20Docs/Salvage%20Manual%20Vol%201%20S0300-A6-MAN-010.pdf>
  and
  <https://www.navsea.navy.mil/Portals/103/Documents/SUPSALV/Salvage%20Docs/Salvors%20Handbook_Rev2.pdf>
- NOAA describes its diving training as stepwise development across basic,
  advanced, working, scientific, medical and first-aid capability, supporting
  the separation of water comfort, work technique, planning and emergency
  competence:
  <https://www.omao.noaa.gov/diving-program/noaa-diving-program-training>
- NOAA's operational account emphasizes a divemaster, equipment checks,
  communication, emergency preparation and debriefing, which informs the
  Community Island institution model:
  <https://www.omao.noaa.gov/diving-program/news-media/article/supporting-below-surface-science>

This is a survival-game simulation, not real-world diving instruction. The game
must never present unsafe improvised breathing or pressure equipment as a
transferable real-life procedure.

## 22. Final identity

The underwater system completes the game's material and human logic:

> **Nature sustains life. Wreckage accelerates capability. Infrastructure
> transforms materials. Knowledge reveals possibilities. The sea hides the
> island's accumulated history.**

And the new amphibious loop is:

> **The player first fears the water, then reads it, then works within it, then
> builds systems that reach beyond the body—while never becoming immune to it.**

The private Spawn Island remembers every marked reef, lost diver, shifted wreck,
recovered machine and unfinished operation. The Community Island remembers who
shared the map, who falsified the inspection, who returned with the missing
diver, and who used a priceless wreck to build a way home.

That makes diving not an optional minigame, but one of THE FIRST NIGHT's defining
forms of survival, discovery and progression.
