# THE FIRST NIGHT — THE ISLAND BECOMES A CIVILIZATION
## Design Bible v2.7 — Fire, Material Economy, Salvage, Shelter, Food, Diving, and Second-Life Stewardship Constitution

**Status:** Authoritative specialist successor to Design Bible v2.6. It adds and reconciles systems; it does not repeal the authority, physical-acceptance, true-return, empty-book invention, knowledge-carrier, permanent-death, or scope-moratorium laws of v2.1–v2.6.

**Director:** Karim F. Z.  
**Design partner and systems author:** OpenAI / ChatGPT  
**Date:** 11 August 2026

# 0. Executive ruling

The Director’s notes reveal the next missing master layer.

The game already knows that a survivor may make fire, find salvage, build a shelter, hunt, fish, grow food, restore a boat, dive, forge metal, and inherit an island after death. It still does not govern these as one circular civilization economy. Fire is currently a list of uses. Fiber is a few recipes. Salvage is a wreck operation without a complete junk economy. Workbenches are mentioned but not developed into a workshop architecture. Shelter resists hazards but does not yet own a playable maintenance budget. Agriculture, husbandry, diving, seasons, work, character growth, and offline time remain related in prose more than in one resolvable model.

v2.7 closes that gap.

The island’s durable game is:

> **Need → Notice → Recover → Understand → Transform → Prove → Integrate → Maintain → Hand On**

This is the **Civilization Weave**. It operates inside the existing Homeward Engine:

> **Anchor → Read → Prepare → Cross → Adapt → Recover → Integrate → Choose**

The Homeward Engine owns expeditions. The Civilization Weave owns what recovered matter becomes and what obligations it creates. Together they make the game a second life instead of a survival checklist.

The new governing proposition is:

> **A convenience is not progression until the survivor can supply it, operate it, repair it, understand its limits, and leave it in a condition another human can inherit.**

This changes the design in twelve decisive ways:

1. Fire becomes an energy and material-transformation network, from tinder to cooking, clean water, light, warmth, charcoal, ceramics, preservation, forging, open-steam processes, pumps, mechanical drive and—only at mature industrial capability—electric generation.
2. Pressure steam is separated from ordinary fire. An improvised sealed drum is never an acceptable boiler.
3. The workbench becomes a modular family of workholding and process capabilities. It never grants recipes.
4. Fiber becomes a full economy connecting plants, animals and salvage to cordage, bags, bandages, nets, sails, clothing, bedding, filters, reinforcement, rigging, animal care and records.
5. Wood, stone, fiber and metal become property families and throughput networks rather than colored tiers.
6. Metal receives three main source routes—reusable forms, recyclable scrap and geological ore—plus distinct cold-work, casting, forging, smelting, refining and heat-treatment gates.
7. Salvage becomes a classification and logistics game: working item, repairable assembly, donor part, reusable material, recyclable feedstock, consumable, information carrier, hazardous matter, contaminated matter, inert waste or unresolved object.
8. Shelter becomes a maintained service network whose protection, comfort and productive value emerge from site, load paths, drainage, enclosure, ventilation, heat, storage, inspection and repair.
9. Food becomes a portfolio across forage, shore harvest, fishing, hunting, cultivation, preservation and husbandry. Taming an animal is not domestication, and livestock is never a free idle generator.
10. Character development becomes specific, causal and felt. Every action changes at least one state or evidence ledger, but trivial repetition does not automatically increase a stat.
11. Swimming and diving become a complete amphibious profession with separate growth in stroke economy, water confidence, breath management, equalization, underwater task economy, recovery engineering and marine knowledge.
12. The “second life” ambition becomes a bounded stewardship clock: passive and proven routines can continue while the player lives their real life; unknown, dangerous or irreversible decisions wait for the player.

The beach may remain visually rich with junk and fresh wash. Useful capability remains scarce. The player should be able to spend a long calm session scavenging, sorting and improving the home, but should never feel that the optimal strategy is to pick up every object or repeat every action.

The production restraint remains binding:

> **Nothing in v2.7 authorizes broad content implementation before RT0/v0.1.2 passes physical Director testing and representative embodied loops prove enjoyable.**

## 1.3 Corrections to tempting but harmful interpretations

### “Every action increases a point”

Rejected literally. Every intentional action creates a **consequence event**—body cost, environmental change, object state, technique evidence, knowledge evidence, emotional memory or adaptation stimulus. It may create no permanent growth if it is trivial, repeated without novelty, performed badly without diagnosis, or not followed by recovery.

### “Getting sick repeatedly increases immunity”

Rejected as a dependable player strategy. Prior infection may change response to a specific organism, but protection can be partial, temporary or absent; different pathogens and doses remain dangerous. The playable progression is source knowledge, sanitation, treatment, storage, diagnosis and recovery—not deliberate contamination. WHO identifies safe water, sanitation and hygiene as prevention, and its microbial guidance notes that previous infection does not necessarily confer immunity. [WHO drinking-water](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/drinking-water), [WHO microbial fact sheets](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/wash-documents/water-safety-and-quality/dwq-guidelines-4/gdwq_4ed_with_third-addenda_microbial-fact-sheets.pdf)

### “The beach is full, so useful loot is abundant”

Rejected. The shore can be visually abundant while containing mostly wet, degraded, contaminated, incompatible, bulky or low-value matter. The game’s pleasure comes from improved reading and selective recovery, not vacuuming an infinite inventory.

### “Steam follows immediately after forging”

Rejected. Fire can create open steam early. Pressure power requires a trustworthy pressure boundary and controls. Pressure-vessel rupture and steam release are catastrophic hazards; OSHA treats boilers and pressure vessels as specialized systems requiring integrity, relief and inspection. [OSHA pressure vessels](https://www.osha.gov/pressure-vessels/standards), [OSHA pressure containment hazards](https://www.osha.gov/otm/section-4-safety-hazards/chapter-3)

### “Every item can be found, repaired and crafted”

Corrected to:

> **Every usable item enters through one or more of three acquisition routes: found functional, restored/remanufactured, or made. Not every item supports all three routes.**

A forged chisel can be made; an antibiotic cannot honestly be recreated by primitive workshop progression. A radio can be restored from parts long before the survivor can manufacture semiconductor components. Route asymmetry is part of the game.

# 2. Research translated into game law

## 2.1 Fire, process heat and power

Industrial heat is used for drying, curing, transformation and mechanical processes, while steam systems lose energy across generation, distribution and end use. This supports a game model in which heat quality, distance, insulation, condensate, control and maintenance matter—not a universal “fire tier.” [U.S. Department of Energy process heating](https://www.energy.gov/documents/qtr-ch8-process-heating-ta-feb-13-2015pdf), [DOE manufacturing steam uses](https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2018/10/f56/2014_mecs_footprint_definitions.pdf)

Translation:

- use direct heat before steam where rational;
- use open steam before pressure steam;
- treat pressure, rotation and electricity as additional hazard domains;
- require relief, control, feedwater, inspection and shutdown for pressure systems;
- let heat infrastructure create fuel, smoke, water and maintenance obligations.

## 2.2 Natural fiber

FAO sources distinguish seed, stem/bast, leaf and husk fibers and describe coir uses including twine, rope, nets, bags, mats, brushes, padding and erosion-control products. Coir extraction can be laborious, quality-sensitive and polluting when retting is poorly managed. [FAO natural fibers](https://www.fao.org/4/i0709e/i0709e.pdf), [FAO coir processing](https://www.fao.org/4/y3612e/y3612e04.htm)

Translation:

- fiber source identity affects length, strength, water behavior, comfort and hygiene;
- extracting, drying, grading and storing fiber is work;
- retting can contaminate water and compete with drinking-water safety;
- a rope, bandage, bowstring, wick and filter do not accept the same material merely because all are “fiber.”

## 2.3 Salvage and waste

NOAA describes abandoned vessels as mobile, polluting hazards that can release fuels and chemicals and damage habitat. NTSB warns aircraft wreck sites may contain sharp/heavy objects, toxic or flammable fluids, hazardous cargo and disease hazards. EPA notes that recycling does not make hazardous material harmless. [NOAA derelict vessels](https://response.restoration.noaa.gov/oil-and-chemical-spills/abandoned-and-derelict-vessels), [NTSB investigation safety](https://www.ntsb.gov/about/Documents/MajorInvestigationsManualApp.pdf), [EPA hazardous recycling](https://www.epa.gov/hw/regulatory-exclusions-and-alternative-standards-recycling-materials-solid-wastes-and-hazardous)

Translation:

- recovery starts with isolation and classification;
- working components are more valuable than equivalent melted mass;
- mixed or contaminated scrap may be worse than inert waste;
- waste consumes space, handling and attention until deliberately routed.

## 2.4 Shelter resilience

NIST resilience guidance emphasizes service performance across hazards, continuous load paths, flood elevation and recovery rather than one durability value. [NIST disaster-resilient buildings](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.1795.pdf), [NIST resilience planning](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1190v2.pdf)

Translation:

- the shelter owns services and failure paths;
- a storm can damage the rain screen without collapsing the sleeping cell;
- inspection and graceful failure are progression;
- rebuilding the same weak design is not mastery.

## 2.5 Food, seed and animals

FAO small-scale poultry guidance treats feed, water, housing, weather protection, health and management as interdependent. Home gardens improve dietary diversity but remain constrained by labor and water. [FAO small-scale poultry](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/73edfce9-c9a1-4814-b907-d86917967ed3/content), [FAO food systems and home gardens](https://www.fao.org/4/i3300e/i3300e.pdf)

Translation:

- livestock converts managed feed, water, space and care into food and materials; it does not create matter;
- capture is not husbandry, and husbandry is not domestication;
- plant diversity, seed storage and preservation make the food system resilient;
- animals, crops, water and shelter compete for the same labor calendar.

## 2.6 Swimming and breath-hold work

Breath-hold diving performance emerges from oxygen conservation, exercise economy, pressure response and learned technique; limits and adverse consequences remain. Hyperventilation can delay the urge to breathe without providing meaningful oxygen reserve and can contribute to blackout. [Breath-hold physiology review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8176094/), [Divers Alert Network hypoxia guidance](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/hypoxia-in-breath-hold-diving/)

Translation:

- no generic Lung Capacity upgrade erases the return reserve;
- training improves economy, confidence, equalization, task sequence and abort judgment;
- a longer breath-hold must never be the only way to solve deeper work;
- flotation, lines, pumps, boats, surface support and remote systems are intelligent progression.

## 2.7 A second life without compulsion

Animal Crossing’s creators describe the meaning produced when in-game time passes with real life; Project Zomboid’s long-term work emphasizes continuing communities and deeper settled-life objectives. These precedents support persistence and routine, but THE FIRST NIGHT must also protect permanent-death agency and healthy disengagement. [Nintendo’s Animal Crossing interview](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/animalcrossing-newleaf/0/1/), [Project Zomboid long-term systems](https://projectzomboid.com/blog/upcoming-features-b42/)

Translation:

- the island should change while the player is away;
- safe prepared processes may progress;
- lethal, creative and irreversible choices do not resolve secretly;
- the return summary helps the player re-enter instead of punishing absence;
- no daily login streak, artificial energy cap, premium timer or fear-of-missing-out cadence belongs in the design.

# 3. Constitutional laws 163–200

## Law 163 — Fire is infrastructure

Fire is a controlled energy flow with fuel, oxygen, heat transfer, smoke, containment, useful loads, loss, extinguishing and maintenance.

## Law 164 — Heat, open steam and pressure steam are different capabilities

Heating water is not a steam engine. Open vapor processes may be early; stored-pressure power is late industrial work.

## Law 165 — No improvised pressure miracle exists

An unqualified sealed drum, tank, pipe or vessel may not become a safe boiler because a progress bar completed.

## Law 166 — Every energy conversion declares loss and waste

Fire creates smoke, ash, heat leakage and fuel demand. Steam creates condensate, leakage and scale. Electricity creates heat, insulation and storage obligations.

## Law 167 — A workbench opens operations, never recipes

Stability, clamping, measurement and access make work possible; they do not insert knowledge into the survivor.

## Law 168 — The workshop grows by modules and clean boundaries

Food, medical, fiber, hot-metal, engine, battery and dirty-salvage work cannot all share one contaminated tabletop without consequence.

## Law 169 — Fiber is a material economy

Fiber has source, extraction, length, grade, cleanliness, dryness, twist, join, load, wear and end-use suitability.

## Law 170 — A clean bandage is not “any fiber”

Medical and food-contact uses require suitable material, cleaning, drying, storage and handling.

## Law 171 — Wood, stone, fiber and metal are property families, not tiers

Every use selects properties and geometry. No material is globally “better.”

## Law 172 — Form preserves value

A working tool, bearing, spring, wire, fastener, sheet or pipe usually has more capability value than its melted mass.

## Law 173 — Metal remembers source and history

Alloy, coating, corrosion, heat, contamination and prior service constrain reuse, melting, forging and food contact.

## Law 174 — Ore is geological, not guaranteed

Mining exists only where the authored island geology supports a deposit. Salvage, trade and Community Island may remain the dominant metal sources.

## Law 175 — Salvage is triage before loot

The survivor identifies need, hazard, mobility, access, condition, compatibility, preservation and closing window before recovery.

## Law 176 — Garbage remains matter

Low-value junk occupies mass, volume and safe storage until reused, recycled, contained, exported or deliberately disposed.

## Law 177 — Visual abundance and useful scarcity coexist

The shore may always look worked by the sea. High-value, compatible, clean and recoverable capability remains uncommon.

## Law 178 — Buried salvage is an earthwork problem

Sand, mud, roots, load and suction determine whether hands, digging stick, shovel, lever, support or machinery is required.

## Law 179 — Shelter protection is a service network

Dry sleep, shade, warmth, clean work, safe storage, drainage, air, fire separation and refuge are separate services with separate failure paths.

## Law 180 — Hazards attack mechanisms

Wind attacks uplift and racking; rain attacks openings and drainage; heat attacks the body and stored goods; earthquakes attack support, bracing and unsecured mass.

## Law 181 — Maintenance follows evidence

Inspection, cleaning, tightening, drying, treating, bracing, replacing and decommissioning respond to causal condition—not a universal repair meter.

## Law 182 — Growth creates an upkeep frontier

A larger home, garden, herd, workshop, boat and utility system create more protection and more obligations. Scale never becomes free.

## Law 183 — Food security is a portfolio

Forage, fish, hunted food, crops, stored food and husbandry have different seasons, risks, nutrients, labor and failure correlations.

## Law 184 — Nature is a visible family, not a universal number

The Skills interface may group Plant Knowledge, Soil and Cultivation, Animal Sign, Husbandry, Fisheries, Weather and Stewardship under **Nature**; progress remains strand-specific.

## Law 185 — Taming is not domestication

Habituating or handling one animal changes that relationship. Domestication is a population and generational process and will not occur through a short progress bar.

## Law 186 — Husbandry is an inherited obligation

Feed, water, shelter, health, waste, breeding and departure plans remain active across time and succession.

## Law 187 — Every action resolves a consequence event

An action affects body, technique evidence, knowledge evidence, object state, environment, habit or social memory. Permanent growth may be zero.

## Law 188 — Adaptation is specific and recovered

Running can improve running economy and endurance; loaded carrying can improve load tolerance and strength; neither improves safely without suitable challenge, nutrition, sleep and recovery.

## Law 189 — Experience improves control before power

Early benefits are better pacing, setup, posture, aim, sequencing, diagnosis and stop decisions. Raw output grows more slowly and remains anatomically bounded.

## Law 190 — Swimming and diving are not one skill

Surface stroke, reboarding, breath control, equalization, underwater movement, marine observation, salvage and equipment operation remain distinguishable.

## Law 191 — Breath training never creates invisible air

The body may improve economy and tolerance; oxygen, depth, exertion, cold and return reserve remain physical.

## Law 192 — The safest successful method is the more advanced method

Using a pole, float, line, pump, boat, surface tender or remote camera may demonstrate more mastery than personally entering danger.

## Law 193 — All items declare acquisition routes

Each item supports one or more of: found functional, restored/remanufactured, or made. Missing routes are intentional and documented.

## Law 194 — Tools are capability multipliers, not permission tokens

An axe, shovel, hammer, spear or bow opens physical operations only when its material, fit, condition and user control are credible.

## Law 195 — Weather is both pressure and opportunity

Rain fills storage and threatens drainage; wind dries fiber and endangers roofs; storms deliver salvage and close the sea; heat accelerates drying and illness risk.

## Law 196 — Time of day and season change decisions, not merely lighting

Visibility, thermal load, tides, animal behavior, plant cycles, work safety, fuel demand and travel routes change with the calendar.

## Law 197 — Offline time may complete prepared processes, never author new intent

Drying, curing, fermentation, rain capture or a proven protected routine may advance. Unknown combinations, hazardous hot work, diving and irreversible choices wait.

## Law 198 — Automation requires proof, supply, limits and shutdown

A process can run unattended only when its routine, inputs, operating envelope, fault response and safe stop have been demonstrated.

## Law 199 — Absence is not a failure state

The game contains no login streak, secret character death, arbitrary base wipe or uniquely valuable event that completely arrives and disappears while the player cannot act.

## Law 200 — Civilization must remain homeward

Every mature system must strengthen expedition, inheritance, human attachment or a truthful route home. Industry that exists only to make a larger industry is cut.

# 4. The Civilization Weave

The weave is the island’s repeatable creation rhythm.

| Movement | Player question | World action | Lasting output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need | What hurts, limits or matters now? | body/home/expedition exposes a real constraint | prioritized problem |
| Notice | What material, event, organism or failure might answer it? | environment communicates affordance and hazard | evidence and hypothesis |
| Recover | Can it be obtained without losing more than it gives? | player retrieves, harvests, dismantles or grows matter | owned material with provenance |
| Understand | What is it, what happened to it, and what might it do? | inspect, compare, clean, test, study | bounded confidence |
| Transform | Which operation changes its useful property? | cut, split, twist, dry, heat, join, shape, culture, repair | physical intermediate |
| Prove | Does it work safely under relevant variation? | load, leak, heat, weather, use or quality test | demonstrated capability |
| Integrate | Where does this reduce pressure or open a route? | install, carry, store, teach, connect | changed daily life |
| Maintain | What does continued usefulness now require? | inspect, supply, clean, repair, replace, shut down | reliability and learned limits |
| Hand On | How can another person understand and use it? | label, journal, standardize, cache, Arrival Station | physical inheritance |

The player does not receive reward popups for every movement. The reward is observable:

- the same rain no longer wets the bed;
- the same load takes fewer trips;
- the same knot is tied without a correction;
- the same beach reveals useful material faster;
- the same storm damages an outer screen rather than the sleeping room;
- the same dive ends with more reserve;
- the same successor finds a labeled tool, dry journal and prepared shelter.

# 5. Fire as the first energy network

## 5.1 Fire capability states

| State | Capability | Required proof | Main new uses | New obligation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F0 — Heat noticed | recognize ember, sun focus, friction, spark and hot surfaces | causal observation | hypotheses | burns and false assumptions |
| F1 — Flame captured | preserve and transfer an existing flame | safe transfer and extinguish | emergency light/heat | finite source |
| F2 — Independent ignition | create ember/flame with one route | repeat under dry control | reliable start | tinder and tool care |
| F3 — Sustained hearth | grade fuel, manage air and containment | useful stable heat plus safe stop | warmth, light, water heat, simple cook | smoke, fuel, weather |
| F4 — Managed food/water heat | vessel support and controllable heat zones | correct cook/boil and clean handling | cooking, purification claim, hot washing | vessel and hygiene |
| F5 — Efficient enclosure | stove, oven, smoker or thermal mass | draft, residue, burn and fire test | preservation, baking, fuel efficiency | cleaning and chimney/fire separation |
| F6 — Controlled transformation | charcoal, kiln, lime, resin, glue, dye, steam bending | process-specific yield/defect evidence | ceramic, refractory, joinery, treatment | long cycles and fire watch |
| F7 — Hot metal | forge or suitable casting/smelting route | temperature, stock, tooling and sample proof | fasteners, edges, fittings, repair | fuel, fumes, burns, metallurgy |
| F8 — Open steam plant | boiler-like open vessel, vapor path, condenser/heat exchanger without stored pressure | flow, material compatibility and safe draining | sterilization support, distillation, bending, heating, evaporation | scale, scald, water and fuel |
| F9 — Pressure power | rated/qualified pressure boundary, relief, gauge, control, feedwater, engine and safe load | hydrostatic/integrity evidence, controlled run and shutdown | pump, winch, mill, shaft or generator | catastrophic-energy maintenance |

No state appears because a Fire level reaches a number. Each is a network of demonstrated relations.

## 5.2 Fire use families

### Cooking

Heat route depends on food:

- direct ember/coal roasting;
- stone or metal surface;
- water boiling/simmering;
- steaming;
- baking;
- smoking/drying;
- rendering fat/oil;
- pressure cooking only with trustworthy equipment and knowledge.

Food identity, thickness, contamination, vessel, temperature history and storage determine safety and quality. “Cooked” is not a universal switch.

### Water

Fire supports microbial inactivation, hot washing, distillation and evaporation. It does not make unknown chemical or metal contamination disappear. Distillation requires a boiler, vapor path, condenser, receiver, cleaning and separation judgment.

### Warmth and comfort

The survivor receives radiant/convective heat only where geometry, wind and shelter allow. Fire can dry clothing and bedding, but also damage fibers, create smoke exposure, consume oxygen in enclosure, start structure fires and leave the player cold after fuel exhaustion.

### Light

Open flame, ember, torch, oil lamp, candle-like route and later electric light have different portability, smoke, wind, fuel, glare, fire risk and task visibility. Light quality affects inspection and fine work.

### Material transformation

Fire can:

- dry and heat-treat wood where appropriate;
- soften pitch, wax, resin and glue;
- char timber and make charcoal;
- fire clay and refractory material;
- calcine suitable limestone for lime only where geology and knowledge support it;
- render fat, prepare soap inputs and sterilize some tools/surfaces within limits;
- dye, steam or soften fibers;
- heat, cast, forge, braze, solder or weld suitable metals at different thresholds;
- power open-steam treatment, a pump or mechanical plant only through the required ancestry.

## 5.3 Fuel ecology

Fuel has:

- species/material identity;
- moisture and seasoning history;
- size and surface area;
- energy and burn-rate tendency;
- smoke, spark, ash and residue behavior;
- collection distance and regeneration;
- storage dryness;
- ecological and structural opportunity cost.

Likely routes include twigs and deadfall, split wood, coconut shell/husk, selected crop residue, charcoal, salvaged fuel only after identity and contamination control, oils/fats where rational, and later biogas or another authored route. Cutting live trees for every fire can destabilize soil, remove shade, reduce fruit/fiber habitat and lengthen future hauling.

The mature home maintains a **fuel portfolio**:

- immediate dry ignition stock;
- daily cooking fuel;
- weather reserve;
- high-temperature charcoal or equivalent;
- hazardous/technical fuels stored separately;
- a regeneration and collection plan.

## 5.4 Energy cascade

The efficient order is:

> **Need the least energetic transformation → capture direct useful heat → reuse residual heat → convert to motion only when motion is needed → convert to electricity only when electricity creates unique value.**

Examples:

- boil water directly rather than generate electricity to run a hot plate;
- use gravity head before a powered water pump;
- use a hand/foot pump before building a steam plant for intermittent water;
- use wind, water or a restored motor when they fit the site better;
- use engine waste heat for drying or hot water only if contamination and fire separation permit.

## 5.5 Pressure-power ancestry

Minimum governed chain:

```text
qualified water source
→ feedwater cleaning / scale control
→ trustworthy boiler material and joints
→ inspected pressure boundary
→ gauge + relief + controlled firing
→ safe steam path and isolation
→ cylinder/turbine/expander + valves
→ lubrication + condensate management
→ flywheel/governor/shaft
→ pump/winch/mill/generator load
→ enclosure, guarding and shutdown
→ inspection, records and trained successor
```

A steam engine is optional. It may be built from salvaged rated components, Community capability or mature metallurgy. The game must never force a solitary survivor to recreate an industrial supply chain when a repaired pump, sail, waterwheel, solar panel, engine or manual hoist is the more credible route.

# 6. Workbench and workshop progression

## 6.1 Workbench states

| State | Physical capability | Coordinated work | Does not grant |
|---|---|---|---|
| W0 — Ground relation | mat, flat stone, stump, body bracing | two active relations | recipes, cleanliness, precise joints |
| W1 — Stable support | level worktop, rigid legs/base, safe height | three relations; controlled cutting/striking | measurement or strong clamping automatically |
| W2 — Held and measured bench | stops, pegs, clamp/vise route, line/square/gauge | four to six relations; repeatable fit | hot work, medical cleanliness, powered work |
| W3 — Modular craft bench | removable jigs, drawers, templates, protected parts | multi-stage assemblies and small batches | universal contamination safety |
| W4 — Specialist cells | separate fiber, food, clean mechanical, electrical, medical and hot-work stations | domain-controlled work | expertise or undocumented knowledge |
| W5 — Powered workshop | shaft/electric/pneumatic/hydraulic tools where supplied and guarded | repetitive precision and larger work | material identity, safe setup or maintenance |
| W6 — Legacy shop/boatyard | lifting, stock control, inspection, test stands, standards and archive | engines, boats, utilities and teaching | guaranteed success or free scale |

## 6.2 Upgrade modules

Modules attach capability rather than a global level:

- stable top and replaceable sacrificial surface;
- clamp, vise, holdfast, dogs, stops and soft jaws;
- square, straightedge, plumb, level, template and marking station;
- boring guide and drill support;
- edge-maintenance/sharpening station;
- fiber rack, spindle, rope walk, loom/gauge and net frame;
- clean food/medical insert;
- soldering/electrical isolation mat;
- engine stand, drain tray and parts board;
- anvil/block, forge relation and quench boundary;
- bending brake, shear/snips, punch and rivet jig;
- power shaft, guarded drive, dust/fume extraction and emergency stop;
- hoist/tripod/gantry relationship;
- proof-load, leak, continuity and run-test stations.

## 6.3 What better work changes

A better bench can reduce:

- dropped and lost parts;
- uncontrolled force;
- misalignment;
- rework;
- hazardous posture;
- active hand count;
- contamination;
- measurement variation;
- setup time for documented repeat work.

It can increase:

- safe force;
- repeatability;
- part preservation;
- quality confidence;
- batch manufacture;
- teachability;
- compatibility with powered or heated operations.

It does not erase waiting, curing, cooling, drying, fermentation, seasoning, material scarcity or the need to test.

# 7. The fiber economy

## 7.1 Source families

Exact species depend on island ecology, wreck history and correct identification.

| Source family | Examples/route | Strength | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Husk fiber | coconut coir | coarse, water-tolerant, rope/mat/brush use | laborious extraction; rough for skin/medical use |
| Leaf fiber | sisal/agave-like or other authored plants | strong cordage and reinforcement | cutting hazards; processing and ecology |
| Bast/stem fiber | suitable bark/stem plants | long spinnable fiber | retting/stripping knowledge; water pollution risk |
| Grass/reed/palm strip | grasses, leaves, reeds, split palm material | quick mats, baskets, thatch, ties | variable life, shrinkage and low load confidence |
| Vine/root | selected flexible plant material | immediate bindings and baskets | species toxicity, rot and unreliable tension |
| Seed/fine fiber | cotton-like seed fiber if present | cloth, padding and finer work | cultivation, cleaning, spinning and volume |
| Animal fiber | sinew, hair/wool-like fiber, hide strips, feathers | specialized cord, insulation, fletching, leather system | anatomy, hygiene, welfare and limited supply |
| Salvaged textile | clothing, webbing, sailcloth, upholstery, belts | immediate high-value forms | salt, rot, fuel, mold, prior load and contamination |
| Salvaged line | rope, cable, fishing line, net | ready geometry and hardware | hidden UV/chafe damage; ghost-gear hazard |

## 7.2 Processing chain

```text
identify source and harvest stage
→ cut/collect without destroying regrowth
→ strip / decorticate / rett / scrape as appropriate
→ wash without contaminating potable water
→ dry under controlled shade/air
→ clean / comb / grade by length and condition
→ spin/twist
→ ply/lay/braid/weave/net
→ finish/protect if appropriate
→ proof by intended load and environment
→ store dry and labeled
```

Fiber residues may become mulch, fuel, bedding, brush, composite filler or waste depending on contamination and ecology. They never vanish.

## 7.3 Use atlas

| Family | Early uses | Developed uses | Qualification concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fire | tinder bundle, ember nest | wick, bellows seal/packing, fuel briquette binding | dryness and combustion residue |
| Carrying | tie, sling, folded wrap | pouch, bag, basket, pack strap, cargo net | seam/load/abrasion and body fit |
| Shelter | lash, thatch tie, mat | woven wall, screen, shade, insulation, reinforcement | wet strength, rot, fire and wind load |
| Water | prefilter cloth, vessel suspension | hose reinforcement, pump packing, catchment mesh | never claims pathogen removal by itself |
| Medicine | clean tie/padding only | sterile/clean dressing route, sling, splint binding | source cleanliness, lint, absorbency and storage |
| Food | basket, hanging line | drying rack, sacks, nets, food covers | food-contact contamination and pests |
| Hunting/fishing | snare line, handline | bowstring, net, trap, tackle, drag line | species load, stretch, cutting and humane method |
| Boat/diving | lashing, painter, tether | rigging, sail, harness, lifeline, lift sling | wet/chafe strength, knots, shock and inspection |
| Clothing/home | sandals tie, mat, bedding | woven cloth, weather layer, mosquito screen, upholstery | comfort, drying, hygiene and fire |
| Industry | simple belt, brush | conveyor/drive belt, gasket/packing, composite reinforcement | heat, oil, alignment and fatigue |
| Knowledge | cord tags, binding | paper/pulp route, book thread, labels, map backing | durability, legibility and dry storage |

## 7.4 Fiber mastery is not Rope Level

Separate evidence ledgers include:

- plant/animal/textile identification;
- extraction and retting;
- cleaning and drying;
- spinning and consistency;
- knots and load-specific fastening;
- weaving/basketry;
- net geometry and repair;
- sewing and fitting;
- rope making and inspection;
- rigging and life-safety use.

A talented basket maker does not automatically know a safe climbing rope. A sailor may understand line handling without being able to produce fine cloth.

# 8. Wood, stone, metal and circular material flow

## 8.1 Four backbone economies

| Backbone | Source forms | Processing ladder | Dominant roles | Persistent limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wood | twig, branch, pole, trunk, bark, resin, found board | cut → split → season → hew/saw/plane → join → preserve → char | fuel, handles, shelter, boat, containers, mechanisms | moisture, rot, insects, fire, grain and regeneration |
| Stone/mineral | pebble, cobble, slab, aggregate, sand, clay, limestone, ore | select → fracture → grind → grade → shape → fire/calcine/smelt | edges, percussion, hearth, foundation, abrasive, ceramic, metal source | mass, brittleness, dust, geology and transport |
| Fiber | plant, animal and salvage strands/forms | extract → clean → dry → grade → twist/weave → test | binding, carrying, shelter, clothing, medicine, rigging | water, rot, abrasion, cleanliness, length and load |
| Metal | working item, sheet/profile/tube/wire, fastener, scrap, ingot, ore | inspect → reuse → cold work → join → hot work → melt/cast → smelt/refine → heat treat | durable edge, spring, bearing, fastener, vessel, machine, electricity | alloy, corrosion, contamination, heat, tooling and finite stock |

These economies continually cross. An axe is not “wood + stone.” It is an edge material, a handle with directional grain, a seating geometry, a fastening/wedge relation, grip, balance, maintenance and proof under repeated load.

## 8.2 Metal source routes

### Route M-A — Reuse preserved form

Highest early value:

- ready knife, axe, shovel, hammer or saw;
- sheet, angle, tube, channel, wire, cable, mesh or chain;
- fastener, hinge, spring, bearing, pulley, valve or fitting;
- pan, tank, barrel, pipe or food-grade container with qualified provenance;
- motor, pump, alternator, starter, radio or instrument assembly.

The survivor cleans, preserves, adapts and tests the existing capability.

### Route M-B — Recycle scrap

```text
collect pieces
→ sort magnetic/visual/provenance evidence
→ isolate coatings, fluids, batteries and mixed materials
→ clean and cut
→ mechanically reuse when possible
→ accumulate compatible stock
→ melt only with known-enough alloy and suitable crucible/mold/heat
→ cast billet/simple part
→ finish and test
```

Mixed alloy scrap may produce brittle, porous or unworkable material. Melting can destroy a high-value heat treatment or shape. Recycling is not an automatic upgrade.

### Route M-C — Geological ore

```text
geological indication
→ prospect/sample
→ identify useful mineralization
→ excavate safely
→ crush and concentrate
→ prepare fuel, flux and refractory
→ smelt/reduce
→ separate slag/metal
→ consolidate/refine
→ cast/forge/heat-treat
→ test
```

This is labor-, fuel-, water-, transport- and knowledge-intensive. The island may support clay and stone without supporting useful metal ore. The game never spawns ore merely to complete a conventional tech tree.

## 8.3 Metal operation families

| Operation | Example outputs | Key ancestry |
|---|---|---|
| cold cut/punch/drill | patch, bracket, washer, hole, shim | cutter/punch/drill, workholding, edge control |
| bend/form | tray, flashing, strap, pipe bracket | mallet, brake/form, annealing knowledge where relevant |
| mechanical join | rivet, bolt, pin, folded seam | holes/alignment, fastener identity, access and test |
| soft join | solder/braze route | clean surface, compatible filler/flux, heat control |
| forge | chisel, edge, hook, fastener, fitting | forge heat, stock, tongs, anvil, hammer, normalization/heat treatment |
| cast | weight, simple fitting, blank, pulley form | meltable known metal, crucible, mold, gating, safe pour and finishing |
| smelt | bloom/metal from ore | ore, concentration, reducing environment, refractory, flux and large fuel budget |
| machine/finish | shaft fit, bearing seat, thread, accurate interface | files, drill, lathe/mill or jigs, measurement and surface finish |

Aluminum, copper alloys, steels, cast iron, lead and unknown metals do not share one forging/casting behavior. The game teaches through color, fracture, spark, magnetism, markings, corrosion, density, work response and documented tests without pretending field identification is perfect.

## 8.4 Circular routing

Every material lot can move among:

> **Use intact → maintain → repair → adapt → cannibalize → remanufacture → recycle → contain/dispose**

The earlier branch generally preserves more capability. Melting a pump to obtain anonymous metal should be a desperate or informed choice, not the default recycle button.

# 9. Living salvage and the always-worked beach

## 9.1 Shoreline composition

The beach uses four simultaneous layers:

1. **Persistent old burden** — faded plastic, fragments, rope, wood, corroded metal, buried objects and ecological damage.
2. **Tide-cycle movement** — small matter shifts, exposes, reburies or concentrates at wrack lines.
3. **Weather delivery** — storms and currents deposit new batches and move older masses.
4. **Wreckfall operations** — rare aircraft, boats, containers and major debris create temporary work windows and long-lived sites.

The player can always find something to inspect, clean, move, sort or recover. They cannot always find the exact part they need.

## 9.2 Salvage classes

| Class | Example | Best first action | Failure if treated as generic loot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Working item | intact knife, hand tool, sealed light | identify, clean, condition-test | hidden corrosion or contamination ignored |
| Repairable assembly | pump, radio, motor, reel | preserve, diagnose, isolate subsystems | unique fault multiplied by random disassembly |
| Donor assembly/part | bearing, spring, valve, fastener set | record identity and compatibility | useful form melted or lost |
| Reusable material | clean sheet, tube, board, rope | inspect history and property | unsuitable food/load use |
| Recyclable feedstock | compatible scrap, broken casting | sort and store by material | mixed alloy or hazardous coating |
| Consumable | fuel, oil, sealant, battery, medicine | identify, date, contain and ration | fire, poisoning or ruined equipment |
| Information carrier | label, chart, manual, log, black box | preserve context and dry carefully | knowledge destroyed during cleaning |
| Hazardous matter | battery, pressure cylinder, flare, chemical | isolate and mark | fire, toxic exposure, explosion |
| Contaminated matter | fuel-soaked cloth, bilge component, moldy padding | quarantine and assess cleanability | contaminates home, water or hands |
| Inert low-value waste | degraded plastic/foam/ceramic | route, compact only if safe, contain | inventory and shoreline clutter |
| Unresolved object | sealed box, unknown cylinder, anomaly part | observe, stabilize, defer | false confidence and destructive access |

## 9.3 Beach operation loop

> **Read tide/weather → scan → mark → approach → expose → stabilize → recover or defer → transport → quarantine → clean/preserve → inspect → test → catalog → allocate**

Experience improves selection and sequence. It does not reveal contents through a closed case.

### Buried and pinned objects

Recovery variables:

- burial depth and compaction;
- wet sand suction;
- object shape and trapped water;
- roots, rock or debris entanglement;
- tide and collapse window;
- sharp or hazardous surfaces;
- available shoring, lever, rollers, sling and lift points;
- return transport.

Hand clearing is possible for loose shallow sand. A digging stick or scoop improves control. A wooden or salvaged-metal shovel increases throughput. Deep or unstable excavation may require shoring, drainage, a second access route or abandonment. Digging in front of a heavy object can release it onto the survivor.

## 9.4 Salvage value is contextual

Salvage priority is driven by:

- current need;
- capability uniqueness;
- closing-window pressure;
- hazard and uncertainty;
- condition and preservation cost;
- mass/bulk and route cost;
- compatibility with owned systems;
- reuse value versus donor/material value;
- ecological cleanup value;
- whether recovery displaces a more urgent obligation.

A heavy perfect engine can be less valuable today than dry rope, a food-grade pan, a shovel head or sealed medicine.

## 9.5 Junk yard, quarantine and catalog

The mature homestead separates:

- clean ready tools;
- food/water-contact candidates;
- mechanical parts by system and identity;
- fasteners and small hardware;
- metal stock by form/provenance;
- fiber/textile drying and quarantine;
- batteries/electrical;
- fuel/oil/solvents;
- pressure containers;
- unknown/anomaly evidence;
- recyclable low-grade stock;
- inert waste and ecological-removal staging.

This physical organization reduces search time, contamination and duplicate work. It also occupies land, containers, roofing and maintenance. Hoarding everything is not free.

# 10. Shelter as survival machine and maintained home

## 10.1 Shelter services

The shelter provides separate service scores, never one level:

- dry sleeping area;
- wind reduction;
- shade and passive cooling;
- warmth retention and safe hearth relationship;
- drainage and flood escape;
- structural stability;
- clean food/water work;
- tool/material storage;
- medical recovery;
- animal/pest exclusion;
- smoke, fume and fire separation;
- storm refuge and redundant exit;
- workshop support;
- psychological home and inheritance.

## 10.2 Development states revisited

| State | Core achievement | New maintenance frontier |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate refuge | body out of worst exposure | temporary material, poor drainage, no reserve |
| Dry sleeping camp | rain/wind/ground relation proved | roof repair, bedding dryness, fuel |
| Working shelter | storage, hearth edge, clean surface and routine | pests, smoke, water, stock organization |
| Homestead | garden, workshop, boat/shore route, defensive boundaries | drainage network, fences, crop/animal labor |
| Resilient house | continuous load path, shutters, ventilation, fire separation, redundancy | inspection, spare parts, complex services |
| Fortified legacy compound | safe cell, distributed caches/utilities, Arrival Station and archive | large maintenance calendar and succession |
| Departure house | supports leaving without abandoning a trap | shutdown, animals, records, caretaker or decommission plan |

## 10.3 Hazard mechanism matrix

| Hazard | Attacks | Readable early evidence | Effective responses | False solution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| high wind/storm | roof edges, openings, racking, anchors, loose objects | flutter, fastener movement, frame gap, noise | compact geometry, continuous ties, bracing, shutters, secured yard | heavier material without load path |
| heavy rain | roof seams, wall junctions, drainage, foundations, stored goods | drip line, stain, pooling, scour, door bind | slope, overlap, flashing, gutter, overflow, raised dry storage | sealed room without ventilation/drainage |
| flood/surge | site, access, foundation, contamination, buoyant tanks | rising markers, saturated ground, blocked route | siting, elevation, breakaway elements, escape/cache, anchored systems | shoreline wall as universal answer |
| heat wave | body, roof heat gain, food/medicine, water demand | poor sleep, hot surfaces, wilt, rapid spoilage | shade, ventilation, thermal mass where safe, night work, water reserve | sealed hot bunker |
| drought | water, garden, fire risk, animal feed | falling source, dry soil, fuel dryness | storage, priority allocation, mulch, crop choice, firebreak | larger thirsty garden |
| earthquake | connections, bracing, heavy mass, slopes, utilities | post-event gaps, settlement, leaks, jammed exits | light/ductile routes, bracing, secure mass, shutoffs, inspection | brittle heavy fortification |
| lightning/fire | roof/vegetation, fuel, batteries, hot work | strikes, ember paths, dry debris, hot connections | separation, cleared zone, grounding/surge route when credible, extinguishing reserve | bringing every fire process indoors |
| salt/rot/insects | metal connections, timber, fiber, hidden cavities | rust weep, softness, powder, odor, looseness | dryness, material choice, inspection access, replaceable details | universal durability coating |
| wreck/debris | exposed yard, roof, openings, fuel and escape | warning/smoke/sound, fragment field | site intelligence, stand-off, sacrificial skin, safe cell, distributed assets | direct-impact-proof whole base |

NOAA’s hurricane hazard descriptions separate surge, heavy rainfall, high wind and debris; NWS/OSHA heat guidance also makes humidity, sun, wind and workload part of heat risk. The game uses the same multi-driver logic. [NOAA hurricane hazards](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/prepare/hazards.php), [OSHA heat hazards](https://www.osha.gov/heat-exposure/hazards)

## 10.4 Maintenance cycle

> **Observe → inspect → classify → prioritize → isolate/unload → clean/dry → tighten/repair/replace → test → document → stock next spare**

Inspection triggers:

- first commissioning;
- scheduled low-cost checks;
- after severe rain, wind, heat, flood, earthquake, fire or impact;
- before loading a changed room/roof/hoist;
- before departure;
- on successor arrival.

Maintenance debt does not deal hidden damage by timer. It increases uncertainty and allows real mechanisms to propagate. A loose roof edge under calm weather is a known task; the next storm turns that neglected edge into uplift propagation.

## 10.5 Comfort is productive, not decorative

Comfort can improve:

- sleep continuity;
- thermal recovery;
- pain management;
- morale and attachment;
- attention and fine work;
- illness recovery;
- willingness to document and maintain;
- session-ending safety.

Comfort does not become a luxury score that cancels hunger or danger. It is the return on human work that makes protection feel meaningful.

# 11. Food, water, cultivation and husbandry as one provisioning portfolio

## 11.1 The five food routes

| Route | First useful capability | Developed capability | Main uncertainty | Main infrastructure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forage | recognize and gather a low-risk known food | mapped seasonal plant portfolio and processing knowledge | identity, toxins, season, overharvest | field journal, baskets, clean processing, stores |
| Shore/reef harvest | collect known shellfish, seaweed or stranded food safely | tide-calendar, selective harvest and preservation | toxin, contamination, legality/ecology, surf | footwear, tools, containers, clean station |
| Fish | handline, trap or spear in a known safe setting | selective tackle, nets, boat, cold/smoke/dry chain | location, weather, bycatch, spoilage | fiber, hooks/points, bait, boat, preservation |
| Hunt/trap | small selective opportunity with credible weapon/trap | tracking, population judgment, humane recovery, whole use | animal behavior, wound, disease, future population | spear/bow/snare, processing kit, storage |
| Grow/manage animals | small seed trial or contained habituated animal | soil/seed system, rotation, nursery, husbandry and breeding records | viability, water, pests, disease, feed and labor | beds, tools, water, fence, seed store, enclosure |

The portfolio is valuable because failures are not perfectly correlated. A storm may close fishing while filling rain stores. Drought may reduce crops while improving some drying operations. A disease event may close animal food while preserved plant stores remain.

## 11.2 Forage and plant knowledge

The player begins with observation and possible background hypotheses, not universal edible-plant knowledge.

Plant evidence includes:

- leaf, stem, flower, fruit, seed and root form;
- smell, sap, irritation and damage evidence without reckless taste testing;
- habitat, water and season;
- animal/insect interactions that may suggest but never prove human safety;
- processing traditions found in credible records or teaching;
- small noncritical tests only where ethically and medically rational;
- outcomes recorded over time.

Functional plant families for the authored ecology may include:

- coconut/palm-like multipurpose plants;
- known perennial fruit or nut species;
- fast greens and herbs;
- legumes;
- roots/tubers, including processing-dependent toxic candidates;
- energy staples;
- oil/fat sources;
- fruiting vegetables;
- spice and medicinal-support plants;
- fiber plants;
- fodder and browse;
- cover, pollinator, living-fence and erosion-control plants;
- timber, resin, tannin, dye, soap or insect-management plants.

More species enrich observation only when each has season, habitat, preparation, competing use, ecological role and failure evidence. A larger botany list without those relationships is rejected.

## 11.3 Seed-to-food chain

```text
find viable seed / fruit / cutting / tuber
→ identify and preserve provenance
→ test maturity and viability
→ map site light, wind, soil, salt and water
→ prepare a small bed/container
→ germinate or propagate
→ protect nursery
→ transplant/harden where relevant
→ irrigate and monitor
→ diagnose nutrient, pest and disease evidence
→ harvest selectively
→ process and preserve food
→ retain diverse reproductive stock
→ improve soil and revise calendar
```

The first harvest is not “farming unlocked.” It is one proven plant–site–season–care relation.

### Cultivation tools

Progression may use:

- hand, sharpened stick and scoop;
- digging stick and planting dibble;
- shell, bone, wood or found-metal hoe/scraper;
- wooden shovel with fire-hardened or reinforced edge;
- salvaged shovel head and repaired handle;
- forged shovel, hoe, mattock, rake and pruning tools;
- wheelbarrow/cart, irrigation line, pump and protected storage;
- powered cultivation only when scale justifies it.

## 11.4 Animal management ladder

| State | Relationship | Required evidence | Benefit | Obligation/risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A0 — Wild observation | animal remains independent | sign, rhythm, food/water/cover | prediction and avoidance | no ownership |
| A1 — Attraction/habituation | animal tolerates predictable presence | repeated calm non-harmful relation | closer observation, pest control possibility | dependency and conflict can begin |
| A2 — Controlled capture | individual safely contained briefly | species handling and enclosure | health assessment, relocation, possible taming | stress, injury, escape, disease |
| A3 — Tamed individual | individual accepts handling/routine | repeated welfare-positive contact | eggs/milk/work/material only if species and condition support it | daily care; not inherited genetically |
| A4 — Husbanded group | feed, water, shelter, waste and breeding managed | population and health records | renewable food/material/manure/services | labor, feed conversion, disease and ecology |
| A5 — Selected line | traits tracked over generations | breeding diversity and long calendar | more predictable behavior/production | inbreeding, ethics, long absence risk |
| A6 — Community institution | multiple keepers, standards, vet/health and feed system | teaching, records and public rules | resilient island food/service system | governance and outbreak responsibility |

Plausible candidates depend on what truly exists in the world seed or arrives through Community Island history. Feral fowl and selected small introduced animals may support early husbandry. Larger goats or pigs create major vegetation, fencing, feed and disease consequences and should not be added merely as content. Spawn Island does not receive live animals from ordinary empty wreckfall without a specifically authored, survivable and ecologically consequential event.

## 11.5 Husbandry dependency chain

```text
species and individual knowledge
→ humane capture/attraction route
→ quarantine space
→ secure species-appropriate enclosure
→ reproducible feed and clean water budget
→ weather shelter and bedding
→ handling and health observation
→ waste/manure route
→ breeding and population control
→ product collection/processing
→ departure, release, transfer or successor plan
```

If any binding input fails, production falls before the animal becomes an icon with a low percentage. Behavior, body condition, feed refusal, dirty water, escaping, aggression, disease sign and nesting/breeding changes communicate the problem.

## 11.6 Food preservation ladder

| Method | Inputs | Claim | Failure evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| eat fresh | identification and clean handling | immediate use | spoilage/contamination window |
| shade/cool storage | airflow, evaporative/ground conditions where suitable | slows some deterioration | odor, texture, pests, mold, temperature history |
| drying | suitable food, cut size, clean rack, airflow, humidity control | lower water activity when sufficiently dried | case hardening, mold, insects, rewetting |
| smoking | controlled heat/smoke/air and time | flavor plus drying/preservation only when process supports it | soot, underdrying, rancidity, fire |
| salt/brine | sufficient clean salt and controlled concentration | method-specific preservation | dilution, contamination, excess/insufficient salt |
| fermentation | correct organism/environment/salt/time and clean vessel | controlled transformation | gas, odor, surface growth, pH evidence where measurable |
| sealed heat processing | tested container, seal, heat/time knowledge | specific product/process safety | seal failure, underprocessing, pressure hazard |
| cold chain | powered or environmental cooling with history | temperature-controlled storage | outage, door/opening, warm period |

The game avoids false home-canning confidence. Advanced preservation requires specific evidence and safe authoring.

## 11.7 Water expansion beyond v2.6

The v2.6 water system remains authoritative. Additions:

### Dug well route

```text
terrain/geology and contamination survey
→ test pit/boring
→ groundwater/salinity evidence
→ safe excavation and shoring
→ lining/casing and surface exclusion
→ raised sealed head/apron and drainage
→ bucket/rope or pump
→ initial purge and treatment
→ repeated water-quality evidence
→ protection from latrine, animals, flood and fuel
→ maintenance and seasonal-level record
```

A well is not guaranteed fresh. Coastal salt intrusion, surface runoff, geology, latrine proximity and wreck contamination may disqualify it. Boiling removes microbes within its claim but not chemical contamination. [CDC well-water treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/drinking-water/safety/guidelines-for-treating-well-water.html), [EPA well contaminants](https://www.epa.gov/privatewells/potential-well-water-contaminants-and-their-impacts)

### Rain, fog and dew

- rain capture depends on clean surface, first flush, storage, overflow and treatment;
- fog collection requires frequent suitable fog, wind-oriented mesh, drainage and clean storage;
- dew collection is low-yield and condition-dependent;
- powered atmospheric condensation requires energy, coils/surfaces, cleaning and condensate safety.

Fog mesh can be a credible regional route where geography supports it; it is not a universal humidity extractor. UNEP describes fine mesh intercepting fog droplets into reservoirs. [UNEP fog harvesting](https://www.unep.org/news-and-stories/story/shortages-mount-countries-hunt-novel-sources-water)

### Immunity ruling

Unsafe water exposure may create pathogen-specific immune history in the body record, but it never becomes a displayed grindable skill and never raises a universal “dirty water tolerance.” The rational progression is safer supply.

# 12. Signature tool and weapon ancestry

All tools support one or more acquisition routes:

- **Found functional** — immediate shortcut; condition and compatibility still matter.
- **Restored/remanufactured** — donor parts, cleaning, repair, replacement and testing.
- **Made** — material and operation ancestry from available capability.

## 12.1 Axe

| Stage | Matter | Tools/workspace | Knowledge/skill | Qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| sharp hand edge | suitable stone/shell/metal shard | hammerstone/support | fracture and hand safety | controlled cut on selected material |
| stone adze/axe head | tough knappable/ground stone | striker, abrasive, wet grinding support | edge angle, shock and grain | edge survives representative impacts |
| prepared haft | selected branch/pole | cutter, scraper, fire/season route | grain, handle geometry, grip | no crack/split under dry test |
| mounted stone axe | head + haft + wedge/binding | jig/bench optional, fiber/wood fastening | seating, balance and retightening | repeated controlled strike then inspection |
| repaired found axe | salvaged metal head + new/old handle | rust removal, wedge, file/stone | eye fit, edge damage, temper caution | head security and cut test |
| forged metal axe | qualified steel/iron stock | forge, tongs, anvil, hammer, heat treatment, grinder | metallurgy, geometry, temper, hafting | hardness/toughness and repeated work test |

## 12.2 Shovel

| Stage | Route | Strength | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| hand/scoop | shell, bowl, split wood | loose surface material | poor reach and throughput |
| digging stick | shaped/hardened wood | planting, prying and loosening | cannot move bulk soil efficiently |
| wooden paddle shovel | broad shaped board + handle | sand, compost, light soil | edge wear, splitting, heavy wet soil |
| repaired salvaged shovel | found head + fitted handle/fastener | efficient digging and salvage exposure | corrosion, bent socket and body strain |
| fabricated sheet shovel | suitable sheet + formed socket/rivets + handle | adaptable shape | bending, edge and joint quality |
| forged shovel/mattock | hot-worked stock + haft | hard soil and long life | mature forge, heat treatment and large labor |

## 12.3 Hammer and stone hammer

Progression:

> hammerstone → shaped striking stone → lashed/hafted stone hammer → wooden mallet → repaired found hammer → forged head and replaceable handle → specialized riveting, blacksmithing, geology and joinery hammers

Striking face, mass, rebound, handle, fastening, target and eye protection determine suitability. A stone hammer that fractures rock may destroy a bearing or rivet.

## 12.4 Spear

Routes:

- sharpened/fire-hardened wooden point;
- wood shaft + stone/shell/bone point + binding/adhesive;
- salvaged knife/blade mounted only with credible tang/socket and guard relation;
- metal point cold-formed or forged;
- fishing spear, hunting spear and defensive spear with different geometry;
- detachable harpoon head and recovery line only after line/load knowledge.

Qualification includes straightness, point retention, thrust/throw balance, safe recovery and purpose-appropriate penetration without uncontrolled breakage.

## 12.5 Bow and arrows

```text
appropriate stave/laminations
→ season and inspect grain
→ shape limbs and handle
→ provisional string
→ tiller gradually
→ final string and brace-height evidence
→ low-draw test
→ progressive draw and inspection
→ matched arrows
→ range, background, anatomy and recovery practice
```

Arrow system:

- straight, seasoned shaft;
- nock or string interface;
- point matched to target/purpose;
- secure point relation;
- fletching or other stabilization where used;
- mass and spine consistency;
- safe storage and inspection.

Fiber quality controls bowstring, binding and fletching. Wood controls stave and shaft. Stone/bone/metal controls points. Glue/resin/heat and the workbench control assembly. The bow is therefore a cross-material examination, not a Crafting-level purchase.

## 12.6 Snares and traps

A trap requires:

- target species and sign;
- non-target risk;
- tested line/trigger/anchor;
- placement and weather response;
- check frequency;
- humane dispatch/release capability;
- population and legal/ethical rule;
- decommissioning to prevent ghost harm.

No idle trap produces meat while the player is absent beyond its safe check interval. An unresolved capture becomes a return intervention, not background loot.

# 13. Character development and the universal consequence resolver

## 13.1 Six consequence channels

Every intentional action writes one event across at least one channel:

1. **Immediate body** — stamina, hydration, heat, energy, pain, injury, breath, attention.
2. **Trainable stimulus** — strength, endurance, load tolerance, mobility, balance, dexterity, grip, acclimatization or water confidence.
3. **Technique evidence** — task-specific control, setup, pace, force, aim, rhythm and stop decisions.
4. **Knowledge evidence** — material, organism, system, weather, failure and causal diagnosis.
5. **World/object change** — material state, wear, structure, ecology, route and cleanliness.
6. **Character/meaning** — confidence, aversion, attachment, restraint, grief, habit or social trust where narratively supported.

This satisfies the Director’s demand that everything matters without creating exploitable universal XP.

## 13.2 Adaptation equation

The tuning model uses:

`adaptation candidate = specificity × productive challenge × quality feedback × novelty × recovery × health readiness × anti-repetition`

Where:

- specificity matches the capacity or technique;
- productive challenge falls below destructive overload;
- feedback means the player can compare result and cause;
- novelty falls as an identical solved action repeats;
- recovery includes sleep, hydration, food and injury/illness state;
- anti-repetition approaches zero for meaningless loops.

The result is a candidate response over time, not an instant point after every animation.

## 13.3 Activity development map

| Activity | Immediate cost | Body candidates | Technique/knowledge | Felt growth | Overload path |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 13.4 Speed and carry are outputs

The character never owns a single Walking Speed or Carry Capacity stat. Current performance resolves from:

- stature/stride and body history;
- strength, endurance, load tolerance, balance and mobility;
- current stamina, hydration, energy and thermal readiness;
- pain, illness, sleep debt and body-part impairment;
- load mass, bulk, asymmetry and pack fit;
- surface, slope, weather, visibility and footwear;
- chosen pace and reserve policy.

Growth is felt through stride stability, acceleration, breathing, posture, tool rhythm, recovery and safe task envelope—not merely a larger number.

- surface stroke economy;
- sprint/rescue swimming;
- flotation and treading economy;

## 14.3 Underwater work families

- edible/biological observation and selective harvest;
- route and hazard mapping;
- hull exterior inspection;
- line/net removal;
- container marking and stabilization;
- fastener/access work;
- small-part recovery;
- rigging and lift preparation;
- pump/drain/reposition projects;
- camera/sonar/ROV survey;
- deep heavy salvage;
- medical/rescue support;
- Triangle evidence.

Every target retains mass, buoyancy, attachment, contamination and surface-handling requirements. Reaching an engine is not recovering an engine.

## 14.4 Aquatic ecology roster rules

The underwater world should contain roles before catalogue size:

- reef grazers and schooling fish;
- predatory fish;
- benthic shellfish/crustaceans;
- filter feeders;
- seaweed/algal communities;
- coral/rock habitat organisms;
- rays or other substrate hazards where ecologically credible;

Each added family must affect sign, food web, season, hazard, harvest ethics or salvage access.

## 14.5 Diving and offline time

The player can schedule no personal dive while offline. Safe passive support may continue:

- battery charging;
- freshwater soak/preservation of recovered components;
- drying cleaned gear;
- pump-down of a qualified stable compartment with automatic safe shutdown;
- camera/sonar logging from a tested fixed station;
- tide/weather observation.

An entangled diver, flooded line or live wreck decision never resolves without the player.

# 16. The Second-Life Stewardship Clock

## 16.1 Design intent

The Director wants a game that feels like the second life lived on the screen while real life continues. The correct translation is not a traditional idle economy where numbers grow behind a closed app. It is an island that remembers processes, seasons, maintenance and care.

The player should be able to:

- play actively for tactile expeditions, invention and crisis decisions;
- spend a calm session sorting salvage, gardening, fishing or improving home;
- start long causal processes;
- leave safely;
- return to meaningful progress, changed conditions and a readable next decision;
- avoid punishment for having a real life.

## 16.2 Five time ownership modes

| Mode | Examples | Player presence | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Embodied | chop, dig, swim, aim, inspect, fit unique part | required | real-time tactile control |
| Managed | boil, kiln, smoke, engine test, pump operation | nearby/periodic | can interleave safe work; warnings and intervention |
| Passive causal | dry, cure, season, ferment, germinate, charge | not continuously required | world clock changes state |
| Proven routine | known irrigation, feed distribution, guarded machine | may continue offline within limits | consumes staged inputs; stops safely on fault/limit |
| Decision window | storm response, unknown wreck, sick animal, failed seal, anomaly | player required | pauses at readable nonterminal state |

## 16.3 Offline eligibility contract

A task can progress offline only if all are true:

- pattern or routine is demonstrated and documented;
- site and equipment are commissioned;
- required inputs are physically staged;
- waste/output capacity exists;
- hazard grade is permitted;
- operating limits are defined;
- a fault has a safe stop;
- the task does not require novel judgment, personal risk or irreversible intent;
- simulation can conserve matter and state.

### Generally eligible

- clay/fiber/timber drying and seasoning;
- fermentation/curing with suitable vessel and safe range;
- rain/fog collection with overflow;
- seed germination and crop growth;
- passive solar charging with controller and safe battery state;
- corrosion, spoilage and weather exposure;
- low-risk animal routine only while feed, water, shelter and welfare limits are satisfied;
- scheduled observation instruments.

### Conditional after mature proof

- irrigation;
- pump cycles;
- cold storage;
- protected smoker/dryer;
- grain/fiber mill;
- mechanical workshop routine;
- Community-operated utility.

### Never ordinary offline work

- unknown experimentation;
- open unattended campfire;
- forge, smelting, casting or pressure-steam operation without staffed institutional safety;
- diving/swimming/hunting/combat;
- unknown container opening;
- first repair of a unique engine/radio/medical device;
- surgery or unstable medical care;
- sea trial, crossing or Triangle experiment;
- permanent-death decision.

## 16.4 Absence protection

- The character logs out in a declared safe state; they do not walk an unseen task list into danger.
- Offline resolution cannot secretly kill the character.
- A qualified routine can consume only staged resources and reserve floors.
- Animals cannot starve invisibly because a real-world emergency kept the player away; the system stops at an intervention threshold, applies nonterminal welfare consequences, and requires redesign before expansion.
- Severe weather can create causal damage, but critical irreversible loss is bounded by declared exposure and warning rules.
- A major wreck opportunity cannot fully arrive and disappear offline.
- No system demands a daily check-in.

## 16.5 Return-to-island report

The report is a short physical ledger, not a mobile-game reward shower:

- time/weather passed;
- completed and halted processes;
- consumed inputs and produced outputs;
- maintenance cues;
- animal/crop/water condition;
- new shore/wreck evidence;
- decisions waiting;
- anything protected by a safe-stop rule.

The player can walk to every reported result and see it in the world.

## 16.6 Pace seed

v2.6’s active-play seed—72 real minutes per game day—remains a prototype calibration value, not final canon. v2.7 requires comparative testing of:

- 72-minute active day;
- 96-minute active day;
- 120-minute active day;
- sleep target of roughly 45–90 real seconds for a stable 7–9 game-hour night;
- bounded offline detail window and diminishing coarse resolution beyond it.

The selected pace must support a useful 20–40 minute session, a satisfying 1–2 hour session, and safe multi-day absence. It must not make daylight, seasons or animal care inaccessible to players with fixed real-life schedules.

# 18. Quantitative resolver seeds

Numbers remain calibration seeds, not real-world engineering certification.

## 18.1 Fire and energy

`useful heat = fuel potential × dryness factor × combustion control × capture factor`

`allocated useful heat ≤ useful heat`

Allocations include body/shelter, vessel/water, food, material process and losses. If allocations exceed capacity, temperature falls or duration rises; the system does not duplicate energy.

Pressure-power eligibility is boolean before it is numeric:

`eligible = qualified boundary AND relief AND gauge/control AND feedwater route AND engine path AND safe shutdown AND current inspection`

If false, no normal operation occurs.

## 18.2 Fiber yield and use

`usable fiber mass = raw mass × extraction yield × grading yield × condition`

Use suitability is a property match across length, wet strength, abrasion, cleanliness, comfort, heat and load proof. One generic strength value is insufficient for life-safety line.

## 18.3 Salvage priority

Prototype score:

`priority = need + uniqueness + closing window + reuse value + ecological value − hazard − uncertainty − transport − preservation burden`

The score suggests attention; it never automatically recovers the object.

## 18.4 Shelter event risk

`event consequence seed = hazard demand × exposure × mechanism vulnerability × condition uncertainty × criticality ÷ redundancy`

Damage resolves on components and service loss. Redundancy reduces consequence, not hazard existence.

## 18.5 Food continuity

`food security = diversity of independent routes + safe stored cover + current production − correlated hazard exposure − labor/water/feed debt`

The game may track nutrients internally at useful groups—energy, protein, fat, key micronutrient diversity, salt/electrolyte context—without making the player manage a clinical dashboard.

## 18.6 Body and learning

Immediate action resolution retains the v2.6 workload model. Add:

`learning evidence = novelty × diagnostic feedback × technique relevance × attention × result clarity`

`growth realization = accumulated specific stimulus × recovery quality × health readiness × time`

## 18.7 Breath-hold work

`safe useful task window = minimum(oxygen-limited reserve, thermal reserve, pressure/equalization limit, task-control reserve, return/exit reserve)`

Training may improve economy and control terms. It never removes the minimum relationship.

## 18.8 Offline process

`resolved progress = minimum(elapsed eligible time, staged-input time, output-capacity time, maintenance limit, safe-stop limit, offline authority cap)`

The first exhausted constraint stops the process and records why.

# 19. Player-facing interaction and comprehension

## 19.1 No global Craft or Build button

The Backpack remains:

- Inventory;
- Vitals;
- Skills.

Making starts from held items, a combination surface, a workbench/module, or contextual construction in the world. Known demonstrated patterns may be selected from the journal/pattern record to stage work; unknown items never appear as grey recipes.

## 19.2 Material inspection language

An item communicates:

- what the survivor can observe now;
- what remains unknown;
- condition and provenance evidence;
- plausible operations;
- current hazard;
- why a tool/workspace/body route is blocked;
- next non-destructive test.

Examples:

- “Long clean fibers separate when scraped; wet-load behavior unknown.”
- “The pump turns a quarter-cycle and stops. Salt crystals at the shaft.”
- “The sheet bends without cracking in this direction. Coating identity unresolved.”
- “The roof tie moved after the last gust; the post below remains plumb.”
- “The animal eats near the fence but retreats when the gate moves.”

## 19.3 Workbench interaction

The player places material/subassemblies into active positions, selects or physically uses an operation, sees support/clamp/alignment, and can pause with persistent work state. Upgrades add visible fixtures and accessible operations, not a larger recipe list.

## 19.4 Salvage interaction

Beach scanning supports:

- low-attention visual wandering;
- close inspection;
- marking for later;
- quick collection of safe small matter;
- deliberate dig/pry/rig operations;
- quarantine and catalog at home.

Expertise changes what cues mean, not glow color or loot rarity beams.

## 19.5 Growth feedback

The player should feel change without opening Skills:

- smoother pace and breathing;
- fewer foot corrections under load;
- faster safe setup at a known bench;
- stable bow draw and grouping;
- better line tension control;
- calmer water entry and earlier abort;
- more precise verbal/journal diagnosis;
- home routines that require fewer emergency actions.

## 19.6 Second-Life return surface

On return, the game offers:

- **Resume body** — current safety and condition;
- **Island changed** — weather/time and visible world state;
- **Work completed/halted** — with physical causes;
- **Needs attention** — no more than a short prioritized set;
- **Quiet option** — continue a calm home task instead of responding to a crisis.

The interface does not shame absence.

# 22. Acceptance constitution

## 22.1 Fire and power

- Fire supports heat, light, food, water and transformation through one conserved energy model.
- Wet fuel, wind, enclosure and smoke visibly matter.
- Open steam and pressure steam cannot be confused.
- Pressure power cannot operate without all safety gates.
- Steam is optional where a safer simpler energy route works.

## 22.2 Workbench

- Upgrades are visible fixtures and operations.
- The pattern book does not gain unknown items from bench construction.
- Clean and dirty domains create real workflow boundaries.
- Better workholding reduces rework and hazardous posture.

## 22.3 Fiber

- At least four source families have distinct preparation and use limits.
- Coarse rope, medical dressing, bowstring, net and sail do not accept one generic Fiber item.
- Wetness, abrasion, cleanliness and proof matter.
- Fiber by-products and failed batches remain physical.

## 22.4 Materials and salvage

- Found form is normally preserved before melting.
- Metal alloy/provenance constrains operations.
- Ore appears only through geology.
- The beach remains interesting without flooding useful progression.
- Garbage occupies space and has deliberate routes.
- Buried salvage reads as excavation and load work.

## 22.5 Shelter

- No universal shelter HP exists.
- Rain, wind, heat and earthquake/fire each attack mechanisms.
- Service loss can be partial and recoverable.
- Fortification creates tradeoffs and upkeep.
- Comfort improves felt recovery and daily work.

## 22.6 Food and nature

- Nature is legible as a family with specific strands.
- A viable food plan uses multiple routes.
- Seeds, water, soil, pests, preservation and season interlock.
- Taming is distinguished from husbandry and domestication.
- Animals have feed, water, welfare, disease and departure requirements.
- Repeated contaminated-water exposure is not an immunity grind.

## 22.7 Character

- Every action creates at least one consequence event.
- Meaningless repetition may create zero permanent growth.
- The same route/task demonstrates change without numbers.
- Running, load carriage, chopping, mining, swimming and diving develop different capacity combinations.
- Recovery and injury remain binding.

## 22.8 Diving

- The underwater world contains useful work at multiple depths.
- Surface support and remote methods are rewarded.
- Breath-hold development improves economy/control, not magical air.
- Recovered objects still require landing, preservation and integration.

## 22.9 Second life

- At least six safe process families can advance without continuous player presence.
- Unknown and dangerous actions never author themselves.
- Offline resolution conserves matter and stops at real constraints.
- Absence is not punished.
- No daily login or unique offline disappearance is required.
- Return information is short, causal and physically verifiable.

## 22.10 Automatic failure conditions

The design fails if:

- a workbench adds recipes;
- a pressure vessel is made safe by skill percentage;
- fire produces motion/electricity without machinery ancestry and loss;
- any clean bandage accepts contaminated generic fiber;
- all metal behaves as one material;
- melting is always the best salvage route;
- beach play becomes pick-up-everything labor;
- shelter damage is a random durability tick;
- husbandry produces food without feed/water/time;
- deliberate pond sickness is optimal progression;
- running in circles is optimal stat training;
- breath-hold grinding removes drowning risk;
- offline time kills the character or deletes a major unseen opportunity;
- the player returns to a wall of mandatory chores;
- mature industry stops serving home, expedition, inheritance or return.

# 23. Director-level rulings closed by v2.7

1. Fire grows into cooking, water, warmth, light, manufacture, forging and optional industrial power.
2. Steam power is late, pressure-governed and optional; open steam arrives earlier.
3. Workbench upgrades open operations and precision, never recipes.
4. Fiber becomes a full source–process–use economy.
5. Stone, wood, fiber and metal are the backbone materials but never universal tiers.
6. Metal supports preserved form, recyclable scrap and ore routes.
7. Salvage includes working items, broken items, parts, raw forms, feedstock, hazards and garbage.
8. The beach remains visually busy and can support long scavenging sessions without making valuable capability common.
9. Some buried objects require shovel, support, leverage, time and body work.
10. Shelter protection, comfort and productive value are maintained services.
11. Weather and natural hazards attack actual shelter mechanisms.
12. Forge capability follows fire control, fuel, refractory, air, tools, metal knowledge and proof.
13. Pre-metal tools remain wood–stone–fiber systems or found/repaired salvage.
14. Nature visibly contains growing food, animals, fisheries, weather and stewardship as specific strands.
15. Every action has consequence; not every action grants permanent progress.
16. Carrying heavy loads can develop strength/load tolerance/endurance while slowing pace and increasing cost/injury risk.
17. Running develops running economy and stamina within recovery limits.
18. Diving develops multiple capacities and skills; it does not simply enlarge an oxygen bar.
19. Every item declares one or more of three acquisition routes.
20. Low knowledge increases misidentification and damaging action risk; it never creates arbitrary hidden deletion.
21. Food uses forage, fish, hunt/trap, cultivation and husbandry as a portfolio.
22. Water includes well, rain, fog/dew, source treatment and distribution with hazard-specific truth.
23. Repeated illness does not become a reliable immunity route.
24. Axe, shovel, hammer, spear, bow, arrows and snares have full ancestry.
25. Time of day, season and weather change resources, work, body, sea and shelter.
26. The game may support calm idle-like stewardship, but risky agency remains embodied.
27. Safe prepared routines can progress offline.
28. Absence never becomes a moral failure or secret permadeath.
29. The return report is physical and causal.
30. The scope moratorium remains intact.

# 25. Final governing identity

THE FIRST NIGHT is not about climbing from Stone Tier to Metal Tier. It is about a human learning what this island’s matter can do, building control around that matter, paying the body and time costs honestly, and leaving the result in a form another human can continue.

The beach is not a loot box. It is a moving archive of accidents, industries and lives.

The workbench is not a menu. It is the moment the survivor stops asking one hand to be a clamp, ruler, vise and memory at once.

Fire is not an icon. It is warmth, light, food, clean water, smoke, fuel, danger, ceramic, metal and—in the far distance—power.

The shelter is not a health bonus. It is a machine for turning rain, wind, heat, debris, fatigue and fear into repairable human safety.

Nature is not a green XP bar. It is seasons, soil, seed, animal lives, water, restraint and the consequences of taking too much.

Diving is not a larger breath meter. It is learning when to enter, when to turn, how to work, how to recover, and when intelligence should keep the body out of the water.

Idle time is not free production. It is evidence that the systems the player built can continue safely without constant touch.

The signature is:

> **Let fire become infrastructure. Let the bench become control. Let fiber connect the first shelter to the last sail. Let metal remember its source. Let junk demand judgment. Let the house reveal what it protected and what it still owes. Let work change the body without turning life into grind. Let the sea reward restraint. Let time continue without punishing absence. Let every convenience acquire an ancestry, every system acquire an obligation, and every life leave the island more understandable for the next.**
